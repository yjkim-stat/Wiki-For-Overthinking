## Where the field stands

### 2026

- **LoongReflect: Boosting Long-Horizon Reflection in Search Agents via Global Perspective Distillation** — Gives a search agent an explicitly reversible trajectory tree with reflect and backtrack as first-class actions, and trains the reflection policy with a dense local signal distilled from a teacher that can see the whole trajectory alongside the sparse terminal reward the local decision is ultimately judged by.
- **Calibrating Post-Training Feature Shifts for LLM Data Contamination Detection** — Calibrates feature-based data-contamination detectors against the systematic feature shifts that post-training introduces, by measuring how controlled prompt variants move scores on known non-members and then correcting only the recurring shift directions, with the gains concentrated at the low-false-positive operating point rather than in AUC.
- **V-FiLLM: Verified Financial LLM Reasoning Benchmark** — Generates financial reasoning benchmarks from executable computation trees over real tables so that answers are correct by construction with no model in the labelling loop, exposes four independently controllable difficulty axes, and finds that unit and scale perturbations collapse the strongest model from 98.4 percent to 3.0.
- **SafeSceneReason: A Multimodal Reasoning Benchmark Connecting Industrial Hazards with Accident Knowledge** — Builds an industrial-safety reasoning benchmark from two pipelines -- program execution over safety scene graphs, and evidence graphs extracted from real accident-investigation reports -- and shows that general multimodal capability does not transfer to it while a 9B model fine-tuned on its chain-of-thought split matches frontier systems.
- **Beyond Solvability: Task Learnability as a Static Prior for LLM RL Post-Training** — Separates how well a policy currently does on a task from how positively that task responds to further training, shows the second is reproducible across independent runs and predicts downstream value at matched current pass rate, and estimates it from a short probe run before RL begins.
- **Mismatch Matters: On-Policy Distillation Beyond Token Agreement** — Identifies degenerate agreement -- students reaching near-perfect token agreement with a teacher by looping while the response as a whole is broken -- and replaces the agreement objective with two directional mismatch corrections, one bounding runaway excess tokens and one injecting teacher-preferred mass at positions the student almost never samples.
- **Post-Hoc Sparse Coding of Latent Communication Between Vision-Language Model Agents** — Fits a post-hoc sparse autoencoder to the frozen dense tensors that two vision-language agents exchange, finds a 128-fold payload reduction at near-identical reconstruction and roughly unchanged single-run accuracy, and then spends most of the paper enumerating the alternative explanations its own design cannot rule out.
- **MathShikkha: A Controlled Study of Answer-Only and Chain-of-Thought Supervision for Bangla Mathematical Reasoning in Small Language Models** — Compares chain-of-thought against answer-only supervision under a protocol where the two conditions differ in nothing but the training target, and finds the rationales buy nothing in-domain for strong backbones while buying 20 to 28 points out of domain -- with a human study attributing the measurable effect to language adherence and inspectability rather than to better reasoning.
- **MedCalc-R1: Knowledge-Guided Reward Framework for Medical Mathematical Reasoning** — Replaces the single tolerance threshold that RLVR uses to score floating-point answers with a hybrid reward pairing a hard clinical-safety constraint against a continuous precision-sensitive term, and adds a reward for stating the computational formula explicitly so an external verifier can check it.
- **PAST: Privileged Adaptation from Complete Student Trajectories for On-Policy Self-Distillation** — Gives the teacher in on-policy self-distillation access to each completed student rollout and its verified outcome, adapting it to preserve behaviour on successes and redirect failures toward verified success, while the student keeps a prefix-only interface it can actually deploy.
- **When Is Benchmark Contamination Detectable? Information Limits and Power-Calibrated Audits** — Casts benchmark contamination auditing as sparse-mixture detection, proves that detectability is governed by the single quantity alpha*rho*sqrt(m), and shows empirically that the resulting power predictions transport while the sample-size budgets derived from them do not.
- **Think Deep, Speak Once: Relit, A Recursive Latent Implicit Transformer Framework** — Bolts a small trainable recurrent block between a frozen 1.1B language model's body and its output head, so reasoning happens as repeated refinement of two latent vectors rather than as generated tokens.

### 2025

- **Validating LLM-as-a-Judge Systems under Rating Indeterminacy** — Shows that when a rating task admits several defensible interpretations, forcing raters to pick one answer biases LLM-as-a-judge validation so badly that the selected judge can be up to 31% worse than the one chosen by asking raters for the set of all plausible ratings.
- **A Implies B: Circuit Analysis in LLMs for Propositional Logical Reasoning** — Uses causal mediation analysis on a minimal propositional logic task to recover a sparse reasoning circuit in Mistral-7B and Gemma-2 up to 27B, and decomposes it into four families of attention heads that execute rule locating, rule moving, fact processing and decision making as sequential steps.
- **Style Outweighs Substance: Failure Modes of LLM Judges in Alignment Benchmarking** — Builds a large standardized meta-benchmark and finds that LLM-judge preference scores do not correlate with concrete measures of safety, world knowledge or instruction following, because judges systematically prioritize style over factuality and safety.
- **Dynamic Early Exit in Reasoning Models** — Detects the points where a reasoning model switches thought chains, interrupts to induce a trial answer, and stops generation when that answer's confidence is high enough — cutting chain-of-thought length substantially while raising accuracy, with no training.
- **Does Reinforcement Learning Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity in LLMs Beyond the Base Model?** — Measures RLVR-trained models against their base models with pass@k at large k and finds the base wins, concluding RLVR sharpens sampling toward paths the base already had rather than adding new ones.
- **Understanding and Mitigating Numerical Sources of Nondeterminism in LLM Inference** — Shows that greedy decoding is not deterministic across hardware: changing GPU type, GPU count or evaluation batch size shifts a reasoning model's AIME'24 accuracy by up to 9 percentage points and its response length by 9,000 tokens under BF16, because floating-point addition is non-associative.
- **Provable Scaling Laws for the Test-Time Compute of Large Language Models** — Gives two aggregation algorithms whose failure probability provably decays to zero as inference compute grows, assuming only that the model can sometimes be right and can compare two solutions better than chance.
- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards Implicitly Incentivizes Correct Reasoning in Base LLMs** — Shows that base models win pass@K on mathematics by producing wrong chains that land on right answers, and that scoring the chain too — CoT-Pass@K — reverses the verdict in RLVR's favour at every K.

## Core ideas

### credit assignment

Deciding which part of a trajectory earned the reward, and across 31 sources the problem the archive's training literature keeps rediscovering under different names. The complaint is uniformly one of granularity mismatch: a policy is rewarded on whole outputs or whole trajectories while the decisions that produced the result happen at a finer scale, so nothing identifies which retrieval found the key document, which translation choice made the output unfaithful, or which of several changes packed into one code action earned the test pass. The difficulty is worst exactly where the trajectory is longest, since a sparse terminal reward over many turns supplies no supervision at the point of greatest ambiguity. The archive's methods split by where they cut. Some cut by turn, some by trajectory segment type, some by sub-parts of a single action matched across rollouts, and some by token selection -- and their reported contributions are consistent in size, typically 2 to 8 points, against ablations of the terminal signal that are typically 20 to 65. That asymmetry is the corpus's most reliable finding here: finer credit is a refinement of outcome supervision, never a substitute, and every source that removes the terminal reward finds it the largest single loss. Two design constraints follow and are stated independently by several sources. A local term should not be able to flip the sign of the trajectory-level advantage or create an update where the outcome reward is silent -- hence multiplicative rather than additive turn weighting, and gates that stop partial reference agreement overriding a wrong answer. And a criterion for allocating credit is not credited until it has been raced against random allocation at matched cost, because a randomised control routinely captures much of the benefit attributed to the targeted criterion.

Seen in: BiCAA: Bidirectional Credit Assignment for Search-Augmented Agent; EviSD: Evidence-Conditioned Self-Distillation for Search-Augmented Agents; Latent Thought Credit: Multi-Answer Credit Assignment for Latent Reasoning; GradCuit: Credit-Assigned Gradient Flow Enables Robust and Interpretable Test-Time Latent Reasoning.

### construct validity

Whether a measurement is measuring the thing it is named after, and across 28 sources the archive's most frequently applied critical lens -- usually applied by the authors to their own work, which is why it appears in limitations more often than in results. The recurring failure is a correlated alternative that the design cannot exclude. A temporal-horizon steering direction also separates abstraction, urgency, strategic scope and framing, so the intervention may be steering a broad answer register rather than the named variable, and a transfer result shows the construct is involved without identifying the direction's semantic basis. A probe's margin can be generic: training the identical probe on a small model that never produced the responses reaches 0.72 because the items are by construction more ambiguous as prompts, so only the 14-point margin above that is unexplained. And a total-effect measurement can be answering a different question entirely -- a revision that turns an unparseable output into a parseable one changes the score without changing any reasoning. Two conclusions the corpus reaches are worth carrying. Validity is a property of the whole protocol rather than of the model: measured performance depends on template, decoder, controller, reducer, verifier, stopping rule and numerical settings, so a number without its protocol is not comparable to another. And some constructs have no clean measurement window at all -- one source shows that models weak enough to have headroom lack the signal while models with the signal lack the headroom, leaving the design space where the construct could be measured cleanly nearly empty.

Seen in: Soft Guidance Starts to Outperform CoT Prompting as LLMs Improve; Intertemporal Preference Steering in Qwen3 via Contrastive Activation Addition; Test-Time Scaling in Reasoning LLMs: Inference Regimes, Evaluation, and Reproducibility; Monte Carlo Tree Search for Table-to-Multimodal Report Generation.

### overthinking

Producing more reasoning than the problem needs, and across 28 sources the archive's most-attacked inefficiency. The waste is measured rather than asserted: a median of 2.7 thousand emitted tokens against a median 830 needed to reach the correct answer on one benchmark, and 11 to 29 percent token reduction at matched accuracy across three model sizes -- with the savings heavily skewed, reaching 53 percent for the top decile of problems and 81 for the top percentile. Compression sometimes improves accuracy outright (77.5 against 72.5 on one set), which the sources read as redundant steps introducing noise. Three cautions the corpus supplies. Redundancy is diffuse rather than located in an identifiable class of step, so importance criteria agree on what to keep and disagree on what to cut. The same signal that marks productive reasoning marks overthinking on retrieval-style tasks, where amplifying deliberation makes a model derive facts it should retrieve -- one source's candid failure analysis. And a length penalty not gated on correctness is satisfied vacuously, which is why the archive's reward-shaping entry treats conditional length rewards as the only form that converts preserved length into accuracy.

Seen in: Reinforcement Learning with Evolving Rubrics as Rewards for Audio Reasoning; The Tell-Tale Trace: Detecting Reasoning Failures in LLMs Using Chain-of-Thought Dynamics; Fewer Tokens, Smaller Cache: Reward-Coordinated Efficient Reasoning; CAT: Confidence-Adaptive Thinking for Efficient Reasoning of Large Reasoning Models.

### test-time scaling

Spending more computation at inference to get a better answer, and across 28 sources a phrase covering algorithms with different statistical structure, compute accounting and failure modes -- extending one trajectory, sampling completed candidates and reducing them by voting or verification, and searching over unfinished partial states. One source argues the consequence directly: reporting these under a single scalar budget, or reporting accuracy without the inference protocol that produced it, makes results incomparable across papers. The archive's accumulated findings are mostly about where the curve bends. It is not monotone in the sample count, because more candidates strengthen whatever the aggregation rule already does; the selection signal is the binding constraint past a modest budget, and where confidence does not track correctness no verifier-free rule recovers the available headroom (measured at 14 to 17 oracle points on one hard benchmark against 0.8 percent captured). Uniform spending is wasteful in both directions, and adaptive allocation is worth having but hard to learn and hard to audit. And its value is conditional on the instance rather than on the method: budget increases that pay on a feasible instance change nothing on an infeasible one. Two structural observations recur. The budget is almost always spent on the output side -- re-running the model on the same input -- and input-side diversity is a direct competitor for the same calls that has rarely been benchmarked against it at matched compute. And in multimodal settings the usual selection signals fail for a specific reason: neither answer frequency nor verbalised confidence tests whether an answer depends on the image, so a grounded answer and a confident language-prior guess look identical at the selection layer.

Seen in: s1: Simple test-time scaling; It's the Decoding Format, Not the Perturbation: Auditing Consistency-Based Selection for Vision-Language Test-Time Scaling; GradCuit: Credit-Assigned Gradient Flow Enables Robust and Interpretable Test-Time Latent Reasoning; Test-Time Scaling for Safe Text-Guided Image Generation via Intermediate Clean Estimates.

### chain of thought faithfulness

Whether a stated chain of thought is an account of the process that produced the answer, and across 26 sources the archive's central negative result. The measurements are consistent: faithfulness varies substantially across tasks, with models sometimes relying heavily on the trace and sometimes largely ignoring it, and it decreases with model size and capability on most tasks studied -- so the earliest source's own conclusion is a conditional (traces can be faithful if size and task are chosen carefully) rather than an endorsement. Two structural problems recur. The standard test detects unfaithfulness only where an injected cue changes the answer, so it is silent whenever the answer is unchanged, and it intervenes on the prompt rather than on the trace. And the property is not simply desirable: a perfectly faithful agent executes a harmful action whenever its reasoning arrives at one, which makes the usual requirement self-contradictory -- faithful enough to be monitored, robust enough to override its own reasoning. One source measures that tension directly, finding the most faithful model follows tampered safe reasoning in 97.5 percent of cases while rejecting unsafe reasoning in only 12.3, higher faithfulness going with lower safety across seven models, and steering along the two directions trading them against each other by 5 to 9 points against magnitude-matched random vectors moving either by at most 1.1. The archive's operative position is that faithfulness is a relation between the trace and the answer rather than a property of the trace: monitoring works as a consistency check between the two, and a trace can be clean, correct and on-topic while the action taken is arbitrary.

Seen in: Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think: Unfaithful Explanations in Chain-of-Thought Prompting; Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning; Does Accuracy Equal Evidence? Reasoning Faithfulness under KV Cache Compression; How Much Does a Reasoning Summary Reveal? An Observability Ladder for Large Language Models.

### advantage estimation

Deciding how much better than a baseline a rollout was, which is what a policy update is actually made of. Across 23 sources the corpus's contribution is diagnostic rather than definitional: it shows what goes wrong when the estimate is asymmetric, degenerate or unidentified. Asymmetry is the sharpest case. A regression critic that overestimates success probability gives failed rollouts large negative advantages and successes small positive ones -- measured at -0.597 against +0.227, a ratio of 2.63, growing more skewed over training -- so the update spends most of its magnitude on failures; a distributional critic brings the ratio to 1.02, and the paper notes correctly that batch-wise normalisation is an affine rescaling that cannot remove a relative asymmetry. Degeneracy is the group-relative case: a prompt whose rollouts all share a reward has zero advantage for every member and contributes no gradient, and one source measures a first stage cutting all-wrong groups by 4.95 points precisely to address it. Collapse is a third failure: in latent reasoning, within-thought answer variance exceeds between-thought variance in every setting and training makes it worse (noise-to-signal 1.54 rising to 13.10), so the thought-level advantage is estimating differences that have largely disappeared. Two further findings recur. Weighting is a bias-variance trade rather than a free improvement -- one truncation-order sweep peaks at 4 and degrades beyond it because higher moments cannot be estimated from eight rollouts, and one interpolation sweep runs 45.4, 52.1, 44.8 as its coefficient goes 0.00, 0.25, 1.00. And the corpus's methodological caution applies with unusual force here: margins of 0.75 to 2.6 average points from single runs with no seeds are the norm, and one source's headline is the best average across checkpoints evaluated on the evaluation benchmarks rather than on a held-out criterion.

Seen in: BiCAA: Bidirectional Credit Assignment for Search-Augmented Agent; EviSD: Evidence-Conditioned Self-Distillation for Search-Augmented Agents; Latent Thought Credit: Multi-Answer Credit Assignment for Latent Reasoning; Self-Improving Large Language Models via Progressive Experience Evolution.

### chain of thought

The intermediate tokens a model emits before its answer, and across 23 sources an object the archive is careful to describe rather than to interpret -- one source proposes 'derivational trace' as a neutral stand-in precisely because reading these tokens as reasoning is unsupported by the available evidence and, it argues, actively harmful. What is established is largely formal and negative. Formally, generating intermediate tokens lifts the expressive ceiling of a bounded-depth transformer, with the number of steps tied to a circuit class; one archived construction realises graph traversal in two layers and a linear-step branching measure without layer normalisation, and another shows a sharpness-based bound growing linearly rather than exponentially in the relevant parameter for a parity family. Empirically the trace and the answer are separate information channels: content present in one can be absent from the other in both directions, traces can be swapped between problems or deleted with the answer unchanged, and post-training that improves accuracy need not improve the trace's semantic validity. The corpus's practical positions are that the trace is useful as a consistency check against the answer rather than as an account of computation, that its length is a compute knob with a bend in the curve, and that a large fraction of it is redundant -- with the caveat from a head-to-head comparison that redundancy is diffuse rather than located in any identifiable class of step.

Seen in: STaR: Bootstrapping Reasoning With Reasoning; Tree of Thoughts: Deliberate Problem Solving with Large Language Models; Attend to Your Own Thoughts: Breaking the Barrier for Post-Training Quantization of Reasoning LLMs through the Lens of 1.58-Bit Quantization; CURV: Enhancing Chart Understanding Through Curriculum Visual Grounded Reasoning.

### pass@k

The probability that at least one of k sampled responses is correct, used across 21 sources as the archive's measure of what a model can reach as opposed to what it reliably produces. Reading it correctly is most of what the corpus has to say. Small k measures sampling efficiency and large k probes the boundary, and the two can move in opposite directions within one training run -- pass@1 rising across steps while coverage at pass@256 falls, and in one distillation study the curve crossing between k=1 and k=1024 in every setting measured. A rising curve is also compatible with within-question stability collapsing, so reporting only coverage hides that the same model now answers inconsistently. Two corrections to naive use are established. Scoring the chain as well as the answer reverses the verdict about reinforcement learning at every k, because base models win plain pass@k partly by producing wrong chains that land on right answers. And for candidate banks produced by adaptive search or dependent procedures the usual estimator is a descriptive summary of what was returned rather than an unbiased probability -- one source states this and notes most papers reporting the metric do not. The archive's practical demand is that a method report pass@k across a range rather than at one budget, since the property worth having is competitiveness with the base model uniformly across k rather than a win at either end.

Seen in: BODHI: Do LLMs Branch Out and Discover Heterogeneous Inferences?; When Correct Solutions Repeat: Rarity-Aware Credit Redistribution for GRPO; Test-Time Scaling in Reasoning LLMs: Inference Regimes, Evaluation, and Reproducibility; PAST: Privileged Adaptation from Complete Student Trajectories for On-Policy Self-Distillation.

### test-time compute

Computation spent at inference rather than training, and across 21 sources the resource the archive's scaling literature is about. Its foundational result here is a trade against parameters: a compute-optimal allocation strategy improves scaling efficiency more than fourfold over a fixed best-of-N baseline, and in a FLOPs-matched evaluation lets a smaller base model outperform a 14 times larger one on problems where it already has non-trivial success rates -- with that last clause being the condition the archive keeps finding attached. The corpus's accumulated qualifications: the same nominal budget buys different things under different regimes, so a single scalar makes results incomparable; more of it is not monotone in accuracy because the aggregation rule is amplified rather than averaged; and its value is conditional on the instance, with budget increases that pay on a feasible instance changing nothing on an infeasible one. One source separates it from a confound worth noting -- an improvement attributed neither to added compute alone nor to information encoded in phrasing.

Seen in: Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning; Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally can be More Effective than Scaling Model Parameters; GradCuit: Credit-Assigned Gradient Flow Enables Robust and Interpretable Test-Time Latent Reasoning; Refining Over Resampling: Test-Time Self-Correction for LLM Reasoning.

### outcome reward

Scoring a whole trajectory by whether its final answer is correct, which collapses everything the model did into one bit. Across 18 sources it is the cheap, load-bearing signal that nothing has been able to replace. The complaint is uniform: a ten-retrieval search trajectory receives one scalar and nothing distinguishes the retrieval that found the key document from one that repeated a query; the reward supervises only the answer, so a model can reach it without attending to the audio, the image or the intermediate quantities at all; and over a long tool-use trajectory it supplies no supervision at the exact point where credit is most ambiguous. Yet every source that ablates it finds it indispensable rather than merely helpful, and the archive holds no instance of dense supervision anchoring a policy on its own -- removing the terminal reward is repeatedly the largest single loss, and one system with it removed keeps perfect step-level tool selection while never terminating. The design consequence the sources converge on is that dense signals should be applied as modifiers that cannot flip a sign or create an update where the outcome reward is silent: one applies its turn-level term multiplicatively rather than additively for exactly this reason, another gates partial reference agreement so it cannot override a wrong final answer, and a third bounds a privileged correction by the magnitude of the outcome advantage. A separate limitation is that its scope is the answer: post-training against it improves solution accuracy without necessarily improving the semantic validity of the traces, which one source notes should be unsurprising given what it scores.

Seen in: BiCAA: Bidirectional Credit Assignment for Search-Augmented Agent; EviSD: Evidence-Conditioned Self-Distillation for Search-Augmented Agents; Reinforcement Learning with Evolving Rubrics as Rewards for Audio Reasoning; PAMT: Process-Aligned Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Domain Machine Translation.

### residual stream

The additively updated activation vector each transformer layer reads from and writes to, and in this archive the substrate every interpretability method operates on rather than an object studied in itself. What the 17 sources establish about it is mostly negative and methodological. It has no privileged basis: individual neurons are polysemantic and there is no reason to expect features here to align with the neuron basis at all, which is the premise the sparse-autoencoder literature is built on -- and that literature's own numbers show the cost, with automated interpretability scores declining from about 0.30 at layer 0 to 0.15 by layer 5 and reconstruction raising perplexity from 25 to 40 when substituted back. The causal information is often compact: replacing the whole stream at a site has a comparable effect to a low-rank patch, so the relevant subspace is small. And one source names the interpretive gap directly -- aligning explanations to a separately learned codebook answers what a direction means in a proxy space while leaving implicit the rule that actually writes into the stream. Practical scope caveats recur: methods tuned for it do not robustly transfer to intermediate MLP layers, and attention heads are typically untouched.

Seen in: Cultural Awareness is Represented but Not Decoded: Tracing Mythological Knowledge across 18 Open-Source LLMs; A Theory of Conditional Collapse under Low-Rank Weight-Space Ablations: I. The Single-Block Theory and Synthetic Validation; Reasoning Errors Have a Region and a Direction in the Residual-Stream Trajectory of LLMs; MI-MIDI: Mechanistic Interpretability of Text-to-MIDI Generation Models via Probing, Lenses and Steering.

### self-correction

A model detecting and repairing its own error, and across 17 sources a capability the archive can measure the behaviour of more easily than the benefit. The central finding is a dissociation: how often a model announces a correction is anti-correlated with whether the corrections help. Against two prior supervised methods on a shared backbone, a step-level preference method scores higher on both mathematics benchmarks while self-correcting at 28.8 and 23.9 percent against 45.3 and 46.5 and recalling fewer errors, and two of its own five backbones lower their correction rate after training while improving. The diagnosis offered is behaviour collapse under supervised training, which biases a model toward following correction templates rather than selectively revising real errors. Two measurement problems follow. The behavioural metrics are defined by matching a list of signal phrases, so a model that corrects without using one is invisible to the measurement, and the training data's preferred continuations are defined by the same convention. And the construct has almost no clean measurement window: models weak enough to have headroom lack the signal, models with the signal lack the headroom, so the design space where self-correction could be measured cleanly is nearly empty -- one source's calibration-floor argument finds only one marginally viable cell with negligible held-out gain. Where the archive finds it working is as a trained selectivity rather than a prompted behaviour, with the preference formed between a repair and the continuation that would have followed had the error stood, so the signal exists only where an error does.

Seen in: How Much Does a Reasoning Summary Reveal? An Observability Ladder for Large Language Models; The Tell-Tale Trace: Detecting Reasoning Failures in LLMs Using Chain-of-Thought Dynamics; The Calibration Floor: Format Repair Can Masquerade as Self-Correction at Small-to-Mid Scale; Refining Over Resampling: Test-Time Self-Correction for LLM Reasoning.

## Reading path

**Start here** — the anchor papers for this topic:

1. 2110.14168
1. 2103.03874

**Then, in order of relevance:**

1. **Your Reasoning Benchmark May Not Test Reasoning: Revealing Perception Bottleneck in Abstract Reasoning Benchmarks** (2026)
   - Separates perception from reasoning in ARC-style benchmarks with a two-stage pipeline, and finds about 80% of vision-language model failures are perception errors, not reasoning errors.
   - <https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.826>
2. **Training Verifiers to Solve Math Word Problems** (2021)
   - Introduces GSM8K, 8.5K grade-school math word problems, and shows that training a verifier to rank many sampled solutions beats finetuning the generator directly.
   - <https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168>
3. **SafeSceneReason: A Multimodal Reasoning Benchmark Connecting Industrial Hazards with Accident Knowledge** (2026)
   - Builds an industrial-safety reasoning benchmark from two pipelines -- program execution over safety scene graphs, and evidence graphs extracted from real accident-investigation reports -- and shows that general multimodal capability does not transfer to it while a 9B model fine-tuned on its chain-of-thought split matches frontier systems.
   - <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09230>
4. **Calibrating Post-Training Feature Shifts for LLM Data Contamination Detection** (2026)
   - Calibrates feature-based data-contamination detectors against the systematic feature shifts that post-training introduces, by measuring how controlled prompt variants move scores on known non-members and then correcting only the recurring shift directions, with the gains concentrated at the low-false-positive operating point rather than in AUC.
   - <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10462>
5. **V-FiLLM: Verified Financial LLM Reasoning Benchmark** (2026)
   - Generates financial reasoning benchmarks from executable computation trees over real tables so that answers are correct by construction with no model in the labelling loop, exposes four independently controllable difficulty axes, and finds that unit and scale perturbations collapse the strongest model from 98.4 percent to 3.0.
   - <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11047>
6. **MathShikkha: A Controlled Study of Answer-Only and Chain-of-Thought Supervision for Bangla Mathematical Reasoning in Small Language Models** (2026)
   - Compares chain-of-thought against answer-only supervision under a protocol where the two conditions differ in nothing but the training target, and finds the rationales buy nothing in-domain for strong backbones while buying 20 to 28 points out of domain -- with a human study attributing the measurable effect to language adherence and inspectability rather than to better reasoning.
   - <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08503>
7. **MedCalc-R1: Knowledge-Guided Reward Framework for Medical Mathematical Reasoning** (2026)
   - Replaces the single tolerance threshold that RLVR uses to score floating-point answers with a hybrid reward pairing a hard clinical-safety constraint against a continuous precision-sensitive term, and adds a reward for stating the computational formula explicitly so an external verifier can check it.
   - <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08623>
8. **When Is Benchmark Contamination Detectable? Information Limits and Power-Calibrated Audits** (2026)
   - Casts benchmark contamination auditing as sparse-mixture detection, proves that detectability is governed by the single quantity alpha*rho*sqrt(m), and shows empirically that the resulting power predictions transport while the sample-size budgets derived from them do not.
   - <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07914>
9. **Zero Gap Is Not Restoration: Stratified Per-Question Probability Evaluation and Step-wise Mitigation of Benchmark Contamination** (2026)
   - Shows that the standard metric for judging contamination-mitigation strategies can be scored by cancellation rather than by restoration, replaces it with a per-question stratified metric under which the published ranking reverses, and proposes a mitigation that decides its intervention during decoding instead of from a prior estimate.
   - <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07341>
10. **Mitigating Scoring Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge via Random Number Generation** (2026)
   - Measures an LLM judge's latent number bias by asking it to emit random numbers, then rectifies its scoring token probabilities against that measured bias.
   - <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05726>

## Open problems

Drawn from the limitations each paper states about itself, so this is what the field admits it cannot do yet.

- **LoongReflect: Boosting Long-Horizon Reflection in Search Agents via Global Perspective Distillation** — Stated in an appendix and unusually specific. The method depends on retrieval quality and on the controller's checkpoint and recovery judgements, so retrieval noise affects verified-state construction, checkpoint selection and the compressed update handed to the next branch -- and an error in an early diagnosis can propagate through every later search and answer decision. The normalised exact-match reward under-credits valid semantic aliases that token-level F1 would partially credit. The teacher's binary terminal outcome, while providing useful trajectory-level context, may shape the style of the local diagnoses rather than only their content. Reversible multi-turn search and the three-fast-one-slow schedule add both inference and optimisation cost. Most importantly, the experiments use a single recorded seed configuration, with comparing multiple independent seeds listed as future work alongside quantifying robustness to retrieval noise, measuring checkpoint-selection accuracy and analysing error propagation through the compressed updates. Not stated but worth noticing: a 12.6-point average gain reported from one seed on two training datasets is a large effect with no variance estimate attached, and the ablation deltas of 5 to 15 points are likewise single-run. The evaluation is English Wikipedia retrieval throughout, which the authors flag as shaping the learned behaviour.
- **Calibrating Post-Training Feature Shifts for LLM Data Contamination Detection** — Stated, and the most important one is a disclaimer about the paper's own framing: the method corrects score-increasing feature shifts observed on known non-members, and while post-training motivates the study, those shifts may equally reflect dataset artifacts, decoding behaviour or the feature extractor -- so what is identified is a shift associated with higher non-member scores during calibration, not a shift proved to be caused by post-training. The method also requires a pool of known non-members held outside the supervised classifier's training, which temporal benchmarks and post-cutoff data supply but which is hard to obtain when a model's cutoff is unknown or no trusted non-member pool exists; and the calibration examples remain in the reporting pool under their protocol. Not stated but worth noticing: the AUC improvements are one to four points, which on four benchmarks and three model families with no confidence intervals or repeated runs reported is a thin margin, and the large TPR movements are measured at a single operating point on benchmarks whose positive sets are small. The three target models are described only by family, so what specific post-training each underwent -- the variable the whole framing rests on -- is not controlled or varied, and no experiment compares a base model against its own post-trained descendant, which is the comparison that would establish the causal claim the method is named for.
- **V-FiLLM: Verified Financial LLM Reasoning Benchmark** — Stated. Final answers are correct by construction but the questions are not guaranteed to be, since the underlying mathematical steps may be rendered into English ambiguously or without sufficient context. Compute constraints limit the study to six models, English-language documents and a single table-formatting convention, with no repeated trials over the same questions -- the authors name increasing the number of tests and running multiple trials as future work for statistical significance. Most consequentially, the LoRA hyperparameter sweep was conducted on the same 90-problem held-out set used to report the result, so the authors state the gains should be read as preliminary evidence rather than as a measured improvement; the external-dataset check exists specifically to test whether that improvement was overfitting, and its 5-point movement on 100 items is described as a proof of concept. Not stated but worth noticing: the depth curves rest on 100 questions per cell with no intervals, and the two models compared under perturbation are a subset of the six benchmarked, so the robustness conclusions are about that pair. The generated-question design removes annotation noise but substitutes a different constraint -- what can be asked is what a computation tree over the table can express, which is arithmetic composition over retrieved values rather than the interpretive judgement much financial analysis involves.
- **SafeSceneReason: A Multimodal Reasoning Benchmark Connecting Industrial Hazards with Accident Knowledge** — Stated: the benchmark is described as a focused step rather than a representation of the occupational-safety lifecycle, with coverage bounded by the available workplace annotations, the selected accident-report sources and the safety concepts that traceable visual and textual evidence can support; small domains carry wide intervals that the authors explicitly warn against over-reading; and they conclude that high aggregate accuracy is insufficient for safety-critical use given the persistent failures in evidence integration, calibration and intervention selection. Not stated but worth noticing: the headline that a 9B model matches frontier systems is a within-distribution result -- it is fine-tuned on this corpus and evaluated on this corpus's test split, so it measures how learnable the benchmark's question templates are, not safety reasoning transfer, and the paper offers no held-out evaluation on a different safety resource. The evaluation is entirely multiple choice scored by exact option match, which cannot distinguish a correct answer reached from the wrong evidence, and the mitigation category -- arguably the one that matters for deployment -- rests on 27 questions. Scene-centric answers are deterministic by construction because they are program outputs over a scene graph, so their correctness inherits whatever the underlying annotations and rule encodings assume, and the report-centric half depends on MinerU's parsing of figures and captions from PDFs. Finally the domain-difficulty analysis uses a historical Kimi-K2.6 replay at 65.0% overall rather than the benchmark table, so the domain conclusions rest on one mid-tier model.
- **Beyond Solvability: Task Learnability as a Static Prior for LLM RL Post-Training** — The paper states three: the signal is validated only on mathematical and logical tasks with binary rewards, and whether it transfers to non-binary or model-graded rewards is open; evaluation is text-only; and compute limits confine experiments to smaller models, with larger scales left to future work. That last sentence says 'up to 8B parameters' while the models actually used are Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B and Llama-3.2-3B, so the stated ceiling is not the one exercised. A reader should add that all results are means over two seeds with no variance reported, and the standalone gains are 1.3 to 2.1 accuracy points -- real and consistent in direction across every cell, but small enough that two seeds is thin support for any individual number; the steps-to-baseline reductions are the more robust claim. Learnability is explicitly regime-conditional, so a prior estimated for one model family, algorithm and reward is not a property of the task, and the cross-regime reuse section reports transfer as useful but weaker than in-regime estimation. Finally, the probe reduction is verified by rank correlation against the oracle on one model and one pool, so the 0.940/0.876 figures are the evidence that the whole estimator rests on and they are measured once.
- **Mismatch Matters: On-Policy Distillation Beyond Token Agreement** — The paper states no limitations section. What a reader should notice: the effect size is almost entirely a function of the mismatch regime, and the weak-mismatch gain of 1.0 point over standard OPD sits within the spread of three other baselines (46.2 to 46.7), so the method is well supported where the student is far below the teacher and barely distinguishable where it is not -- the authors' own framing that gains are more pronounced under strong mismatch is accurate but does not fully convey that the near-tie is the ordinary case. Two teacher-student pairs support the whole claim, both on mathematics, and no seed variance is reported for the main tables, so the 13.4-point strong-mismatch gap is one comparison rather than a distribution. The strong-mismatch baseline is a 1.7B base model at 6.9 percent that generates 22K-token unterminated responses under standard OPD, which is a badly broken starting point, and much of what TIDE demonstrates is the repair of that breakage rather than transfer of the teacher's reasoning -- a distinction the generation diagnostics make visible but the accuracy headline does not. The 95.5 percent figure motivating analytic deficit injection is measured on one setting. And the method adds two gates plus a deficit weight; the sensitivity analysis shows accuracy is stable across them but response length and format errors are not, so tuning is still required if generation behaviour matters.
- **Post-Hoc Sparse Coding of Latent Communication Between Vision-Language Model Agents** — The paper's limitations section is longer than its results and is the most useful thing in it. The central admission is that the measurements establish empirical redundancy without identifying which part is structural, numerical, low-rank or specifically sparse, so the evidence is channel analysis and not a claim that sparsity is responsible for the compression. Two controls are named as decisive and absent. First, the codec's own documentation concentrates semantic structure in 18 positions, and an 18-position float16 payload is already close in size to the reported four-coefficient payload -- so simple position selection may account for most of the 128-fold figure. Second, with 98.78 percent of the dictionary dead, the 50-feature support may reflect autoencoder optimisation or low-rank structure rather than an identifiable sparse code. The authors list the baselines needed to separate these: matched-rate position selection, quantisation, PCA or a linear autoencoder, and vector quantisation, together with multiple seeds and held-out splits. All downstream results are single-run point estimates with no paired uncertainty, and the authors decline an equivalence claim on that basis. Everything runs on one sender-receiver pair, so feature overlap establishes reuse within this channel and not dictionary transfer. And the post-hoc design, while it cleanly separates observation from method, does not establish that an end-to-end sparse bottleneck would be better. Not stated but worth noticing: with a mean accuracy difference of 0.08 points against per-task swings of three to seven points in both directions, the headline that accuracy is preserved rests on cancellation across tasks rather than on stability within them.
- **MathShikkha: A Controlled Study of Answer-Only and Chain-of-Thought Supervision for Bangla Mathematical Reasoning in Small Language Models** — Stated or directly visible. The in-domain null is on one 291-item test set and its sign varies by seed, which the authors report rather than selecting the favourable run. Fine-tuning shows mild overfitting -- validation loss rises while training loss falls after an intermediate stage -- and the evaluated checkpoint is selected by lowest validation loss, which is a defensible choice made on the same validation split both conditions share. Some models fall slightly below the full external item count due to non-generating items, and the authors score each model on the common set its conditions all produced rather than imputing. A small fraction of one model's outputs were degenerate or truncated at the generation ceiling and are scored incorrect. Not stated but worth noticing: the two primary human annotators are co-authors, with an external expert used for adjudication rather than for independent scoring, so the reasoning-quality result rests on annotators who knew the study's structure even if not the condition labels. The rationales come from a single proprietary teacher, so 'what rationale supervision buys' here is what that teacher's Bangla rationales buy. Four backbones and 1,030 training problems in one language and one subject support the whole conclusion, and the capability-dependence thesis -- weakest backbone gains most in-domain, most language-adapted model gains least -- is an interpretation of four points.
- **MedCalc-R1: Knowledge-Guided Reward Framework for Medical Mathematical Reasoning** — Stated. Evaluation is confined to a single benchmark because it is the only dataset containing both calculation traces and the safety intervals the hard constraint requires, so generalisability is untested by construction rather than by omission. The framework is text-only while real clinical assessment synthesises imaging and vital signs. And the knowledge verification module depends on an external verifier, here a separate 14B model, which is a component whose own accuracy bounds the reward it supplies. Not stated but worth noticing: that verifier's error rate is never measured, so a formula reward computed by a model is subject to the same validity questions the archive raises about any model-supplied reward, and no human check of the verified formulas is reported. Formula recall is itself scored by a proprietary judge. The reward coefficients are fixed at 1.0 to avoid task-specific tuning, which is honest but means no sensitivity analysis over them exists, and the hard-constraint magnitudes and soft temperature are set by hand. Main-table numbers are single runs without seeds or intervals, and several of the strongest results -- dosage at 90.00, diagnosis at 73.33 -- sit on subtasks with 40 and 60 test instances.
- **PAST: Privileged Adaptation from Complete Student Trajectories for On-Policy Self-Distillation** — The paper states no limitations section. What a reader should notice: the headline rests on one 1.7B model, three seeds and 100 updates, evaluated on three 30-problem competition sets, so despite Avg@12 and a stratified paired bootstrap the confidence interval spans 2.6 to 8.3 points -- the effect is clearly positive but its size is not well determined. The scale check is a single seed and regresses on one of three tasks, so 'task-dependent gains' is the accurate reading and generality beyond 1.7B is not established. Most ablations are one seed, including the objective, regularization and sampling studies. The comparison holds the student-update count fixed, which is the right control for the claim being made but is not compute parity: PAST additionally trains a teacher and draws teacher samples, and the authors report those costs in a separate table rather than equalising them, so nothing here shows PAST beats a baseline given the same total budget. The theory is stated for the unclipped population objective under support and integrability conditions while the implementation uses pointwise clipping and a shared-parameter policy, so the fixed-point and improvement-direction results characterise the target the implementation aims at rather than the update it performs. Finally, verified success is the only outcome signal, so everything here is confined to domains with a reliable verifier.
- **When Is Benchmark Contamination Detectable? Information Limits and Power-Calibrated Audits** — The paper's own limitations section is unusually complete and should be read as part of the result. The mixture channel is an auditable modelling assumption rather than a law of training: contaminated examples can affect unexposed ones and post-training can alter both the clean and seen channels, and the stability diagnostics can reveal large violations but cannot prove transportability. The lower bound is pointwise in a chosen behavioural access channel and says nothing about direct corpus search, cryptographic provenance or watermarking. The matching optimality is local and assumes finite chi-square divergence. The certificate needs independent matched controls and a score learned on separate data, which an external auditor of a closed model generally cannot construct, so neither efficacy nor the exposed fraction is identified without extra assumptions. Six named gaps are open and unexecuted, including the locked external validation on the 25-model release that motivates the paper. A reader should add that the causal exposure claim rests on one base checkpoint (pythia-160m), one benchmark (SQuAD), one copy count and one protocol; that 'Pile-trained' does not certify the base never saw SQuAD, so only the incremental effect of the paired continuations is claimed; that the mechanism arms are not dose-matched, so the ordering is descriptive rather than a causal comparison; and that the three adjacent mechanism differences are significant per seed by sign-test but not after Holm correction.
- **Think Deep, Speak Once: Relit, A Recursive Latent Implicit Transformer Framework** — No limitations section, and the central comparison does not support the claim it is used for. ReLIT is **trained on each dataset's training split** -- 5,000 ProofWriter examples, 5,000 RuleTaker, 800 HELP, 2,000 TaxiNLI -- while every LLM number in the same table is taken from GLoRE under few-shot prompting. The paper says as much when introducing the setup and then tabulates the two side by side, so 'matching or outperforming significantly larger models' is a comparison between a task-specific trained head and models that saw no training data. On the two tasks where the training set is large and the label space is a verdict, ReLIT reaches 97-99 percent; on the three where it is small or the task is linguistic, it does not. Second, the repeated inference that stability implies correctness is not supported: 'the outputs remain consistent after this stabilization phase' and 'high confidence in the final supervision loops proves that the model is not hallucinating' conflate convergence with being right, and this archive holds a direct counter-case in work showing intermediate answers stabilize regardless of whether the answer is ultimately correct. Third, one backbone, one scale, no seeds or variance anywhere, and no ablation separating the residual-refinement novelty from deep supervision, adaptive halting or the frozen head. Finally, the efficiency argument is asserted rather than measured -- no latency, token or FLOP comparison against chain-of-thought appears, though avoiding token generation is the paper's motivation.
