## Scope

How a language model acquires long-form reasoning: reinforcement learning against verifiable rewards, process versus outcome supervision, distillation of reasoning traces, and self-training. The question the archive answers is which training signal produces reasoning that generalizes, and what each one costs.

## Where the field stands

### 2026

- **Chain-of-Thought Shows the Path to a Tree: Realizing Branching Complexity** — Constructs explicit two-layer hard-attention Transformer decoders that execute depth-first search and Dijkstra's algorithm step by step under chain of thought, then reuses those traversals as a substrate to compute two branching-complexity measures of a tree in a linear number of steps, without layer normalisation or positional encodings.
- **GRPO for Financial Advice Generation: Outperforming Commercial LLMs under CATE Evaluation** — Trains an open-weight model with GRPO against a safety-gated LLM-as-a-judge rubric for financial advice, then audits the result with a judge-independent causal estimator on logged outcomes -- and finds the two evaluations rank the other systems differently, with the untrained base model last on the rubric and second on the audit.
- **SCOUT: Unlocking Enhanced Spatial Reasoning via Structured Chain-of-Thought and Multi-Objective Process Reward** — Splits a spatial-reasoning chain of thought into explicitly typed segments -- perception, including depth, and reasoning -- and gives each its own process reward and its own advantage term, so that the two do not compete for credit under a single outcome signal.
- **LEMUR: Latent Entropy-aware Multimodal Unlearning via Visual-anchored Reasoning Redirection** — Finds that a fact successfully unlearned from a multimodal model's final answer can still be reproduced in its reasoning trace, far more in natively RL-trained models than in their base versions, and uses the token-level entropy signature RL leaves behind as a training-free control signal for redirecting the trace at decoding time.
- **When the API Speaks the Wrong Language: Revisiting Post-Training for Multilingual Tool Use** — Names and measures a multilingual tool-calling failure in which the model picks the right API but writes argument values in the wrong language, then compares supervised fine-tuning against PPO and GRPO under matched budgets and finds that a well-selected supervised checkpoint matches or beats reinforcement learning on the task while costing more elsewhere.
- **PAIR: Pairwise-Aware Inclusion Reweighting for Adaptive Rollout Allocation in RLVR** — Points out that the group-relative policy gradient is a second-order U-statistic over pairs of rollouts rather than a sum of independent per-rollout contributions, and reallocates rollout compute accordingly -- treating prefixes as vertices and pair-gradient terms as edges, then correcting the resulting selection bias by inverse joint-inclusion weighting.
- **FaithformBench: Benchmarking Faithfulness of Mathematical Chain-of-Thought Autoformalisation** — Tests whether systems that translate natural-language reasoning steps into Lean preserve invalidity as well as validity, by automatically perturbing steps to make them wrong, and finds pervasive silent correction -- with the systems best at preserving valid inputs the most likely to repair invalid ones.
- **ThinkRetrieve: Retrieval-Augmented Reasoning Traces for Test-Time Scaling** — Injects a retrieved solved problem, with its full worked solution, into the middle of a reasoning model's own thinking trace at each step boundary, using the model's current intermediate answer as the retrieval query.
- **XCoT-VLA: Executable Chain-of-Thought for Vision-Language-Action Driving** — Replaces a verbose natural-language rationale with two to six executable action tokens drawn from a fixed vocabulary, supervised automatically by pairing logged trajectories with scene context, so that driving-oriented reasoning fits inside a real-time control budget that verbose chain-of-thought exceeds by three to four times.
- **Social Chain of Thought: A Multi-Agent Architecture Grounded in Medical Differential Diagnosis Methodology** — Structures multi-agent medical differential diagnosis as rounds of persona-conditioned specialist deliberation, and shows the recall advantage is not reproduced by best-of-n sampling from the same model, concentrates entirely in the cases where monolithic inference fails, and reverses on the easiest quartile.
- **CARE: Confidence-Aware Reasoning for Reliable Medical VQA** — Adds a correctness-conditioned confidence term to the GRPO reward for medical visual question answering -- rewarding answer-token confidence when the answer is right and penalising it when wrong -- on top of an SFT cold start built from answer-conditioned reasoning traces filtered by a verifier.
- **ConRub-Med: Reinforcement Learning with Consensus Rubrics for Open-Ended Medical Question Answering** — Trains open-ended medical question answering by scoring each response against rubric criteria that three frontier models independently agreed on, grading each criterion as correct, missing or wrong rather than yes/no, and recovering a gradient in groups where every response ties by judging the responses pairwise in both orders.

### 2025

- **DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning** — Shows that reasoning ability can be incentivized in an LLM by pure reinforcement learning on verifiable tasks, with no human-annotated reasoning trajectories, and that the resulting reasoning patterns can be transferred to smaller models.
- **On Reasoning Strength Planning in Large Reasoning Models** — Shows that a reasoning model decides how long to think before emitting a single reasoning token — the eventual token count is linearly decodable from the question's activations at Spearman 0.84 — and that this plan is carried by one shared direction vector whose magnitude encodes strength and which acts by shifting the logits of the end-of-thinking token.
- **Measuring Chain-of-Thought Monitorability Through Faithfulness and Verbosity** — Argues that faithfulness alone is insufficient for CoT monitoring and adds verbosity — whether the trace lists every factor needed to solve the task — combining the two into a monitorability score, then shows models can look faithful while omitting key factors.
- **Dynamic Early Exit in Reasoning Models** — Detects the points where a reasoning model switches thought chains, interrupts to induce a trial answer, and stops generation when that answer's confidence is high enough — cutting chain-of-thought length substantially while raising accuracy, with no training.
- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards Implicitly Incentivizes Correct Reasoning in Base LLMs** — Shows that base models win pass@K on mathematics by producing wrong chains that land on right answers, and that scoring the chain too — CoT-Pass@K — reverses the verdict in RLVR's favour at every K.
- **Transformers Provably Learn Chain-of-Thought Reasoning with Length Generalization** — Gives the first optimization guarantee that gradient descent trains constant-depth transformers to solve NC1-complete problems with chain of thought, and shows the algebraic structure of the task decides how far the learned reasoning extrapolates.
- **Demystifying Reasoning Dynamics with Mutual Information: Thinking Tokens are Information Peaks in LLM Reasoning** — Tracks mutual information between each reasoning step's representation and the correct answer, finds it spikes at sparse 'MI peaks' that decode to reflective tokens like 'Wait' and 'Hmm', and shows suppressing exactly those tokens degrades reasoning while suppressing equally many others does not.
- **The Entropy Mechanism of Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning Language Models** — Establishes that RL trades policy entropy for reward along a predictable exponential curve R = -a·exp(H) + b, derives that entropy change is driven by the covariance between action probability and advantage, and controls it by restricting updates to the highest-covariance tokens.
- **The Overthinker's DIET: Cutting Token Calories with DIfficulty-AwarE Training** — Trains reasoning models to be concise in proportion to difficulty by modulating the token penalty and the target length per problem, and fixes a distortion that naive reward weighting introduces into group-normalized RL.
- **Beyond the 80/20 Rule: High-Entropy Minority Tokens Drive Effective Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning** — Shows that the roughly 20% of CoT tokens with the highest entropy act as decision forks, and that restricting RLVR policy-gradient updates to only those tokens matches or beats full-gradient training, with the advantage growing with model size.
- **Rethinking the Global Convergence of Softmax Policy Gradient with Linear Function Approximation** — Shows by two four-armed bandits with nearly identical approximation error, one of which converges and one of which does not, that approximation error cannot characterize whether softmax policy gradient reaches the optimum, and replaces it with a condition on whether the features preserve the ordering of the rewards.
- **Does Reinforcement Learning Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity in LLMs Beyond the Base Model?** — Measures RLVR-trained models against their base models with pass@k at large k and finds the base wins, concluding RLVR sharpens sampling toward paths the base already had rather than adding new ones.

### 2023

## Core ideas

### credit assignment

Deciding which part of a trajectory earned the reward, and across 31 sources the problem the archive's training literature keeps rediscovering under different names. The complaint is uniformly one of granularity mismatch: a policy is rewarded on whole outputs or whole trajectories while the decisions that produced the result happen at a finer scale, so nothing identifies which retrieval found the key document, which translation choice made the output unfaithful, or which of several changes packed into one code action earned the test pass. The difficulty is worst exactly where the trajectory is longest, since a sparse terminal reward over many turns supplies no supervision at the point of greatest ambiguity. The archive's methods split by where they cut. Some cut by turn, some by trajectory segment type, some by sub-parts of a single action matched across rollouts, and some by token selection -- and their reported contributions are consistent in size, typically 2 to 8 points, against ablations of the terminal signal that are typically 20 to 65. That asymmetry is the corpus's most reliable finding here: finer credit is a refinement of outcome supervision, never a substitute, and every source that removes the terminal reward finds it the largest single loss. Two design constraints follow and are stated independently by several sources. A local term should not be able to flip the sign of the trajectory-level advantage or create an update where the outcome reward is silent -- hence multiplicative rather than additive turn weighting, and gates that stop partial reference agreement overriding a wrong answer. And a criterion for allocating credit is not credited until it has been raced against random allocation at matched cost, because a randomised control routinely captures much of the benefit attributed to the targeted criterion.

Seen in: BiCAA: Bidirectional Credit Assignment for Search-Augmented Agent; EviSD: Evidence-Conditioned Self-Distillation for Search-Augmented Agents; Latent Thought Credit: Multi-Answer Credit Assignment for Latent Reasoning; GradCuit: Credit-Assigned Gradient Flow Enables Robust and Interpretable Test-Time Latent Reasoning.

### construct validity

Whether a measurement is measuring the thing it is named after, and across 28 sources the archive's most frequently applied critical lens -- usually applied by the authors to their own work, which is why it appears in limitations more often than in results. The recurring failure is a correlated alternative that the design cannot exclude. A temporal-horizon steering direction also separates abstraction, urgency, strategic scope and framing, so the intervention may be steering a broad answer register rather than the named variable, and a transfer result shows the construct is involved without identifying the direction's semantic basis. A probe's margin can be generic: training the identical probe on a small model that never produced the responses reaches 0.72 because the items are by construction more ambiguous as prompts, so only the 14-point margin above that is unexplained. And a total-effect measurement can be answering a different question entirely -- a revision that turns an unparseable output into a parseable one changes the score without changing any reasoning. Two conclusions the corpus reaches are worth carrying. Validity is a property of the whole protocol rather than of the model: measured performance depends on template, decoder, controller, reducer, verifier, stopping rule and numerical settings, so a number without its protocol is not comparable to another. And some constructs have no clean measurement window at all -- one source shows that models weak enough to have headroom lack the signal while models with the signal lack the headroom, leaving the design space where the construct could be measured cleanly nearly empty.

Seen in: Soft Guidance Starts to Outperform CoT Prompting as LLMs Improve; Intertemporal Preference Steering in Qwen3 via Contrastive Activation Addition; Test-Time Scaling in Reasoning LLMs: Inference Regimes, Evaluation, and Reproducibility; Monte Carlo Tree Search for Table-to-Multimodal Report Generation.

### overthinking

Generating more reasoning than a problem needs, and the archive's largest cluster at 25 sources. The sources agree on the symptom and split on the cause, which is what keeps the term loose. One account locates it after the answer, where double-checking continues once the correct result is derived. One locates it before the problem starts, since models cannot recognize difficulty in advance — and a reasoning model's eventual token count is linearly decodable from the question's activations before a single reasoning token is emitted, which makes the length a decision rather than an outcome. One locates it in the reward, where a sequence-level efficiency penalty implicitly punishes long but correct trajectories so that training against length damages the reasoning it was meant to trim. Reported reductions run from roughly 40% to 87%, occasionally with accuracy gains, which suggests a substantial share of a long chain does no work. Three results added since sharpen the picture. Redundancy turns out not to sit in an identifiable class of step: pruning that targets reflective statements is reported to do no better than pruning that ignores them, because the reasoning skeleton is repeated and rephrased throughout. Cutting by structure is nonetheless not the same as cutting by length — removing the same token count by position rather than by graph role costs twenty points of accuracy. And the decision of when to stop is proved harder than the field has assumed: a fixed threshold on the probability that the current prefix is already correct can be arbitrarily far from optimal even when that probability is known exactly, because the comparison that matters is against the value of continuing.

Seen in: Reinforcement Learning with Evolving Rubrics as Rewards for Audio Reasoning; The Tell-Tale Trace: Detecting Reasoning Failures in LLMs Using Chain-of-Thought Dynamics; Fewer Tokens, Smaller Cache: Reward-Coordinated Efficient Reasoning; CAT: Confidence-Adaptive Thinking for Efficient Reasoning of Large Reasoning Models.

### test-time scaling

Spending more computation at inference to get a better answer, and across 28 sources a phrase covering algorithms with different statistical structure, compute accounting and failure modes -- extending one trajectory, sampling completed candidates and reducing them by voting or verification, and searching over unfinished partial states. One source argues the consequence directly: reporting these under a single scalar budget, or reporting accuracy without the inference protocol that produced it, makes results incomparable across papers. The archive's accumulated findings are mostly about where the curve bends. It is not monotone in the sample count, because more candidates strengthen whatever the aggregation rule already does; the selection signal is the binding constraint past a modest budget, and where confidence does not track correctness no verifier-free rule recovers the available headroom (measured at 14 to 17 oracle points on one hard benchmark against 0.8 percent captured). Uniform spending is wasteful in both directions, and adaptive allocation is worth having but hard to learn and hard to audit. And its value is conditional on the instance rather than on the method: budget increases that pay on a feasible instance change nothing on an infeasible one. Two structural observations recur. The budget is almost always spent on the output side -- re-running the model on the same input -- and input-side diversity is a direct competitor for the same calls that has rarely been benchmarked against it at matched compute. And in multimodal settings the usual selection signals fail for a specific reason: neither answer frequency nor verbalised confidence tests whether an answer depends on the image, so a grounded answer and a confident language-prior guess look identical at the selection layer.

Seen in: s1: Simple test-time scaling; It's the Decoding Format, Not the Perturbation: Auditing Consistency-Based Selection for Vision-Language Test-Time Scaling; GradCuit: Credit-Assigned Gradient Flow Enables Robust and Interpretable Test-Time Latent Reasoning; Test-Time Scaling for Safe Text-Guided Image Generation via Intermediate Clean Estimates.

### chain of thought faithfulness

Whether a stated chain of thought is an account of the process that produced the answer, and across 26 sources the archive's central negative result. The measurements are consistent: faithfulness varies substantially across tasks, with models sometimes relying heavily on the trace and sometimes largely ignoring it, and it decreases with model size and capability on most tasks studied -- so the earliest source's own conclusion is a conditional (traces can be faithful if size and task are chosen carefully) rather than an endorsement. Two structural problems recur. The standard test detects unfaithfulness only where an injected cue changes the answer, so it is silent whenever the answer is unchanged, and it intervenes on the prompt rather than on the trace. And the property is not simply desirable: a perfectly faithful agent executes a harmful action whenever its reasoning arrives at one, which makes the usual requirement self-contradictory -- faithful enough to be monitored, robust enough to override its own reasoning. One source measures that tension directly, finding the most faithful model follows tampered safe reasoning in 97.5 percent of cases while rejecting unsafe reasoning in only 12.3, higher faithfulness going with lower safety across seven models, and steering along the two directions trading them against each other by 5 to 9 points against magnitude-matched random vectors moving either by at most 1.1. The archive's operative position is that faithfulness is a relation between the trace and the answer rather than a property of the trace: monitoring works as a consistency check between the two, and a trace can be clean, correct and on-topic while the action taken is arbitrary.

Seen in: Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think: Unfaithful Explanations in Chain-of-Thought Prompting; Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning; Does Accuracy Equal Evidence? Reasoning Faithfulness under KV Cache Compression; How Much Does a Reasoning Summary Reveal? An Observability Ladder for Large Language Models.

### adaptive compute allocation

Spending different amounts of inference compute on different inputs, on the premise that a fixed budget wastes effort on easy instances and underserves hard ones. Across 25 sources the case for it is well established and the case for any particular controller is not. Three findings shape how the archive reads such claims. Confounding: a method that changes both the budget and the answer selector cannot say which produced its result, which is why replay protocols holding the candidate bank fixed and varying only the reducer are the informative design -- and under one, selecting by mean token log probability falls from 75.56 to 65.83 percent as the budget grows to eighty while answer plurality rises to 78.33 on the same banks. Controllers are hard to learn and hard to audit: one paper evaluated a learned controller that degenerated to a near-greedy policy under leave-one-dataset-out evaluation and reports it rather than dropping it, and another argues for a transparent controller on the grounds that a fixed budget at least explains itself while a learned policy explains nothing about why a prompt got what it got. That same source reports its own ablations against itself -- removing the entropy signal improves mathematics accuracy, and neutralising confidence improves it while forcing the budget to maximum -- and concludes the controller should be read as a transparent allocation policy rather than an optimised one. And the gain is conditional on the instance rather than on the method: a search planner's yield rises smoothly from 15 to 39 percent between 100 and 5,000 iterations on a nominal instance while a fourfold budget increase changes nothing on an adverse one, because feasibility rather than thoroughness binds. Where the savings are real they are usually large and skewed: a latent-reasoning system cuts 268.56 rationale tokens to 1.60 with an 8.9-fold speedup, and a safety pipeline raises median latency on unsafe prompts from 1.91 to 7.06 seconds while leaving benign prompts at 1.8 to 1.9 regardless of the maximum allowed -- the cost is conditional on detection, not on the budget. Several sources note that adaptive often means whether rather than how much, and that controller overhead against the samples saved is rarely reported.

Seen in: Test-Time Scaling for Safe Text-Guided Image Generation via Intermediate Clean Estimates; LatentGuard: Efficient and Inspectable Latent Reasoning for LLM Safeguards; Interpretable Adaptive Sampling for LLM Test-Time Scaling; Test-Time Scaling in Reasoning LLMs: Inference Regimes, Evaluation, and Reproducibility.

### advantage estimation

Deciding how much better than a baseline a rollout was, which is what a policy update is actually made of. Across 23 sources the corpus's contribution is diagnostic rather than definitional: it shows what goes wrong when the estimate is asymmetric, degenerate or unidentified. Asymmetry is the sharpest case. A regression critic that overestimates success probability gives failed rollouts large negative advantages and successes small positive ones -- measured at -0.597 against +0.227, a ratio of 2.63, growing more skewed over training -- so the update spends most of its magnitude on failures; a distributional critic brings the ratio to 1.02, and the paper notes correctly that batch-wise normalisation is an affine rescaling that cannot remove a relative asymmetry. Degeneracy is the group-relative case: a prompt whose rollouts all share a reward has zero advantage for every member and contributes no gradient, and one source measures a first stage cutting all-wrong groups by 4.95 points precisely to address it. Collapse is a third failure: in latent reasoning, within-thought answer variance exceeds between-thought variance in every setting and training makes it worse (noise-to-signal 1.54 rising to 13.10), so the thought-level advantage is estimating differences that have largely disappeared. Two further findings recur. Weighting is a bias-variance trade rather than a free improvement -- one truncation-order sweep peaks at 4 and degrades beyond it because higher moments cannot be estimated from eight rollouts, and one interpolation sweep runs 45.4, 52.1, 44.8 as its coefficient goes 0.00, 0.25, 1.00. And the corpus's methodological caution applies with unusual force here: margins of 0.75 to 2.6 average points from single runs with no seeds are the norm, and one source's headline is the best average across checkpoints evaluated on the evaluation benchmarks rather than on a held-out criterion.

Seen in: BiCAA: Bidirectional Credit Assignment for Search-Augmented Agent; EviSD: Evidence-Conditioned Self-Distillation for Search-Augmented Agents; Latent Thought Credit: Multi-Answer Credit Assignment for Latent Reasoning; Self-Improving Large Language Models via Progressive Experience Evolution.

### detection versus control

Whether a direction that separates a property from its absence is also a direction that changes it when you push along it -- and across 21 sources the assumption that the two coincide is the one the archive has most thoroughly falsified. The assumption is load-bearing rather than incidental: it is what licenses selecting a steering direction by its discriminability, which is exactly what the foundational method does when it picks the most discriminative attention heads. The direct refutation is quantitative. Inverted vectors -- high discriminability with a strongly negative relationship between steering strength and concept score -- are mined systematically rather than found by chance, three per model-concept pair across 15 pairs, one reaching an AUC of 0.97 with a Spearman correlation of -1. So alignment with positive representations does not determine even the sign of the steering effect, and the worst untreated case moves a concept backwards (promotion score 1.50 against an unsteered 1.75, corrected to 3.57). The authors read this as the explanation for the variability in steering efficacy reported across model-concept pairs in prior work. Several sources have absorbed the lesson into their methods rather than their conclusions: one reports its difference-of-means family as the weakest of four classifiers and declines to pick the intervention by classification accuracy; another finds probe-steering agreement near zero in early layers (-0.03 to 0.07) rising to about 0.91 at the final layer, and derives an early-layer waiver from that curve rather than from convenience. Two further asymmetries appear. Detection generalises where control does not, and control generalises where detection does not: a temporal-horizon direction trained on binary answers transfers to a monetary task with a different format, while a defect direction fitted on controlled pairs scores 83.3 percent held out on those pairs and 19.6 to 30.0 percent on real benchmarks. And a steering effect can be damage: the archive's recurring demand is a matched null -- a random direction of comparable activation volume -- without which suppressing a feature and observing degradation reports that something changed rather than that the right thing did.

### pass@k

The probability that at least one of k sampled responses is correct, used across 21 sources as the archive's measure of what a model can reach as opposed to what it reliably produces. Reading it correctly is most of what the corpus has to say. Small k measures sampling efficiency and large k probes the boundary, and the two can move in opposite directions within one training run -- pass@1 rising across steps while coverage at pass@256 falls, and in one distillation study the curve crossing between k=1 and k=1024 in every setting measured. A rising curve is also compatible with within-question stability collapsing, so reporting only coverage hides that the same model now answers inconsistently. Two corrections to naive use are established. Scoring the chain as well as the answer reverses the verdict about reinforcement learning at every k, because base models win plain pass@k partly by producing wrong chains that land on right answers. And for candidate banks produced by adaptive search or dependent procedures the usual estimator is a descriptive summary of what was returned rather than an unbiased probability -- one source states this and notes most papers reporting the metric do not. The archive's practical demand is that a method report pass@k across a range rather than at one budget, since the property worth having is competitiveness with the base model uniformly across k rather than a win at either end.

Seen in: BODHI: Do LLMs Branch Out and Discover Heterogeneous Inferences?; When Correct Solutions Repeat: Rarity-Aware Credit Redistribution for GRPO; Test-Time Scaling in Reasoning LLMs: Inference Regimes, Evaluation, and Reproducibility; PAST: Privileged Adaptation from Complete Student Trajectories for On-Policy Self-Distillation.

### test-time compute

Computation spent at inference rather than in training, and the resource this archive's largest engineering literature allocates. Nineteen sources treat it as something to be spent well rather than merely spent, and they differ on what to buy with it: more samples, longer chains, refinement of existing chains, search over reasoning strategies, re-examination of the input, or evaluation of candidates — with one source showing evaluation-time compute substitutes for generation-time compute at a comparable rate. Two results give the concept firmer footing than a scaling curve. Complexity theory makes the number of decoding steps a computational resource akin to time, with named classes attached to each regime. And optimal-stopping theory says when to stop spending: aggregation schemes exist whose failure probability provably decays to zero, while majority voting can converge to zero success when a wrong answer is individually more likely than the right one. The recurring practical finding is that uniform allocation is wrong, because the gain is concentrated on problems the model finds hard and the waste on the ones it does not.

Seen in: Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning; Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally can be More Effective than Scaling Model Parameters; GradCuit: Credit-Assigned Gradient Flow Enables Robust and Interpretable Test-Time Latent Reasoning; Refining Over Resampling: Test-Time Self-Correction for LLM Reasoning.

### monitorability

How much an observer can learn about what a model is doing by reading what it emits, and across 18 sources the safety-adjacent property the archive treats as a relation between task, access and monitor rather than a property of a model. The measured findings: reasoning models are consistently more monitorable than their instruction-tuned counterparts on both axes across all four model pairs tested, with the highest at 78.3 percent average and the largest gaps on the cue where concealment would matter most; difficulty is inversely related to it, with the hardest science benchmark lowest; and scores are similar across all four trace-transition types, which is that source's argument that marking non-switch cases faithful by default throws away most of the available signal. Three limits are stated by the sources themselves. It is almost always evaluated where the prompt directly incentivises concealment, which is not the deployment case. Detection depends on the monitor, so a number bounds that monitor rather than monitorability in general. And where the trace is latent there is no trace to read, so monitoring must fall back on probing or verbalising the latent states, and how much is retained that way is unknown. The archive's own position, from its findings, is that monitoring works as a consistency check between a trace and the answer it accompanies rather than as anomaly detection within the trace.

### outcome reward

Scoring a whole trajectory by whether its final answer is correct, which collapses everything the model did into one bit. Across 18 sources it is the cheap, load-bearing signal that nothing has been able to replace. The complaint is uniform: a ten-retrieval search trajectory receives one scalar and nothing distinguishes the retrieval that found the key document from one that repeated a query; the reward supervises only the answer, so a model can reach it without attending to the audio, the image or the intermediate quantities at all; and over a long tool-use trajectory it supplies no supervision at the exact point where credit is most ambiguous. Yet every source that ablates it finds it indispensable rather than merely helpful, and the archive holds no instance of dense supervision anchoring a policy on its own -- removing the terminal reward is repeatedly the largest single loss, and one system with it removed keeps perfect step-level tool selection while never terminating. The design consequence the sources converge on is that dense signals should be applied as modifiers that cannot flip a sign or create an update where the outcome reward is silent: one applies its turn-level term multiplicatively rather than additively for exactly this reason, another gates partial reference agreement so it cannot override a wrong final answer, and a third bounds a privileged correction by the magnitude of the outcome advantage. A separate limitation is that its scope is the answer: post-training against it improves solution accuracy without necessarily improving the semantic validity of the traces, which one source notes should be unsurprising given what it scores.

## Reading path

**Start here** — the anchor papers for this topic:

1. 2501.12948
1. 2305.20050
1. 2203.14465

**Then, in order of relevance:**

1. **CAT: Confidence-Adaptive Thinking for Efficient Reasoning of Large Reasoning Models** (2026)
   - Feeds a reasoning model's own self-certainty into preference optimization so it compresses confident answers and keeps deliberating on uncertain ones.
   - <https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-industry.152>
2. **Red Teaming Large Reasoning Models** (2026)
   - A trustworthiness benchmark for reasoning models over truthfulness, safety and efficiency, using training paradigm as an analytical axis, and finding reasoning models more fragile than plain LLMs to reasoning-induced risks.
   - <https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1034>
3. **FoE: Forest of Errors Makes the First Solution the Best in Large Reasoning Models** (2026)
   - Finds that a reasoning model's first solution is usually its best and that later alternatives are actively harmful, characterizes the errors as a forest structure, and prunes accordingly.
   - <https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1128>
4. **Think Better, Not Longer: Token-Level Marginal Utility for Efficient Reasoning in Large Reasoning Models** (2026)
   - Defines a token's marginal utility as its log-probability gain for the ground-truth answer, then trains against negative-utility tokens to shorten chains of thought.
   - <https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1386>
5. **CiPO: Counterfactual Unlearning for Large Reasoning Models through Iterative Preference Optimization** (2026)
   - Reframes unlearning in reasoning models as an intervention on the CoT itself, having the model generate logically valid counterfactual traces and iteratively preference-tuning toward them.
   - <https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.143>
6. **ReasoningGuard: Safeguarding Large Reasoning Models with Inference-time Safety Aha Moments** (2026)
   - An inference-time safeguard that reads a reasoning model's attention to find key points in its reasoning path and injects safety reflections there, then scales sampling to pick a safe path.
   - <https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1453>
7. **Optimizing Length Compression in Large Reasoning Models** (2026)
   - Identifies double-checking after the correct answer is already derived as 'invalid thinking', and trains a GRPO variant with a compress reward that targets exactly that portion.
   - <https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.146>
8. **Quantifying and Understanding Uncertainty in Large Reasoning Models** (2026)
   - Applies conformal prediction to the joint reasoning-answer structure of reasoning models, then attributes coverage to specific training examples and reasoning steps with Shapley values.
   - <https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1511>
9. **Think How to Think: Mitigating Overthinking with Autonomous Difficulty Cognition in Large Reasoning Models** (2026)
   - Two-stage fine-tuning that first injects difficulty cues into output prefixes for prospective strategy selection, then injects redundancy cues mid-reasoning for retrospective correction.
   - <https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1766>
10. **Scaling Reasoning, Losing Control: Evaluating Instruction Following in Large Reasoning Models** (2026)
   - A benchmark showing that as reasoning capacity grows, instruction adherence falls, and that recovering obedience costs reasoning performance.
   - <https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1878>

## Open problems

Drawn from the limitations each paper states about itself, so this is what the field admits it cannot do yet.

- **Chain-of-Thought Shows the Path to a Tree: Realizing Branching Complexity** — The authors state: the constructions are not unique and alternatives satisfying the same requirements may exist -- demonstrated within the paper, since bilinear gating used in one theorem is shown circumventable in another; several constructions invoke a bilinear operation, which they place within the conventions of this line of work; the closing operation that fixes the Strahler number in the path construction sits marginally outside the chain-of-thought iteration; and the analogous correspondence with Lukasiewicz paths is unstudied. They also raise optimality as open -- nothing here minimises embedding dimension, depth or head count. Reader-visible additions: there are no experiments, so nothing establishes that a trained Transformer finds these constructions or anything like them; the results are about what the architecture can represent, not what gradient descent produces. The decoders are unique hard-attention, an idealisation of softmax attention, and the NC1-completeness anchor is proved for binary trees given as terms while the constructions are for arbitrary ordered trees, so the completeness claim and the construction are adjacent rather than identical settings.
- **GRPO for Financial Advice Generation: Outperforming Commercial LLMs under CATE Evaluation** — Stated. Randomised experiments are not available, so the causal audit is computed from logged observational data with the standard weaknesses of off-policy evaluation, and the authors explicitly decline to treat it as a standalone measure, presenting it as a judge-independent complementary signal to be read alongside the rubric. Coverage is the second stated limit and it is a real one: recommendations with no confident match in the 60-action catalogue are dropped, so each policy is audited on whatever subset of its own output the catalogue can express, and a policy whose unmatched recommendations differ systematically in quality from its matched ones would be scored on an unrepresentative sample -- with no measurement of how large that excluded fraction is per policy. The audit scores a single recommended action per business state rather than multi-step advice where effects compound, and targets gross profit alone rather than a range of financial indicators. Not stated but worth noticing: the rubric that supplies the training reward and the rubric that supplies the headline evaluation are the same instrument, so the first result is a within-objective measurement by construction and only the audit is an outside check; the audit's own action mapping and outcome bins are authored by the same team; and the whole study runs on one company's proprietary business records, so nothing here is externally reproducible.
- **SCOUT: Unlocking Enhanced Spatial Reasoning via Structured Chain-of-Thought and Multi-Objective Process Reward** — The paper states no limitations section. What a reader should notice: the coefficient sweep is monotone over its three tested values with the chosen setting at the edge of the swept range, so nothing establishes that 0.3 is optimal rather than the smallest value tried, and the sweep constrains the two coefficients to be equal while the ablation's whole argument is that they govern different things. The out-of-domain regression on numerical video questions is large -- 11 points at 7B, worse than at 3B, so it grows with scale -- and the explanation offered is plausible but untested; a method that improves multiple-choice while degrading numerical estimation on the same benchmark has changed something more specific than 'intrinsic structural spatial understanding'. The ablation runs at 3B only, so whether the same components carry the same weight at 7B is unmeasured. No confidence intervals, seeds or repeated runs are reported anywhere, and several headline margins between adjacent configurations are under a point. Comparison against a proprietary model is against whatever version and prompt the authors used, with the reasoning-oriented instruction adaptation applied where applicable, so that ranking is not a controlled comparison.
- **LEMUR: Latent Entropy-aware Multimodal Unlearning via Visual-anchored Reasoning Redirection** — The paper has no limitations section. The most consequential caveat is one the authors report themselves in the transfer experiment: the entropy signature the method is named for is a property of RL training and does not exist in non-RL models, where the method still works but for a different reason -- so the mechanism story and the empirical result come apart, and what generalises is the visual anchoring rather than the entropy control. Not stated but worth noticing: the Reasoning Retention Ability metric, which carries the entire utility-preservation claim against gradient-based baselines, is an automatic judgement of fluency and naturalness by a single proprietary model, with no human validation and no reported agreement, and the differences it reports between methods are fractions of a point on a small scale. Subject-level reasoning leakage is measured against curated attribute lists, so it detects the attributes someone enumerated and cannot bound what else a trace reveals. Forget ratios run only to 15 percent and the evaluation is one dataset family across three splits; no adversarial probing of the sanitised model is attempted, so the result is that the fact stops appearing under the evaluated queries rather than that it has been removed. And the intervention is inference-time, which is its main advantage over retraining and also means it is a filter that a deployment must keep running rather than a change to the model.
- **When the API Speaks the Wrong Language: Revisiting Post-Training for Multilingual Tool Use** — The paper states no limitations section. Reader-visible limits: training is on one language and the entire generalisation claim rests on transfer to three related European languages, so nothing is established for scripts or morphologies further from the training language. The headline comparison depends entirely on which protocol is read, and the two disagree in direction; the authors report both and argue for the best-checkpoint reading, but validation selection on function-call match is itself a form of tuning that the epoch-fixed runs do not receive on any other axis. The reasoning-preservation result is a single benchmark with one run per method and language, and the authors themselves describe the non-English movements as likely noisy, which leaves the claim resting on one 8.6-point English drop. The reward ablation varies granularity but not the reward's scale or the number of terms, so denser and better-shaped are not separated. No seeds, no intervals anywhere. The argument-language-consistency metric is thresholded at 90 percent of a three-level per-argument score, so a call with one mismatched argument out of many can still pass.
- **PAIR: Pairwise-Aware Inclusion Reweighting for Adaptive Rollout Allocation in RLVR** — Stated precisely. The exact guarantee concerns an on-policy, sequence-level, unclipped, unstandardised leave-one-out score gradient, whereas standard practice divides by a random within-group standard deviation and performs several clipped policy-optimisation epochs -- so the deployed variant is an approximation to the target the theory covers, and the authors say so directly. Prefix predictors may drift as the policy changes, and severe miscalibration can assign low continuation probabilities to valuable endpoints, producing high-variance inverse weights despite the positivity floor. The convex objective is a valid upper bound only under stated conditions. Deterministic outcome pruning is deliberately excluded because it violates the positivity the correction depends on, which means the method cannot be combined with the aggressive filtering some allocators use. Not stated but worth noticing: the benchmark comparison reports single numbers per cell with no seed variance, and the margins over the strongest pointwise allocator are 1.2 and 1.4 points, which is small relative to the spread among the pointwise allocators themselves; the frozen-population audit is the statistically rigorous half and the training comparison is not held to the same standard. All results are on one model family at two sizes.
- **FaithformBench: Benchmarking Faithfulness of Mathematical Chain-of-Thought Autoformalisation** — Stated, and the authors are explicit that their benchmark is a diagnostic rather than a certificate. The method does not assess semantic drift in full generality and is therefore not complete: a perfect score does not imply faithfulness, and they give a constructed example that would pass while being clearly unfaithful. It also depends on access to a strong prover, and where the prover is not powerful enough the method returns inconclusive results even for a system that is clearly error-inducing or silently correcting -- which means the inconclusive rate is partly a property of the prover rather than of the system under test, and the specialised systems carry high inconclusive rates. Their recommendation is that the benchmark be used as one diagnostic among others rather than as a complete reliability certificate. They also note that Lean formalisations inherit errors and biases from the underlying libraries. Not stated but worth noticing: the perturbations are generated to render a step invalid, so what is measured is preservation of a particular constructed kind of invalidity rather than of naturally occurring reasoning errors, and whether models silently repair the errors real chains actually contain is a different question. The comparison between specialised and frontier systems is confounded by size and context length, which the authors acknowledge without controlling for.
- **ThinkRetrieve: Retrieval-Augmented Reasoning Traces for Test-Time Scaling** — No limitations section in the material read. What a reader should weigh first is the retrieval encoder: the related-work section cites a result that structurally faithful retrieval over mathematics is hard with off-the-shelf encoders, and the method then uses an off-the-shelf E5-Large, with the encoder choice relegated to an ablation. Second, decontamination is by cosine similarity at 0.90 and the audit reports maximum retained similarities of 0.898 and 0.891 -- immediately below the cut, with mean retained similarities of 0.866 and 0.845, which the paper attributes to the structural density of synthetic math corpora rather than leakage, and defends with an answer-distinct retrieval control rather than with a lower threshold. Third, the headline gains sit on AIME 2025, which is 30 problems, so +13.4 is four problems even at three seeds. Fourth, three of the four benchmarks share the same NuminaMath bank and the fourth uses its own training split, so 'adapts to different example banks' rests on one swap, and nothing here tests a domain where a bank of solved problems does not exist. Finally, the entropy reduction is offered as the explanation of the gain but is measured alongside it rather than manipulated, so it is a correlate of the improvement and not shown to cause it.
- **XCoT-VLA: Executable Chain-of-Thought for Vision-Language-Action Driving** — Stated where it matters. The latency analysis explicitly covers only the reasoning interface and excludes perception preprocessing, trajectory postprocessing and full-stack scheduling overhead, so the budget comparison is about the component under study rather than about a deployed system. The ablation split is marked as not numerically comparable with the main results table, which prevents the most likely misreading. Not stated but worth noticing: all results are open-loop, so nothing here measures what happens when the policy's own errors compound over a closed loop, which is the regime the real-time framing is about. The token vocabulary is designed by hand and its coverage bounds what the reasoning can express -- a situation requiring a manoeuvre outside the vocabulary has no representation, and no analysis of vocabulary coverage or of out-of-vocabulary behaviour is reported. The automatic Reason-Action supervision infers intent from what was logged, so it encodes the demonstrated behaviour as the correct reason, and a systematically suboptimal driving log would teach systematically suboptimal reasoning without that being visible in the metrics. Single runs throughout, no seeds or intervals, and the reinforcement-learning refinement is described as optional but its separate contribution is not isolated in the tables reported here.
- **Social Chain of Thought: A Multi-Agent Architecture Grounded in Medical Differential Diagnosis Methodology** — Stated: the demonstration is not exhaustive and other configurations remain untested; the comparison with frontier models is described as exploratory and restricted by proprietary API cost; more open-weight testing is wanted; and the authors flag that persona conditioning risks reproducing stereotypes the models were trained on. They are also explicit that a shared model backend leaves the endogeneity problem the architecture is meant to address only partly addressed, since the personas are simulated rather than genuinely independent. Not stated but worth noticing: difficulty quartiles are defined by the baseline's own F1 on each case, so the hardest quartile is selected for baseline failure and some of the 71.4 percent relative lift is regression toward the mean -- the convergence-pathway analysis is what makes the mechanistic reading credible, not the quartile lift itself. The headline is a recall advantage while the easiest-quartile cost is a precision loss, and in a diagnostic setting those have very different consequences; no operating-point analysis or downstream harm framing is offered. Evaluation is against ground-truth diagnosis term lists on 570 vignettes with no clinician assessment of the differentials produced.
- **CARE: Confidence-Aware Reasoning for Reliable Medical VQA** — The paper has no limitations section and its evaluation has a structural problem the text does not address: the calibration reward optimises mean answer-token probability, and ECE is computed from confidence scores binned the same way, so the training objective and the headline calibration metric are functions of the same quantity. A lower ECE under those conditions is partly a check that optimisation succeeded rather than independent evidence of calibration; nothing here tests the confidence against a held-out proper scoring rule, against sampled agreement, or against clinician judgement. Second, the ablation shows the full pipeline is not the best configuration for closed-ended questions on any benchmark, which is not reconciled with the main table. Third, the reasoning traces are generated with the answer in hand and admitted only when they reach that answer, so the corpus is selected for post-hoc rationalisations that happen to land correctly; the hallucination reduction the authors attribute to 'answer-grounded reasoning paths' is equally consistent with traces that were filtered for agreeing with the label. Fourth, the hallucination metric is a single VLM judge with a hand-chosen quadratic penalty and a global normalisation constant, reported without agreement against human labels or a second judge. Fifth, single runs, no seeds, no intervals, and the verifier is a proprietary model.
- **ConRub-Med: Reinforcement Learning with Consensus Rubrics for Open-Ended Medical Question Answering** — The authors state: three generators can share an error and unanimity can exclude a criterion only one model saw; the expert study covers 17 matched questions; rubric construction depends on several large hosted models; pairwise advantages apply only to exact ties, and extending them to near ties would need a calibrated margin; the pairwise directions come from one Qwen3-32B judge whose agreement with other judges and with clinicians is unstudied; and compute cost limits all policy comparisons to a single training seed. Their ethical statement adds that errors in model-generated criteria or model judges become training signals. Reader-visible additions: the expert agreement kappas are low (0.357 for non-redundancy, 0.475 for clinical relevance) even where raw agreement is above 0.93, which is what happens when a class is near ceiling and means the headline 12.5-point relevance gain rests on a small number of discordant judgements; only the clinical-relevance dimension moves, and the all-four-properties gain of 2.9 and 5.9 points has intervals straddling zero. The pairwise component changes the medical average by 0.25 and is credited with a 30-point IFEval swing, which is a large effect from a mechanism reaching 10.63 percent of tied groups and invites the alternative reading that the tied-group intervention mainly prevents a degeneracy the other components introduced. Single training seed throughout, and most benchmark scores are a single evaluation pass.
