Mechanistic Interpretability Should Prioritize Feature Consistency in Sparse Autoencoders

Xiangchen Song, Aashiq Muhamed, Yujia Zheng 0001, Lingjing Kong et al. · ACL · 2026-01-01 · dblp

<https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.99>

Argues run-to-run feature consistency should be a standard SAE evaluation axis alongside reconstruction and sparsity, and gives a metric showing high consistency is achievable.

Problem. SAEs are used to decompose activations into interpretable features, and the aspiration to find a canonical feature set is undermined by learned features being inconsistent across training runs. That inconsistency breaks reproducibility and makes model comparison unreliable — if two runs on the same activations give different dictionaries, a claim about a feature is a claim about a run.

Contributions.

  • The argument that run-to-run feature consistency belongs alongside reconstruction and sparsity as a standard SAE evaluation axis
  • PW-MCC, an assignment-based metric for dictionary consistency across runs
  • Theoretical grounding for strong consistency in the idealized TopK SAE setting
  • Synthetic validation on a model organism verifying PW-MCC as a proxy for ground-truth recovery
  • Empirical demonstration of PW-MCC around 0.80 for TopK SAEs on LLM activations, correlating with explanation similarity

Method. Run-to-run feature consistency is studied directly and proposed as a reported evaluation axis alongside reconstruction and sparsity. The Pairwise Dictionary Mean Correlation Coefficient is an assignment-based metric quantifying consistency: features are matched between runs before being compared, which is what makes the measure invariant to arbitrary feature ordering. Three lines of support are given — theoretical grounding for strong consistency in the idealized TopK setting, synthetic validation on a model organism verifying PW-MCC as a proxy for ground-truth recovery, and empirical analysis on LLM activations.

Results. High consistency is achievable with appropriate architectural choices, with PW-MCC around 0.80 for TopK SAEs on LLM activations. In the synthetic setting PW-MCC is verified as a reliable proxy for ground-truth feature recovery. On LLM activations PW-MCC correlates with the similarity of automatically generated natural-language feature explanations.

Limitations. The theoretical result covers the idealized TopK case, so it does not cover other SAE architectures. PW-MCC around 0.80 is high but not near-identity, so a fifth of the dictionary still differs between runs and which features those are is not characterized. Synthetic validation uses a model organism whose relationship to real activation structure is an assumption. Correlation with automatically generated explanations inherits the reliability of the auto-interpretation pipeline.

Relevance. Directly conditions how much weight this archive's SAE results can carry. Every claim here that a particular feature does a particular thing was read off one training run, and if a fifth of the dictionary is run-dependent then some fraction of those claims are artefacts of a seed. PW-MCC makes that checkable and the paper shows ~0.80 is reachable, so the fix is architectural rather than hopeless. It is also the most concrete instance of what doi:10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.159 in this drain calls for — an auditing axis the field should report as standard — and it pairs with arxiv:2608.06300's finding that SAEs improve recoverability while attenuating measured influence: consistency and validity are separate properties, and an SAE can now be shown to have the first without the second.

CircuitSteer: Geometrically Aligned Multi-Layer Steering via Sparse Autoencoder Circuits

Mehrshad Saadatinia, Parsa Razmara, Ardalan Aryashad, Ali Abbasi et al. · 2026-08-06 · arxiv

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05732>

Builds multi-layer steering vectors from SAE features selected by co-activation and decoder-direction alignment, and intervenes at several points instead of one.

Problem. Steering methods such as Contrastive Activation Addition rely on fixed single-layer interventions derived from aggregate activation differences. One intervention is imposed across semantically diverse inputs, and the effect often fails to persist across layers, which limits how much behaviour actually changes.

Contributions.

  • CircuitSteer: identification of multi-layer semantic subcircuits from SAE features
  • A feature flow circuit built from feature co-activation plus geometric alignment of decoder directions
  • Synthesis of dense steering vectors from sparse features, applied as multi-point interventions
  • Evidence that multi-layer circuit steering preserves fluency where single-point methods do not, including on sycophancy and refusal where baselines fail entirely

Method. CircuitSteer uses Sparse Autoencoders to find coherent semantic circuits distributed over multiple layers. A feature flow circuit is constructed from feature co-activation together with the geometric alignment of decoder directions — the alignment criterion is what links features across layers into a circuit rather than a set. The isolated multi-layer subcircuit's sparse features are then synthesized into dense steering vectors, applied as multi-point interventions to guide the model's internal semantic trajectory.

Results. Evaluated with contrastive examples on toxicity, emotion-intensity, sycophancy and refusal across two model families. CircuitSteer is the only method to consistently produce fluency-preserving interventions; competing methods either sacrifice text quality or lack coverage, failing entirely on sycophancy and refusal.

Limitations. No quantitative results in the abstract and the model families are not named. Behaviours tested are alignment-style attributes, not reasoning, so nothing is shown about steering a reasoning process. Requires a trained SAE for every layer involved, which bounds applicability to models with available SAEs. Fluency preservation is reported as a comparative claim without a stated metric.

Relevance. Extends the archive's SAE line from reading features to acting on them across layers, and supplies a selection criterion — decoder-direction alignment plus co-activation — for deciding which features form a circuit. The reported failure of single-layer baselines on sycophancy and refusal is the substantive finding: those are the behaviours that do not localize to one layer, which is indirect evidence about which computations are distributed. Nothing here is tested on reasoning, so whether a reasoning circuit is steerable the same way is open, and that gap is the obvious follow-up given the Step-Level SAE work already in the archive.

MI-MIDI: Mechanistic Interpretability of Text-to-MIDI Generation Models via Probing, Lenses and Steering

Jakub Poćwiardowski, Mateusz Modrzejewski · 2026-08-06 · arxiv

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06638>

Applies linear probing, the logit and tuned lenses, activation patching and difference-in-means steering to two public text-to-MIDI models, and shows that the architecture of the conditioning pathway determines which steering strategy stays stable.

Problem. Mechanistic interpretability of music generation has been done almost entirely on text-to-audio models such as MusicGen; symbolic (text-to-MIDI) generators have been left out, even though the relations between notes are directly measurable in a score, which makes an intervention's effect exactly quantifiable. It is unknown what musical information text-to-MIDI models encode, where in depth their prediction is formed, and whether that information supports causal control.

Contributions.

  • A mechanistic-interpretability study of text-conditioned symbolic music generation spanning probing, the logit and tuned lenses, activation patching and activation steering, which the authors state is the first centred on text-to-MIDI models.
  • A comparative account of how the prediction is formed in two contrasting designs: gradual refinement across depth in a native encoder-decoder, against a sharp late rotation out of an inherited textual basis in a language model repurposed by vocabulary extension, corroborated independently by vocabulary-mass analysis, the tuned lens and activation patching.
  • A bidirectional steering-evaluation protocol that runs every configuration in both orientations and decomposes the response into antisymmetric (directional) and symmetric (drift) components, yielding a specificity score that catches nonspecific layer-0 responses which a generation-volume guard alone would accept.
  • An architecture-dependent intervention recipe: single-layer steering is robust in both models, while all-layer steering is stable and most specific only in the cross-attention-conditioned model and accumulates disruptively in the shared prompt-music residual stream of the decoder-only one.

Method. Two contrasting public models are instrumented: text2midi, an encoder-decoder with a frozen Flan-T5 text encoder, cross-attention conditioning, REMI+ metrical tokenization, 18 analysed decoder layers, d=768; and MIDI-LLM, a Llama 3.2 1B decoder-only model with a MIDI-extended vocabulary, AMT absolute-time tokenization, 16 layers, d=2048. Activations are recorded in predictive alignment (the state from which token t is predicted). Four probes of the same activations follow. (1) Linear probing: one L2-regularised multinomial logistic regression per layer (C=1.0, L-BFGS, 200 iterations), deliberately low-capacity so accuracy indicates linear representation rather than probe power, with 5-fold grouped cross-validation keeping all tokens of one sequence in one fold, and a label-shuffling selectivity control. (2) Logit lens: intermediate states passed through the model's own final normalization and vocabulary projection (untied), plus a tuned lens whose per-layer affine translator is initialised to identity and distilled against the model's final distribution by KL on 60,000 held-out token positions. (3) Activation patching: prompt activations at the input to layer L are replaced with those from a contrasting instrument prompt (piano vs violin) and the shift in piano-note fraction is scored 0 (no transfer) to 1 (full), guarded by a self-patch control that is 0 by construction and a neutral-prompt control for nonspecific disruption. (4) Steering: normalised difference-in-means directions from 25 contrastive prompts per pole are added to the residual stream, either at the single source layer or at every layer. The key methodological addition is a bidirectional protocol: every configuration is run in both orientations (A-B and B-A) and the two fitted slopes are split into an antisymmetric component s_dir and a symmetric component s_non, with specificity defined as |s_dir|/(|s_dir|+|s_non|), so directional control is separated from nonspecific drift.

Results. Probing on 1,000 generated sequences: local concepts are strongly linearly decodable in both models -- instrument family, pitch class, chord root, octave and texture density reach lifts of 0.36-0.63 in MIDI-LLM and 0.22-0.42 in text2midi (best layer: instrument family 0.940 accuracy vs 0.310 baseline in MIDI-LLM). Higher-level concepts are weak in both (chord quality lift 0.098 in each, harmonic function 0.065/0.079). Key is weak per token (lift 0.055/0.114) and much stronger mean-pooled (0.401/0.545), but the authors discount this: a pitch-class histogram control alone reaches 0.486 for text2midi -- exceeding the text2midi activation readout -- and 0.475 for MIDI-LLM, so they treat MIDI-LLM's extra 0.070 lift as suggestive rather than conclusive. On the controlled SynTheory set (N=1,080, MIDI form, mean pooling) MIDI-LLM reaches 1.000 on chord progression and 0.998 on interval; text2midi scores -0.131 lift on scale mode, which against pitch-histogram and lowest-note-relative oracles is consistent with an orderless absolute-pitch encoding. Logit lens: text2midi's agreement with the sampled token rises gradually (0.053 at layer 0 to 0.677 at layer 17), whereas MIDI-LLM stays at 0.000 through layer 8 and jumps 0.050 to 0.712 across layers 12-14. That jump is substantially a basis change, not the decision forming: the MIDI share of full-vocabulary probability mass sits near its size-based chance share of 0.30 through layer 12 and rotates to >0.999 by layer 15 in the type order special to note to time/duration, and the tuned lens makes the final prediction readable two to three layers earlier than the classic lens. Patching corroborates the same band causally: instrument transfer stays near full through layer 13 then falls to 0.62 at layer 14 and 0.27 at layer 15; the text2midi mirror experiment shows no isolated depth peak, consistent with distributed cross-attention conditioning. Steering: register and polyphony clear the exploratory |s_dir| > 2 SE rule in both models, tempo/energy only in MIDI-LLM (text2midi tempo/energy fails it at both +13.6% and -22.5%). Polyphony moves 20-40% (text2midi +39.6%/-25.7%; MIDI-LLM +21.6%/-29.8%), register only one to five semitones (+2.9%/-8.0% and +5.2%/-4.6%). The headline stability result is architecture-conditional: in MIDI-LLM single-layer injection has over fifty times less median symmetric drift than all-layer injection (0.28 vs 15.1) and all-layer injection collapses at alpha=0.75, while text2midi supports all-layer injection to alpha>=2 with the most specific directions found anywhere in the sweep (register, specificity 0.98). Norm-relative steering, which equalises the relative perturbation per layer, rules out overscaling of early layers as the cause: single-layer injection survives the full 20% range in MIDI-LLM while all-layer injection fails at 2% for tempo and 5% for register and polyphony, so the limit comes from pushing all sixteen layers together. All results are from the two released checkpoints; no hardware or human evaluation is involved, and unquantifiable attributes are offered only as audio on a demo site.

Limitations. Stated: with one model per architecture the conditioning pathway cannot be isolated from every other design difference between text2midi and MIDI-LLM, so the steering rule is a hypothesis rather than an established architectural law. Difference-in-means directions come from text contrasts that vary lexically as well as musically, so a direction is only as clean as its contrast. The metrics are proxies -- note density stands in for tempo and energy but also rises when more notes sound together, so the tempo/energy and polyphony numbers are not fully independent. Probing labels on generations are heuristic estimates decoded from the score (key via Krumhansl-Schmuckler), so weak higher-level results may be label noise rather than absent representation. Musical concepts without an unambiguous multi-track MIDI measure are outside the quantitative protocol entirely and appear only as audio. Not stated but worth noticing: the logit-lens and patching comparison across models is confounded by unequal depth axes and by entropies taken over vocabularies of very different size (524 REMI+ tokens against 55,026 musical tokens), which the authors flag as a caution but do not correct for; steering effect sizes rest on ten seeds per point; and the strongest sequence-level probing claim is largely absorbed by a bag-of-pitch-classes control.

Relevance. A clean out-of-domain test of whether the archive's standard interpretability toolbox supports the claims usually made with it, in a setting where the output is symbolic and so an intervention's effect is measured exactly rather than judged. Three results transfer directly to how the archive should read intervention claims about reasoning. First, the paper separates a change of representational basis from the formation of a decision: MIDI-LLM's logit-lens accuracy jumps from 0.050 to 0.712 across layers 12-14, but vocabulary-mass rotation and a tuned lens show most of that jump is the readout becoming legible, not the prediction being made -- a direct caution against reading a late logit-lens jump as the moment a model decides. Second, it insists on a control that most steering work omits: a pitch-class histogram reproduces most of the sequence-level probing signal and actually exceeds the text2midi readout, so the authors decline their own strongest probing claim. That is the shape of argument the archive wants for claims that a reasoning-relevant property is 'represented'. Third, and most transferable, the bidirectional protocol gives a cheap, general test that a steering vector carries a direction rather than a disruption -- reverse it and the effect should reverse -- and quantifies the difference as an antisymmetric share; it catches layer-0 responses that shift the same way under both orientations and would pass a fluency or volume check. The stability finding is a concrete methodological warning for interpretability at large: all-layer injection was over fifty times noisier than single-layer injection in the decoder-only model and the norm-relative control shows this is the layers accumulating together rather than early layers being overdriven, so where a steering vector is injected is part of the claim being made, not an implementation detail.

Truth as a Trajectory: What Internal Representations Reveal About Large Language Model Reasoning

Hamed Damirchi, Imezadelajara, Ehsan Abbasnejad, Afshar Shamsi et al. · ACL · 2026-01-01 · dblp

<https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.2073>

Reads reasoning validity from layer-to-layer displacement of hidden states rather than from the states themselves, on the grounds that static activations let probes latch onto lexical surface patterns.

Problem. Explainability methods treat hidden states as static points in activation space and assume correct and incorrect inferences separate at a single layer. But activations are saturated with polysemantic features, so linear probes learn surface-level lexical patterns instead of reasoning structure — the probe succeeds for the wrong reason.

Contributions.

  • The argument that static-activation probing learns lexical surface patterns because activations are polysemantic
  • Truth as a Trajectory: modelling inference as an unfolded trajectory and analyzing layer-wise geometric displacement
  • A probe that uses only cross-layer changes, never the activations themselves
  • Evaluation across dense and MoE architectures on commonsense reasoning, QA and toxicity detection
  • Evidence that trajectory analysis outperforms conventional probing while reducing lexical confounds

Method. Truth as a Trajectory models transformer inference as an unfolded trajectory of iterative refinements, moving analysis from static activations to layer-wise geometric displacement. Analyzing only displacement across layers, and never the activations themselves, is what removes the static lexical confound: a lexical feature present at every layer contributes nothing to the difference between layers.

Results. Evaluated across dense and Mixture-of-Experts architectures on benchmarks spanning commonsense reasoning, question answering and toxicity detection. Using only changes in activations across layers and no access to the activations themselves, TaT mitigates reliance on static lexical confounds and outperforms conventional probing.

Limitations. No numbers in the abstract and the models are not named. Displacement discards the originating state, which is exactly the trade-off arxiv:2608.05660 identifies and tries to repair by adding restricted location information — so this paper takes the position that paper argues is incomplete. Whether the residual signal is reasoning structure or a different confound is not established beyond the lexical case.

Relevance. Lands directly on a dispute this drain has now populated from both sides. This paper argues displacement is the right representation because it strips lexical confounds; arxiv:2608.05660, collected in the same drain, argues displacement alone is insufficient because it discards the originating state, and adds a quantized region plus a normalized direction to restore location without shortcuts. Both report gains over the same single-layer probing baseline, on different benchmarks, with no shared model — which is precisely the incomparability that doi:10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.159 in this drain calls the field's auditing failure. The resolvable question is whether the location information the one paper adds is the lexical confound the other removes.

Beyond a Bag of Features: Set-Level Instability in Sparse Autoencoders

Nikolai Bolik, Lennart Stöpler, Artur Andrzejak · 2026-08-11 · arxiv

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11197>

Takes the set of active sparse-autoencoder latents as the unit of analysis and finds that adding a semantically compatible adjective to a noun deactivates 20 to 60 percent of the latents the noun alone had active, which contradicts the bag-of-features reading those sets are usually given.

Problem. Sparse autoencoders are adopted because individual latents admit meaningful semantic interpretations, and sets of active latents are increasingly treated as objects of analysis in their own right. But almost all SAE work validates individual latents, not sets. If SAE latents faithfully decompose an activation into its underlying active concepts, then two things should follow that nobody had checked: the induced similarity should recover human conceptual structure at least as well as dense representations do, and composing an object with a compatible property should approximately expand the active set rather than replace it.

Contributions.

  • Preliminary evidence that active latent sets are a meaningful unit at all -- union consistency in a controlled toy model, and semantically coherent neighbourhoods in natural text
  • A replication of a human-concepts study with set overlap in place of cosine similarity, finding SAE-induced similarity aligns with human structure no better and in fact slightly worse than dense states
  • A controlled compositional test showing large-scale latent loss under a refinement that should only add, with the loss traced to removal rather than to negative suppression
  • Elimination of the two obvious explanations, geometric interference and feature absorption

Method. A latent is active when its post-nonlinearity value is positive, and the signature of an input is the union of the active sets over its token positions. Two preliminary checks establish that signatures carry structure: in a toy model where synthetic residual states are generated from known ground-truth directions, sufficiently wide SAEs satisfy union consistency -- the signature of a composed input matches the union of the individual features' signatures at high Jaccard, recall and precision -- and on 30,000 snippets from a large corpus, neighbourhoods built from signature overlap are semantically interpretable. Experiment 1 replicates a human-concepts study built on classic typicality psychology, where categories are paired with instances and human typicality scores. Words are represented by averaging over their target-token positions under a neutral template so the surrounding prompt is controlled, similarity between two words is Jaccard overlap of their signatures for the SAE and cosine for the dense baseline, and two things are measured: whether clustering the induced similarity recovers human category boundaries by adjusted mutual information, and whether the within-category ranking of instances matches human typicality by Spearman correlation. Experiment 2 builds chains of increasingly specific prompts by prefixing adjectives to a noun, holding the template fixed and comparing signatures at the noun token positions only, so the comparison is always on the same word. The union-of-properties hypothesis predicts that a compatible adjective preserves the noun's latents and adds its own. The lost-latent rate is the fraction of the base prompt's active latents no longer active in the specific one, and three follow-ups decompose it: whether lost latents were active earlier in the computation when probing the full sequence with the same layer's SAE as a cross-layer detector, whether their pre-activations sit in a near-zero band or a strongly negative one, and what an automatic interpretability pipeline says about them.

Results. SAE latent sets do not recover human conceptual structure better than the dense states they are meant to decompose. Category-boundary recovery by adjusted mutual information is no better for signature overlap than for residual-stream cosine, and the best SAE-based score stays below the best embedding-based one across the settings investigated. Within-category typicality is worse than that: per-category Spearman correlations between human typicality rankings and Jaccard rankings fluctuate around zero for most categories, so the overlap does not systematically recover human typicality at all. What signature overlap does track is model-internal similarity -- its correlations with residual-stream cosine are high though not one, so it captures a related but partially distinct structure rather than reproducing the dense geometry. The compositional result is the sharper one. Lost-latent rates run typically between 20 and 60 percent, rising with the number of adjectives and often with model depth, so prefixing a compatible adjective removes a large fraction of the latents that the bare noun had active. The decomposition rules out the easy readings. Around 60 percent of lost latents in middle layers were active upstream when probing the full sequence, and the noun-restricted version agrees closely, meaning those latents were already active on the noun itself and disappear specifically at the position where loss is measured -- so a substantial share remain in use by the model. At that position roughly 80 percent of lost latents on the noun signature sit in the near-zero pre-activation band with only a few strongly negative, so they are removed rather than actively suppressed. Feature absorption does not explain it: Matryoshka SAEs, designed to mitigate absorption, show lost-latent rates qualitatively consistent with other SAE families. Geometric interference is judged insufficient for the magnitude and structure observed. Lost latents also score slightly lower on automatic interpretability, which the paper reads as weak evidence that they participate in more distributed or context-dependent computation.

Limitations. The paper is explicit that its two preliminary checks establish only that signatures can be meaningful in controlled settings and in qualitative neighbourhoods, not that they are compositional in naturalistic ones, and that whether the distributed coding it observes reflects genuine model structure or SAE artifacts remains unsettled. What a reader should add: the main text rests on one small model with one SAE width, with other models and SAE variants in appendices, so 'across models' is an appendix claim. The compositional dataset is 200 hand-curated adjective-noun sequences with at most five adjectives, under one fixed template, so the effect is established on a narrow and deliberately clean construction -- which is the right design for a negative result but means the rate itself should not be transported. The upstream check uses the layer's own SAE as a cross-layer detector, which assumes that detector transfers across layers; the paper cites prior use of SAEs as transferable probes rather than validating it here. The near-zero threshold is a pragmatic constant for non-JumpReLU SAEs. And the human-concepts comparison inherits the earlier study's dataset, prompt template and evaluation procedure wholesale, so both the replication and its extension stand or fall with that apparatus.

Relevance. The archive's interpretability cluster leans on sparse autoencoders, including a step-level SAE for reading reasoning traces, and this constrains what a set of active latents can be taken to mean. Two results matter here. First, the interpretability of individual latents does not license reading their union as a list of the concepts present -- a compatible refinement removes 20 to 60 percent of them, and they are removed rather than suppressed. Second, and less remarked, SAE-induced similarity aligns with human conceptual structure no better than the dense residual stream does; the extra machinery buys per-latent nameability, not a closer match to human categories. Together with the weight-based mounts entry read alongside it, which declines to claim any replacement for SAEs while noting that its own units carry no names, the archive now holds both halves of the trade: dictionaries give names without faithful composition, and weight rules give faithful mechanisms without names.

Intrinsic Structure: Spectral Identifiability for Mechanistic Interpretability

Ashim Dhor, Pin-Yu Chen · 2026-08-10 · arxiv

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10172>

Treats a transformer forward pass as a controlled dynamical system with depth as time, lifts it with the Koopman operator to get a finite linear realisation whose spectrum is coordinate-free, proves that spectrum is recoverable from finite calibration data at the parametric rate, and then proves that the identifiable object and the human-legible object cannot be the same object.

Problem. Mechanistic interpretability explains models by finding circuits in them, but has no way to say whether a circuit is a property of the model or an artifact of the procedure that found it. The evidence that this is not merely philosophical comes from sparse autoencoders: different training seeds and dictionary widths recover materially different features from the same activations, dictionaries absorb and split features, and residual-stream components remain uncaptured by any known variant. What is missing across every paradigm -- sparse dictionaries, transcoders, causal-intervention localisation, distributed alignment search -- is a theorem asserting that the discovered structure is an invariant of the model rather than of the method.

Contributions.

  • A formalisation under which identifiability is a well-posed question for mechanistic interpretability: the forward pass as a depth-indexed controlled dynamical system, lifted by the Koopman operator to a finite linear realisation whose spectrum is invariant under any change of dictionary basis.
  • An identifiability theorem -- stated as the first for a mechanistic-interpretability primitive -- recovering that spectrum from M calibration samples at rate M^(-1/2) up to permutation, with a matching minimax lower bound and a median-of-means variant for heavy-tailed activations.
  • A dissociation theorem: when the realisation is non-normal, the directions carrying activation variance and those carrying information across depth cannot coincide, since alignment would require perfect eigenvector conditioning, and measured conditioning of 10 to 100 makes the divergence unavoidable.
  • The consequence that sparse-autoencoder non-identifiability is structural rather than a training artifact, with an explicit invariance penalty as remedy, shown to reduce split-half spectral distance by 41 percent at matched sparsity while degrading reconstruction, live-feature fraction and cross-seed agreement.
  • An empirical section that reports its two pre-registered failures alongside its confirmations, including a criterion met in 49 percent of cells against a registered 80 percent and a universality criterion that separates seed replicas of one architecture.

Method. The reframing is the contribution. Write the residual stream at a fixed token position as a state, the attention writes contributed by other positions as exogenous control inputs, and the MLP as autonomous nonlinear dynamics; the forward pass is then a discrete-time controlled dynamical system with depth playing the role of time. The Koopman operator, which evolves observables by composition with the dynamics, is linear in the observables even though the dynamics are nonlinear in the state, so if a dictionary of observables spans an invariant subspace the operator restricts to a finite matrix. Its eigenpairs are the Koopman modes and its eigenvalues are properties of the operator: unique up to permutation, invariant under any change of dictionary basis, and -- the main theorem -- recoverable from M calibration samples at rate M^(-1/2), with a matching minimax lower bound and a median-of-means variant for heavy-tailed activations. A dissociation theorem then shows that whenever the realisation is non-normal, the directions carrying activation variance and the directions carrying information across depth cannot coincide, because alignment would require perfect eigenvector conditioning. Four practical corollaries follow: sparse-autoencoder non-identifiability is structural rather than algorithmic with an explicit invariance penalty as remedy, cross-model universality becomes a testable spectral criterion, the intervention calculus is algebraically complete on the spectral-projector algebra to first order, and balanced truncation admits an instance-dependent error certificate sharper than the classical bound in the low-effective-rank regime attention occupies. Empirically the theory is tested on GPT-2 small, Gemma-2-2B and Qwen3-8B-Base at 10^5 to 10^6 calibration samples, with split-half spectral distance as the convergence statistic, sequence-level block bootstraps for error bars, and pre-registered criteria for two of the corollaries.

Results. The spectrum converges on all three models but attains the predicted -1/2 exponent on only the largest: -0.506 +/- 0.031 for Qwen3-8B-Base against -0.329 for Gemma-2-2B and -0.286 for GPT-2 small. The shortfall is explained rather than excused -- plotting each of 30 model-dictionary-width cells against its own sample threshold rather than against raw M collapses most of the scatter onto one curve, with binned medians falling from -0.31 to -0.53 and crossing -1/2 past the threshold (Spearman rho = -0.28, p < 1e-4), though the two Qwen series are named as the residual the collapse does not account for. The behavioural test is where the theory's own dissociation bites, and the authors state the negative result plainly. Ablating the top-j Koopman modes on indirect-object identification moves the logit difference more than j random directions but less than j principal directions -- at j=8, PCA removes 60 percent against the modes' 25 -- with the gap decaying 4.1-fold in depth-distance, monotonically at every layer, and essentially gone by a depth-distance of about 8. Their reading is explicitly narrow: the advantage decays to parity and does not reverse, so PCA stops being the better transport basis without the modes being shown to be one, and two causal tests of a reversal do not discriminate on this testbed because twelve layers is not enough depth to separate depth-distance from layer under a behavioural readout. No head class reaches the alignment target and greedy selection exhausts its budget at 96 of 128 modes at roughly twice the random-subspace null. The sparse-autoencoder corollary is confirmed in ordering and qualified in scope: the relative invariance residual puts SAEs furthest from Koopman invariance on every model, further even than a random orthonormal control, at 2.1x, 3.1x and 3.4x the spectral residual with the gap strengthening with scale across three SAE families -- but the ordering is conditional on the feature-selection rule, and reverses under variance-based selection (0.70x rather than 2.05x). The authors therefore restate the claim as being about the subset a practitioner is most likely to read, the features that fire often, rather than about the SAE's span. The causal test of the remedy works: raising the invariance penalty from 0 to 1 cuts the invariance residual 24 percent (0.438 to 0.333) and the split-half spectral distance -- identifiability itself rather than a proxy -- by 41 percent (0.0170 to 0.0101) at matched sparsity, while reconstruction degrades (fraction of variance unexplained 0.222 to 0.255), the live-feature fraction falls from 0.77 to 0.58, cross-seed feature agreement moves the wrong way, and at a penalty of 10 the dictionary collapses outright across all three seeds. Two pre-registered results fail their controls and are reported as failures: the SAE-gap criterion is met in 49 percent of 75 cells against a registered 80 percent, because the sign of the effect tracks the feature-selection rule; and the universality criterion separates two seed replicas of one architecture, so in its stated form it is not a universality criterion.

Limitations. Stated at length and ordered by how much they constrain the claims. The estimand depends on modelling conventions the authors cannot fully justify -- residualising the attention write against the lifted state moves the spectrum by 6.8 to 9.1 times the resolution floor, so the naive and residualised conventions specify different estimands at the achievable precision, and both are reported. Invariance holds only approximately: every theorem assumes the dictionary spans an invariant subspace, real dictionaries satisfy this approximately, and the resulting bias does not vanish as M grows, so the method is not model-agnostic and the dictionary must be co-designed with the architecture -- though within a fixed dictionary family it is uniform across corpus, seed and hyperparameter, which is precisely the property SAEs lack. Diagonalisability and spectral separation are generic but not universal, and exact coincidence is conjectured measure-zero but not proved. The intervention calculus is proved to first order but never validated against measured patching effects. The rate is asymptotic in a way that bites, since the constant is unknown and the sample threshold is an order-of-magnitude guide. The random-Fourier dictionary bandwidth, fixed rather than retuned per model, degrades at width 4096 in the deepest layers, which the authors identify as a dictionary-construction failure rather than a model or estimator failure. And two stated results fail their pre-registered controls, reported because both bound what may be claimed. Not stated but worth noticing: three models and one behavioural task carry the entire empirical section, and the strongest theoretical claim -- that the identifiable and the legible object cannot coincide -- is supported empirically by one circuit on one testbed the authors themselves say is too shallow to separate the relevant variables.

Relevance. This is the paper the archive's accumulated worry about sparse autoencoders has been pointing at: it asks whether a discovered structure is a property of the model or of the finder, and answers it for one primitive with a theorem rather than an argument. Two results matter regardless of whether Koopman spectral analysis is adopted. First, it makes SAE run-to-run variability structural rather than a training defect -- the reconstruction-and-sparsity objective does not enforce the invariance that identifiability requires -- and then does the thing that turns a diagnosis into evidence: adds the missing term and shows the outcome moves, cutting split-half spectral distance 41 percent at matched sparsity. It also reports the price honestly, with reconstruction degrading, live features falling from 0.77 to 0.58, cross-seed agreement moving the wrong way, and the dictionary collapsing at a higher penalty, leaving open whether a dictionary can be sparse, reconstructive and invariant at once. Second, and more consequential for how the archive reads interpretability claims generally, the dissociation theorem says that in the non-normal regime these models occupy, the directions carrying variance and the directions carrying information across depth cannot coincide -- so the identifiable object and the legible object are different objects, and a safety case needing both needs two tools and must say which it is using where. That is a formal version of a boundary the archive keeps hitting empirically, and it sits alongside detection-versus-control as a second reason a single decomposition cannot serve every purpose. The paper is also a model of reporting that the archive should hold up as a standard. It states plainly that the modes lose to PCA on the one behavioural task (25 percent against 60 at j=8), that the advantage decays to parity without reversing, that its causal tests of a reversal do not discriminate on a twelve-layer testbed, that its headline SAE ordering inverts under a different feature-selection rule, and that two pre-registered criteria fail -- one met in 49 percent of cells against a registered 80, and a universality criterion that separates seed replicas of one architecture and therefore is not one. Cite the theory as theory: three models and one circuit carry the empirical section, and the intervention calculus is proved but never checked against measured patching effects.

Deployable Per-Instance Multi-Layer Activation Steering for Large Language Models

Muhammad Faishal Adly Nelwan, Alfan Farizki Wicaksono · 2026-08-09 · arxiv

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08829>

Shows that which layers a steering vector should be injected at is a property of the individual input rather than of the task, that a greedy per-input rule reaches the exhaustive optimum for structural reasons, and that a label-free predictor trained to imitate that rule recovers most of the oracle at deployment.

Problem. Activation steering fixes its injection layers globally per behaviour and treats the remaining choices -- which layers, and how many -- as configuration. Both are hard for the same reason: the right answer depends on the input. A global layer set leaves gains unrealised on the instances where steering has room to act, and at higher layer counts it over-steers the saturated ones into fluency collapse. Per-instance selection has a catch that makes it look undeployable: every method that can find the best layers scores candidate subsets by steered forward passes judged against the gold answer, which does not exist at inference.

Contributions.

  • A per-instance oracle over layer subsets establishing that the best layers change from input to input, with a permutation control
  • A structural account of why a greedy top-K-by-single-layer-effect rule matches the exhaustive joint optimum: near-collinear mid-band vectors, negligible synergy, and padding
  • A label-free deployable recipe -- a prompt-embedding layer ranker, a direction classifier, and an adaptive gate on how many layers to steer
  • A mechanistic reading, direction over magnitude, that unifies global-rule corruption, high-K collapse, an unflippable ceiling and a cross-model dissociation

Method. Steering follows contrastive activation addition with last-token injection at every generation step across a selected subset of layers. Three gold-aware references bracket what selection can achieve: an exhaustive per-instance oracle enumerable to three layers, a greedy per-instance rule that takes the layers of largest individual effect, and an in-sample global oracle that picks the best fixed set per cell and applies it unchanged to every input. None is deployable, because all score candidates against the gold answer; they serve as ceilings and as the training target. The deployable recipe replaces each with something label-free. Ranking is a single-hidden-layer network over a 25-dimensional PCA of an unsteered prompt embedding, trained listwise to reproduce each training input's distribution of per-layer effects, so one embedding pass yields the ranking. Direction is inferred by a logistic regression on the same features, which orients the push rather than sizing it and costs no extra forward pass. Depth is decided by an adaptive gate that walks the ranked prefix from one layer upward, scores each step's short steered pass against the inferred direction, and halts on a lift plateau, a floor on the yes/no mass, or an abrupt mass drop -- with all six constants set a priori rather than tuned on the evaluation set. Evaluated on Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Aya-Expanse-8B over six binary persona traits, with the primary metric the shift in gold-answer probability under a restricted yes/no softmax and a secondary rationale-perplexity guard against over-steering. Inputs where the base model already assigns near-certainty are separated as saturated, and per-instance claims are read on the remaining steerable stratum.

Results. The best layers are a property of the input: an input's own oracle layers beat those of a different same-sign input in all twelve cells. The greedy rule then matches the exhaustive joint optimum -- statistically indistinguishable on 15 of 24 configurations on the full test set, with the oracle's margin at or below about 2.6 points everywhere and under one point on most cells -- and the paper explains why rather than reporting it as luck. The layers that carry effect sit in a narrow mid-band whose vectors are near-collinear (mean pairwise cosine 0.65 and 0.61, adjacent pairs 0.92), so neighbours substitute for one another; two-layer synergy is a few thousandths of the metric against single-layer effects two orders larger, and correlates negatively with vector cosine; and the exhaustive optimum pads a third of its three-layer picks on one model and 61 percent on the other with a do-nothing bottom layer, so joint search reaches for a third active layer only when one helps. Over-steering turns out to be dose-graded and extremely concentrated: of 942 severe events, 97 percent fall in a single trait-model cell, growing from 30 at three layers to 624 at five with a median perplexity inflation of 810 points. Attributing those events by selector, the global oracle carries 227 and the fixed-depth greedy rule 76, while each adaptively gated variant carries exactly one -- so the pathology belongs to a fixed aggressive dose, not to any particular picker. The deployable recipe recovers 93 percent of the exhaustive steerable lift on Llama-3-8B-Instruct and 65 percent on Aya-Expanse-8B, exceeds the gold-aware global oracle on five of six tasks on the first model despite having no labels, and never drives a trait-model cell below its unsteered baseline -- where the global rules do, on four cells and eight cells respectively. Counting behavioural flips on the generated answer, the per-instance rules corrupt no answer at any depth while the global oracle climbs from 12 corruptions at one layer to 249 at five, going net negative there. And the ceiling is stated plainly: of 206 strongly opposed inputs, 155 are flipped by no selector at all, the exhaustive oracle included.

Limitations. The paper marks its own boundaries carefully, which is unusual and worth noting: the cross-model dissociation is called provisional and the magnitude gap explicitly not a precise figure, and the unflippable ceiling is presented as a limit of the static-coefficient steering paradigm rather than of the selector. What a reader should add is scope. The behaviour space is six persona traits posed as binary yes/no multiple choice, which is a narrow probe -- the primary metric is a two-way probability shift, and nothing here tests steering that must survive open-ended generation beyond a 200-token rationale used as a fluency guard. Two 8B models, and the two disagree enough that the headline recovery figure is 93 percent on one and 65 on the other, with the gap traced to 80 percent of the second model's inputs being saturated so that the prize per instance is smaller for any method. The gate's six constants are set a priori and a large configuration sweep places the deployed setting within about one point of the best, which is good practice, but the sweep is on the same corpus. Finally, the greedy rule's structural match to the joint optimum rests on the mid-band collinearity measured on these two models, and the paper is clear that this is what makes the oracle learnable -- so on an architecture whose effective layers were not near-substitutes, the whole argument would need re-establishing.

Relevance. Sharpens the archive's standing finding that a direction's quality as a detector says nothing about its effect as an intervention, by adding the axis nobody here had varied: where the direction is applied. The same vector at different layers produces effects that differ enough that an input's own layers beat another input's oracle layers in every cell, and the paper's summary -- direction over magnitude, meaning which layers carry the vector and hence which way the push points dominates how hard it is pushed -- means a steering result reported at a fixed layer is a measurement of that layer choice as much as of the direction. It also supplies the archive's first controlled account of over-steering: fluency collapse is content crowding rather than norm inflation, the final-layer residual norm stays flat while the output degenerates to a content-free attractor, and 97 percent of severe events fall in one trait-model cell. Read with the constrained-ablation entry from the same day, the two agree that safe steering is a selection problem -- one selects the direction, the other selects the site -- and neither is solved by the canonical direction.

Measuring Concept Content in Text from LLM Activations: ESG Evidence from Concept Vectors and Linear Probes

Luc Hazenoot, Zhaochun Ren, Amirhossein Zohrehvand · 2026-08-07 · arxiv

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07208>

Compares linear probes against RFM-derived concept vectors for reading how much a sentence concerns a concept out of a frozen LLM's activations, on a human-annotated ESG benchmark, and finds the simpler probe consistently stronger.

Problem. Measures of how much a text is about a concept read the text's surface -- dictionary word shares, topic proportions, embedding similarity -- so they score the words a text uses rather than the judgment a reader forms about it, and they fail wherever a concept resists enumeration. LLMs are known to encode more internally than they express in their responses, and the linear representation hypothesis says concepts are accessible as directions in activation space, but applied work still builds measures on model outputs or embeddings. What was missing was a controlled head-to-head on an applied benchmark: which extractor reads the activations best, and how close a frozen model gets to a classifier fine-tuned for the task.

Contributions.

  • A linear probe on the frozen activations of an out-of-the-box LLM measures ESG concept content within 0.6, 1.0 and 2.1 accuracy points of classifiers fine-tuned for the task, with no gradient updates to the language model and about five minutes of GPU time per pillar.
  • Two comparisons that rule out cheaper explanations for that margin: the probe outscores the same model's own yes-or-no answer in eleven of twelve model-pillar combinations by 0.043 AUC, and outscores the RFM concept vectors in eleven of twelve when both see identical training and test splits.
  • A negative result against the authors' own stated expectation: the RFM concept-vector method of Beaglehole et al. does not transfer as the strongest activation-based extractor in this domain, and on the Social pillar falls below a plain embedding baseline.
  • Evidence that the prompt wrapper, not the extractor, is the least controlled part of the pipeline, moving accuracy across a 9.4-point range with everything else held fixed.

Method. Frozen decoder-only models are run over each labelled sentence wrapped in a fixed statement that names the target concept, and the per-layer activations are pooled to one vector per layer by last-token, mean or max pooling. Two supervised extractors are compared on those activations. Linear probing uses a stacked design: a RidgeClassifier with strong L2 regularisation per layer (31 layers for Llama-3.1-8B, 35 for Qwen-3-8B, 39 for Qwen-3-14B, the earliest layer excluded), then a logistic-regression meta-learner over the per-layer decision scores, trained under nested cross-validation -- an outer 5-fold stratified loop for scoring, an inner 5-fold loop producing out-of-fold scores as meta-features, with standardisation fitted inside each fold. The Recursive Feature Machine (RFM) extractor instead fits a non-linear predictor, computes the Average Gradient Outer Product (AGOP) matrix, and takes its top 5 eigenvectors by eigendecomposition as concept vectors; each is sign-oriented against the training labels, a new sentence is scored by per-layer cosine similarity averaged over layers and then over the five vectors, min-max scaled to [-1,1], and thresholded. Baselines are an embedding model (Qwen-3-embedding-8b features into logistic regression), surface measures (a concept-word dictionary, a 25-topic model, TF-IDF with logistic regression), the fine-tuned classifiers published with the dataset, and -- the comparison the premise rests on -- the same model's own yes/no answer to the same question, scored by the probability it assigns 'Yes' against 'No' so it yields a directly comparable AUC.

Results. Linear probing on frozen activations comes within 0.6, 1.0 and 2.1 accuracy points of the best fine-tuned classifier on the Environmental, Social and Governance pillars: 0.951 (Qwen-3-8B, last token) against EnvRoBERTa's 0.957; 0.924 (Qwen-3-14B, max pooling) against SocRoBERTa's 0.934; 0.876 (Qwen-3-8B, max pooling) against GovDistilRoBERTa's 0.897. The fine-tuned models still lead on every pillar. Against the model's own answer to the same question, the probe wins eleven of twelve model-pillar combinations by 0.043 AUC on average, the exception being a tie (0.975 vs 0.978, Llama-3.1-8B on Environmental) -- this is the paper's direct evidence that activations carry concept content the response does not report. RFM concept vectors clear that same bar in only seven of twelve. The head-to-head between extractors goes to the probe: best RFM is 0.946 / 0.886 / 0.866 across the pillars, and when the probe is retrained on exactly RFM's split (same 1,000 train, 500 test sentences) it still leads in eleven of twelve combinations by 2.9 accuracy points on average, so the ordering is the extraction method rather than the evaluation protocol. This reverses the authors' stated expectation that RFM would transfer as the strongest activation method. Within RFM, the first concept vector beats the mean of the top five on accuracy in eleven of twelve combinations, which the authors call counterintuitive and explain only tentatively; larger models do better, with Gemma-4-31b matching or beating every other model on accuracy on every pillar. Surface baselines sit far below: a concept-word dictionary reaches 0.128 F1 on Governance against the best probe's 0.757, and a 25-topic model 0.373. TF-IDF with logistic regression is a much harder baseline at 0.894 AUC on Governance -- above every RFM configuration -- though still 2.0 accuracy points behind the best probe there and 6.9 and 6.0 behind on Environmental and Social. The authors draw the boundary themselves: the surface-versus-judgment distinction holds against measures that count concept words, not against a classifier free to learn whatever lexical cues the labels carry. Cost on a single H100: under four minutes to extract activations and fit concept vectors per model-pillar, about five for the full nested-cross-validation probe. All results are software-only on a released annotated corpus; no human evaluation was rerun.

Limitations. Stated: the wrapper text is the least controlled step and moves accuracy across a 9.4-point range with model, dataset and method held fixed, and the best wrapper differs by model; only six wrappers were searched, so how much better either method could do is unknown, and this weighs more on RFM than on the probe because a concept vector must carry the concept on its own and a weak wrapper costs it twice. The continuous RFM score -- the concept vectors' main claim to a job the probe cannot do -- is reported as a distribution rather than validated, because current ESG datasets including this one carry only binary labels, so whether the score tracks degree remains untested. ESG is a niche financial subdomain and smaller models likely saw little of it, which the authors read in their own results from Environmental scoring above Governance throughout. The Gemma-4-31b probe is missing from every table because the kernel ran out of memory; the authors report that on adequate hardware it lands at or just below the best Qwen configuration, so the gap is coverage rather than a conclusion. Not stated but worth noticing: the RFM accuracy and F1 in the main tables come from a threshold swept on the split being reported rather than frozen on validation, making them the best attainable on that split -- the authors disclose this and quantify the cost (0.011 accuracy and 0.025 F1 on average, worst case 0.038 and 0.136), but the headline RFM numbers are still optimistic in a way the probe's cross-validated numbers are not. The stacked probe also buys less than its complexity implies: on Environmental the best single layer is within one AUC point of the full stack, and that layer sits mid-network rather than at the end.

Relevance. A rare like-for-like comparison of two extraction methods on the same activations, which is the kind of evidence the archive needs in order to say what a probing result actually supports. Three things it settles. First, it makes the 'the model knows more than it says' claim measurable rather than rhetorical by putting the probe against the strongest fair baseline -- asking the same model the same question and scoring its own Yes/No probability -- and the probe wins eleven of twelve by 0.043 AUC. That is the comparison most probing work in the archive omits, and it should be the default demand. Second, it shows that method sophistication is not the axis that matters: RFM concept vectors, which come out of a Science paper on universal steering and monitoring, lose consistently to plain ridge probes even on identical splits, and on one pillar fall below a plain embedding baseline. Third, and most useful as a caution, it draws the line where the internal-representation story stops working: dictionary counts and topic models are far behind (0.128 and 0.373 F1 against 0.757), but a tuned TF-IDF classifier reaches 0.894 AUC and beats every RFM configuration, so 'reads judgment rather than surface' holds only against measures that count concept words, not against any classifier free to exploit lexical cues. The paper states this against its own interest. Two further findings bear on how the archive should read probe evidence generally: the prompt wrapper alone moves accuracy across 9.4 points, meaning a probing number is a property of a pipeline rather than of a model, and the stacked-over-layers architecture is within one AUC point of a single well-chosen mid-network layer on the easy pillar -- consistent with the archive's other evidence that the reportable content sits in intermediate layers. The domain is financial text rather than reasoning traces, so nothing here bears directly on chains of thought; what transfers is the evidentiary standard.

Automatic speaking assessment systems mark second-language speaking tests in high-stakes settings, so their scores must depend on proficiency rather than on irrelevant speaker attributes such as first language or age.   • An explicit separation of concept recoverability from concept influence, the latter via a gradient-based sensitivity metric

Cultural Awareness is Represented but Not Decoded: Tracing Mythological Knowledge across 18 Open-Source LLMs

Iaroslav Chelombitko, Ekaterina Chelombitko, Mika Hämäläinen · 2026-08-03 · arxiv

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02486>

Builds a parallel entity grid of 27 folk-narrative motifs across 10 cultures and instruments 18 models with probing, logit lens, activation patching and generation, finding that the residual stream separates cultures cleanly in every model while the readout collapses onto Greco-Roman defaults — so the failure is at the decoder, not the encoder.

Problem. Cultural bias in language models is documented behaviourally — a model asked for the supreme sky god returns Zeus, Jupiter and Thor reliably and the Finnish, Slavic or Mesopotamian counterpart far less often — but behaviour cannot say where inside the model the bias is produced. Two mechanisms predict opposite fixes: if the residual stream never separates the cultures, the model lacks the information and needs more data; if it separates them but the readout collapses them, the information is present and the fix belongs at the output. Nothing in the surveyed cultural-bias tasks could tell the two apart, because they lack a parallel structure that asks the same question of many traditions with an unambiguous gold answer.

Contributions.

  • A substrate with the property the diagnosis needs: 27 Thompson Motif-Index roles instantiated across 10 cultures spanning six language families, giving 270 cells each with one canonical entity, so the same question has a gold answer in every tradition
  • A four-instrument design in which each measurement explicitly does not answer the others' question, and the argument is their conjunction — probe for encoding, logit lens for depth, activation patching for causal location, generation for what a user receives
  • A per-cell 2x2 decomposition pairing what the probe reads against what the model emits, yielding Preserved, DecodingSuppressed, SurfaceLuck and RepresentationallyFlat cells
  • An output-format control that poses the same question as a 10-way multiple choice over the parallel fillers, separating a classification-versus-generation artefact from a genuine generation-time loss
  • A within-mode versus cross-mode correlation test that isolates language conditioning from paraphrase sensitivity, and is robust to the language-proficiency confound by construction

Method. For each of the 270 (motif, culture) cells two prompts are built: a contextual one asserting that a named entity embodies a described role, used for hidden-state extraction and never containing the culture word so the probe cannot copy it by attention; and a chat prompt asking for the name given culture and role. Linear probing average-pools residual activations over the entity span at every layer and fits a 5-fold ridge classifier for the 10-way culture label. The logit lens applies the final norm and unembedding to every intermediate state and records the depth at which the gold entity's first sub-token enters the top-k, reported as top-k rather than top-1 because subword segmentation is unstable for morphologically rich languages. Activation patching swaps the residual stream of a source-culture prompt into a target-culture prompt at each layer and measures the change in gold-target log-probability. Output extraction greedily generates up to 256 tokens under the chat template, with thinking traces disabled for reasoning-style models, scored by exact match, substring match or length-normalized Levenshtein at threshold 0.8. Both an English and a target-culture native-language prompt mode are run with five paraphrases each. Eighteen decoder-only models from eight families, 1.2B to 34B, are evaluated at fp16 with no quantization.

Results. The headline is uniform across the sweep: DecodingSuppressed — the probe reads the culture and the generation emits a wrong name — is the largest cell in every one of the 18 models, at 51 to 76 percent (mean 0.65), while RepresentationallyFlat is 0.10 to 0.33 (mean 0.18). Seventeen of eighteen probes clear a 0.60 character-n-gram surface baseline, peaking at 0.61 to 0.88, with significance confirmed by paired bootstrap and exact McNemar; the same models generating land at 0.09 to 0.43. The smallest probe-output gap in the sweep is 0.26 and the largest 0.70. The format control does real work and does not explain the gap away: matching the output task to the probe by multiple choice over the same motif's ten fillers recovers mean accuracy from 0.26 to 0.67, but the chain representation 0.79 to selection 0.67 to generation 0.26 still loses about 41 points at the generation step, and the collapse onto the dominant tradition is visible inside pure selection — on Roman cells 49 percent of errors land on the same-motif Greek counterpart against roughly 11 percent under a uniform error model. Depth is consistent: the lens onset falls in the last 12 percent of layers in every model (median 0.96) while probe-peak depth spreads widely across families, so every model sits on or above the encode-before-decode diagonal. Patching makes the localization causal rather than correlational — the preference-flip rate sits at baseline through the early third, peaks in the last quarter (median peak depth 0.89, median rate 0.75, median lift +0.55), roughly seven times the early-network rate. Scaling shrinks the gap in all five multi-size families and closes it in none: Llama 3.x from 1.2B to 8B moves the probe 0.79 to 0.88 and the output 0.09 to 0.25, leaving 0.63, with a naive log-parameter extrapolation predicting about +0.10 output gain per decade of parameters. Cross-lingual querying gates partially disjoint subsets of the same representation: within-mode correctness correlates at 0.57 against 0.29 cross-mode, so cross-language queries are about twice as decoupled, and per-culture English-versus-native deltas track the language each canon is documented in (Greek +0.241 and Egyptian +0.177 favouring English; Chinese -0.071 and Finnish -0.058 favouring the native query) rather than tokenizer fertility, which does not predict the per-cell delta at all. Simply taking the union of the two query modes lifts cell recovery by 0.08 absolute, a 36 percent relative gain, at zero training cost.

Limitations. The paper's limitations section is unusually thorough and names the right things. The canonical entity-to-culture assignments are genuinely contested at boundaries — Odin and Thor are both Norse sky figures, Perun is shared between Ukrainian and West Slavic traditions, and several cells use a scholarly-contested filler — so modern consensus is followed where it has shifted but not every assignment is unambiguous; four structural absences are catalogued and excluded and others cannot be ruled out. The ten cultures deliberately exclude sub-Saharan African, Native American, Polynesian, Australian and Arctic indigenous traditions; the authors judge the headline claim unlikely to reverse there but flag the per-culture English-versus-native deltas as the part most at risk, precisely for traditions documented predominantly in non-Anglocentric sources. The substrate is 270 cells with all inference at cell level, output scoring on 266 and the multiple-choice control on 263, so the control's accuracies rest on a marginally smaller set than the generation accuracies they are compared against. The logit lens is used in its raw form for cross-model comparability and gives a relative ordering rather than a calibrated probability, since intermediate states are not calibrated for direct decoding. One model's chat-template patching scores at approximately zero through a template artefact and is reported on a bare-prompt estimate instead, with explicit patching left to a follow-up. Robustness is checked rather than assumed: rescoring 48,568 generations under three stricter criteria preserves the per-model ranking at Pearson 0.87 or above.

Relevance. It is a clean instance of the distinction this archive keeps needing and rarely gets stated so sharply: what a representation contains and what the model does with it are separately measurable, and here they disagree in every model tested. The methodological core transfers directly to reasoning work — a linear probe showing a property is decodable establishes encoding and nothing more, which is why the design pairs it per cell with generation and adds patching to make the locus causal rather than correlational. The paper is explicit that each instrument does not answer the others' question and that the argument is their conjunction, which is the standard this archive's probing results should be read against. Two further results carry over. Scaling shrinks the encode-decode gap in every family and closes it in none, extrapolating to about +0.10 per decade of parameters, so a readout failure is not something more capacity fixes. And the practical consequence is a diagnosis of where an intervention can work at all: encoder-side fixes push on the 0.10-to-0.33 slice while the dominant 0.51-to-0.76 slice sits at the unembedding, which is the same shape of argument as the archive's finding that a signal can demonstrably track the right thing and still buy nothing downstream.

UniProbe: A Learnable Token-Level Hallucination Detector for Large VLMs using Multi-Structural Internal Representations

Dvir Samuel, Guy Bar-Shalom, Fabrizio Frasca, Ethan Fetaya et al. · 2026-08-11 · arxiv

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10835>

Reads a frozen vision-language model's forward pass as a structured computational trace -- a graph over image patches, query tokens and generated tokens with attention as edges, processed by interleaved relational, spatial and sequential modules -- and shows that structuring the same internals prior detectors already read lifts hallucination detection from a 69-75 AUC plateau to 90.

Problem. Vision-language models generate content unsupported by the image, and useful mitigation needs the affected tokens located rather than the whole response discarded. The three existing approaches each pay a price: fine-tuning the model to tag hallucinated spans updates billions of parameters and can degrade the model it is meant to safeguard; training an external verifier that compares response to image requires a second vision-language model and ignores how the response was produced; and probing internal signals avoids both costs but reduces the forward pass to isolated features and hand-crafted statistics, discarding the spatial, sequential and relational structure that the computation actually has.

Contributions.

  • A detector that models the forward pass as a heterogeneous computational trace -- a graph over image patches, query tokens and generated tokens with attention as edges -- rather than as isolated features or hand-crafted statistics.
  • Interleaved rather than stacked structure-aware modules for relational, spatial and sequential evidence, ablated to show no single one suffices and that removing the relational graph costs most.
  • The demonstration that flat probes over the same frozen internals plateau where a structured read-out does not, isolating the representation of the evidence rather than access to it as the operative variable.
  • A streaming variant that detects and resamples during generation at 1.06 times vanilla latency, with a self-adaptation step addressing the distribution shift caused by different backbones hallucinating differently.
  • Controls that isolate detection quality from the intervention: the same guardrail run with every competing detector, and self-adaptation applied to the strongest competitor to show adaptation alone does not explain the gain.
  • Evidence that the mitigation does not work by suppression, reporting retained word count, preserved correct-object coverage, eliminated empty captions, perplexity and distribution shift alongside the hallucination reduction.
  • A depth sweep showing grounding evidence concentrated in a narrow mid-network band on every backbone, with an argument for why pooling across layers dilutes rather than aggregates it.

Method. The detector treats a single forward pass as a heterogeneous trace rather than a bag of features. A directed graph is constructed over image patches, query tokens and generated tokens with attention weights encoding the relations between them, and that trace is processed by three structure-aware modules matched to three kinds of evidence -- a graph network for relational structure, a vision transformer for two-dimensional visual geometry, and a recurrent module for response order -- interleaved rather than stacked, so spatial, relational and sequential evidence interact throughout rather than being combined at the end. The model under test stays frozen and no weights are updated. Two deployment variants follow. A streaming version detects and resamples hallucinated tokens during generation, and a self-adaptation step fine-tunes the detector on responses sampled from the target model, addressing the distribution shift that arises because different backbones hallucinate in systematically different ways so a detector trained on other models' outputs faces a train-test mismatch. Evaluation covers token-level detection against zero-shot frontier models and against trained detectors including one that fine-tunes every backbone weight, token-level localisation on a second benchmark, object-hallucination detection on self-generated captions and a heavily imbalanced probing set, and a decoding guardrail. Ablations compare flat multilayer-perceptron and transformer probes against the structured trace, remove each module in turn, sweep the read-out layer across depth, and sweep the decoding threshold.

Results. Zero-shot prompting of frontier models is weak at fine-grained localisation (40.6 and 43.9 on the stricter token metric), and trained detectors close only part of the gap. The frozen structured read-out beats all of them on both backbones tested -- 63.2 and 52.9 against 59.1 and 49.8 for the strongest trained competitor on one backbone, 61.7 and 51.2 against 55.4 and 46.7 on the other -- which the authors note holds even though that competitor fine-tunes every backbone weight while theirs updates none, and at lower latency (1.15 times vanilla generation against 1.21 to 1.30 for the trained detectors). The object-detection result is the one that isolates the paper's claim. Every prior detector plateaus between 69 and 75 AUC on the imbalanced probing set, whether it is an external verifier or a flat hand-crafted read-out of the same frozen internals, while structuring that identical evidence as a trace reaches 90.0 -- and 92.3 F1 on self-generated captions against 82.0 for the strongest prior method. So the gain is attributed to representation of the evidence rather than to access to it. In deployment, the streaming detector cuts object-hallucination rates from 18.0 and 37.2 to 15.3 and 31.4 unadapted and to 8.2 and 16.6 after self-adaptation -- a 55 percent reduction -- at 1.06 times vanilla latency. Two controls make that readable. The same reject-and-resample guardrail is run with every competing detector, isolating detection quality from the intervention, and self-adaptation is also applied to the strongest competitor, which improves it substantially but leaves it well behind, showing adaptation alone does not explain the result. And the improvement is shown not to come from suppressing content: guarded captions retain 95.4 percent of the vanilla word count, preserve correct-object coverage, eliminate the empty captions vanilla decoding produced, and have slightly lower perplexity with a small distribution shift. The ablations are informative in two directions. Flat probes over the same signals trail the structured model by at least 30 points, and no single module suffices -- the best individually reaches 56.9 against 63.2 for the combination, with removing the relational graph costing 10.1 and removing response recurrence 4.6. The layer sweep finds detection climbing through early layers, peaking mid-network, and falling toward the output where representations specialise for next-token prediction rather than grounding, with the same shape on every backbone; reading from several layers at once consistently underperforms the single peak layer, which the authors explain as depth pooling diluting a narrow band with early generic features and late prediction-specific ones, aggravated by varying activation scales biasing naive averaging toward high-norm layers. The threshold sweep reports the trade rather than only the chosen point: a more aggressive setting cuts hallucination a further 36 percent while removing 12 percent of correct mentions.

Limitations. Stated where it matters. The read-out layer is chosen per backbone on validation data, so it is a tuned hyperparameter rather than a constant, and the paper reports that multi-layer pooling underperforms rather than presenting the single-layer choice as obviously right. The decoding threshold is likewise selected as the most aggressive setting that does not remove correct content, with the cost of a more aggressive one quantified. Self-adaptation requires sampling from the target model, so a deployed detector is model-specific and has to be re-adapted per backbone. Not stated but worth noticing: the detector is trained supervised on hallucination labels, so it is a learned classifier over internal states rather than an interpretability result about how grounding is represented -- the layer sweep is suggestive about where grounding lives but the model is optimised for detection, not for locating a mechanism. The comparison against a fine-tuned competitor is favourable but that competitor is also solving a different problem, since fine-tuning changes the model while this leaves it frozen. And the object-hallucination gains are largest on a benchmark the authors describe as heavily imbalanced, where AUC and F1 can move a great deal on relatively few decisions.

Relevance. Two results here bear directly on how this archive reads probing evidence. The first is a controlled attribution that is rare and clean: every prior detector -- external verifiers and flat hand-crafted read-outs of the same frozen internals alike -- plateaus between 69 and 75 AUC on one benchmark, and structuring that identical evidence as a graph over patches, query tokens and generated tokens reaches 90. Nothing about access to the model changed; only the representation of the evidence did. That is a direct challenge to the archive's implicit default of scoring an internal signal with a linear probe over pooled activations, and the flat-probe ablation makes the point again from the other side, trailing the structured model by at least 30 points on the same signals. Where a linear probe underperforms, the correct inference may be that the evidence is relational or sequential rather than that it is absent. The second is a depth result that pairs with material the archive already holds. Detection climbs through early layers, peaks mid-network, and falls toward the output where representations specialise for next-token prediction rather than grounding -- the same shape on every backbone tested -- and reading from several layers at once consistently underperforms the single peak, because pooling dilutes a narrow band with early generic features and late prediction-specific ones while varying activation scales bias naive averaging toward high-norm layers. That is a concrete argument against layer-pooled probes, and it sits alongside the archive's other evidence that the useful band is narrow, model-specific, and not where a hand-picked fraction of depth would put it. Two cautions on how far to read the interpretability claim. This is a supervised detector optimised for detection, not an analysis of how grounding is computed, so the layer profile locates where a classifier finds signal rather than where the model does the work. And the paper's own discipline on the mitigation side is worth borrowing regardless: it runs the same guardrail with every competing detector to isolate detection quality from the intervention, applies its self-adaptation step to the strongest competitor to show adaptation is not the explanation, and reports word count, correct-object coverage, perplexity and distribution shift to establish that the improvement is not suppression.

Label-Free Parkinson's Disease Screening from Face and Voice through Mechanistic Interpretability

Jiaheng Su, Yu Sun · 2026-08-10 · arxiv

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08976>

Builds a Parkinson's screen from control data alone using a contrastive activation direction derived from synthetically degraded healthy speech and a nearest-neighbour anomaly score in face-encoder space, and gives a measurable precondition -- positive cosine between the synthetic and real disease directions -- that predicts in advance which modality the steering primitive will work on.

Problem. Supervised video screens for Parkinson's disease need labelled corpora that require clinical recruitment and expert annotation, so they are small, class-imbalanced, subject-confounded and prone to learning shortcuts. Healthy talking-head video is effectively unlimited while labelled disease video is scarce, which suggests treating the disease as a deviation from healthy in a learned representation. The open question is whether an interpretability primitive built from a proxy contrast -- degrading healthy examples toward what the disease looks like -- yields a direction that actually points at the real disease, and whether that can be checked rather than assumed.

Contributions.

  • A face-plus-voice Parkinson's screen in which no disease label touches any fit -- the reference is training controls only, normalisation constants are frozen at fit time, and a subject can be scored in isolation without reference to other test subjects.
  • A synthetic-dysarthria contrastive activation direction in HuBERT space, built by degrading control speech with time-stretch and breathiness rather than by contrasting labelled patients against labelled controls.
  • The alignment principle: a measurable precondition stating that a synthetic-degradation CAA detector works when the cosine between the synthetic and the real disease direction is positive, confirmed by +0.37 for voice with AUROC 0.765 and -0.48 for face with AUROC at chance.
  • A mechanistic account of the modality asymmetry: HuBERT is trained on raw speech and so encodes the acoustic phenomenon that dysarthria is, while a FER2013-trained expression ViT places its disease signal in static appearance roughly orthogonal to facial motion, which is why a synthetic hypomimia degradation points the wrong way and a direction-free anomaly score recovers the signal instead.
  • An integrity audit that reports its own failure: the encoder-selection transfer check recovers the voice choice but not the face choice, and the authors downgrade their headline accordingly.

Method. Two frozen pretrained encoders and two scoring primitives, with all fitting done on training controls only and disease labels reserved for evaluation and for a post-hoc analysis. For voice, a contrastive activation addition direction is built in HuBERT space as the mean-activation contrast between control speech and the same speech synthetically degraded toward hypokinetic dysarthria by time-stretching and adding breathiness; a test clip is scored by projecting its centred embedding onto that direction. For face, no direction is used: a k-nearest-neighbour anomaly score measures mean distance to the training-control embedding cluster of a face-expression Vision Transformer. Normalisation constants are estimated on controls and frozen, so each score is a function of one input video and a subject can be scored in isolation. The two scores are combined by equal-weight late fusion. The paper's analytical contribution is the alignment principle: a derivation predicting that a synthetic-degradation CAA detector works when the cosine between the synthetic direction and the real disease direction is positive, and scores at or below chance otherwise -- a quantity that can be measured directly once labels are available for audit. Evaluation is on the YouTubePD benchmark, test subset n=157 with 20 PD and 137 controls, so prevalence is 13% and naive accuracy is uninformative at 0.87; reporting therefore uses AUROC, F1 and negative predictive value at the Youden-optimal operating point, with bootstrap confidence intervals.

Results. The alignment principle is confirmed in both directions on the two modalities: the measured cosine between synthetic and real disease directions is +0.37 for HuBERT and -0.48 for the face-expression ViT, and correspondingly voice CAA reaches AUROC 0.765 while an anti-aligned face 'hypomimia' CAA direction scores about 0.50, chance. The face signal is recoverable by the direction-free anomaly primitive instead (AUROC 0.751), and equal-weight fusion reaches 0.802 with a 95% interval of [0.70, 0.89], sensitivity 0.70, specificity 0.81, NPV 0.95 and PPV 0.35. Across three speech encoders AUROC tracks the cosine monotonically -- SEW-D at +0.24 gives 0.619, wav2vec 2.0 at +0.28 gives 0.728, HuBERT at +0.37 gives 0.765 -- though this is three points. The face-encoder ablation shows the choice matters enormously and is not explained by general representational quality: the FER2013-trained expression ViT reaches 0.751 while DINOv2 scores 0.41, CLIP 0.30, MARLIN 0.47 and a sibling expression encoder 0.47, all at or below chance. Against the published YouTubePD baselines, the label-free voice detector alone improves F1 from 0.27 to 0.43 over the supervised audio-only baseline and the fused system reaches 0.47, while the supervised face baseline at 0.86-0.92 AUROC remains well ahead -- which the authors state plainly rather than claiming state of the art. Four integrity audits are run and one of them fails informatively. Detector scores correlate near zero with face crop size, video length and frame rate; permuting test labels gives p < 0.01; within-test split-halves match the full-test AUROC inside the bootstrap interval. But the dev-to-test transfer audit, which asks whether the encoder choices could have been made without test labels, recovers the voice choice (HuBERT ranks first on dev and transfers) and does not recover the face choice: on the dev split the chosen face ViT ties DINOv2 at 0.629, and DINOv2 then scores 0.41 on test against the ViT's 0.751, so the +0.28 test margin is not identifiable from dev alone. The authors therefore report the face-side and fused AUROC as potentially optimistic and defer the face-encoder choice to pre-registered external validation. All results are on recorded video from one public benchmark; no clinical deployment or prospective data is involved.

Limitations. Stated, and unusually forthrightly: the face encoder was selected in a way the dev split cannot justify, so the face and fused AUROC are labelled potentially optimistic pending external validation, and a definitive face-encoder choice is explicitly deferred. The system is framed as a rule-out triage screen and not a diagnostic -- NPV 0.95 is the operating use case while PPV is about 0.34 at 13% prevalence and would be lower in the community, so a positive output should trigger follow-up rather than a label. The dev split's PD voices are less separable than test's, verified with a model-free speaking-rate marker showing the PD-control gap widening from -0.37 SD on dev to -0.88 SD on test, and the authors decline to analyse the face modality's dev behaviour because it is inconsistent across encoders. Not stated but worth noticing: the test set contains 20 positive cases, so every clinical metric rests on a handful of subjects and the fused interval spans 0.70 to 0.89 -- wide enough that the fusion's 0.802 is not clearly separated from either single modality at 0.751 and 0.765. The alignment principle is validated on three voice encoders plus one face pilot, which is enough to show the sign is predictive but not enough to establish the monotone relationship between cosine and AUROC that the encoder table suggests. Everything is one benchmark of YouTube talking-head video, whose subjects, recording conditions and severity distribution are not a screening population, and the synthetic degradation is a hand-specified pair of transformations rather than anything validated against clinical dysarthria.

Relevance. Two things here transfer, and neither depends on the clinical domain. First, this is a use of a contrastive activation direction purely as a detector, with no steering at all, and it supplies the thing the archive has been missing for such directions: a falsifiable precondition, measurable in advance of the outcome, for whether a direction built from a proxy contrast points at the phenomenon of interest. The alignment principle says a synthetic-degradation CAA works when its cosine with the real direction is positive, and it is tested in both directions on the same paper -- +0.37 giving AUROC 0.765 for voice, -0.48 giving chance for face. Making a negative prediction and then confirming the failure is rare, and it is the shape of evidence the archive should want when a paper claims that a mean-difference direction encodes what its contrast pair was designed to encode. The mechanistic explanation is equally portable: the direction points the wrong way not because the concept is absent but because the encoder was trained for static emotion classification and places the relevant signal roughly orthogonal to motion, so what a contrast direction means depends on what the representation was trained to separate. That is the same lesson the archive holds about steering vectors whose contrast pairs vary along more axes than the intended one. Second, the encoder ablation is a caution about representational quality as a proxy for usability: DINOv2 and CLIP, far stronger general encoders, score at 0.41 and 0.30 under the identical anomaly protocol where a small FER2013-trained ViT reaches 0.751. A direction or a distance is a property of a specific representation, not of how good the model is. The dev-to-test audit is worth borrowing outright as a discipline -- ask whether your component choice could have been made without the labels you are reporting against, and if it could not, say so and mark the number optimistic, which is exactly what these authors do. The main caution in citing this: 20 positive cases, one benchmark, and the alignment principle validated on three encoders, so the sign result is convincing and the monotone cosine-to-AUROC relationship is not.

Avalon-ToM-Bench: Evaluating Fine-Grained Theory of Mind via Asymmetric Game Mechanics

Yen-Shan Chen, Yu Chian Duan, Chih-En Kuo, Jian-Bin Wu et al. · 2026-08-10 · arxiv

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09638>

Turns the hidden-role game Avalon into a diagnostic instrument rather than an arena, decomposing theory of mind into a 2x2 taxonomy of perspective-constrained binary statements, and shows by probing, ground-truth injection and steering that models represent the right answer internally while failing to say it.

Problem. Theory-of-mind evaluation for language models sits between two unsatisfying options. Static benchmarks ask directly about a character's belief or emotion, stripping away the asymmetric information, deception and multi-party dynamics that make social reasoning hard. Interactive hidden-role games restore those, but score models on end-to-end outcomes such as win rate, which conflates mind-reading with strategic planning, persuasion and memory and makes it impossible to isolate a theory-of-mind deficit. Neither approach says why models fail or what would fix it.

Contributions.

  • A 2x2 decomposition of theory of mind -- epistemic versus motivational mental states crossed with inference versus action -- instantiated as 408 human-authored, perspective-constrained binary propositions over 97 Avalon scenarios, with a separate rules-recall control set that isolates game knowledge from reasoning about minds.
  • A permutation-test validation that the taxonomy carves at real joints rather than imposing an arbitrary partition: difficulty loads on one axis and the benefit of chain-of-thought on the orthogonal one, a double dissociation.
  • Three converging analyses -- correlational probing, behavioural ground-truth injection, and causal steering with random and negated controls -- localising theory-of-mind failure to expression rather than representation, with the injection result extending the claim to proprietary models whose internals cannot be reached.
  • A separation of test-time deliberation from learned reasoning policy on the same base weights: +1.1 pp for toggling chain-of-thought against +11.0 pp for reasoning training, with the chain-of-thought number shown to be a near-cancellation of 16 percent fixes against 12 percent breaks rather than a small reliable gain.

Method. Avalon is used as a controlled measurement environment rather than a game to be won. Theory of mind is factored along two axes -- whether the targeted mental state is epistemic (what another seat knows) or motivational (what another seat wants), and whether the cognitive demand is passive inference or active action -- giving four abilities: Perspective-Taking, Strategic Signaling, Intention Attribution and Tacit Coordination. Each item is a human-authored, human-verified binary proposition evaluated from one designated player's point of view, with the available evidence restricted to that seat's knowledge plus the public interaction history, so a model relying on omniscient context is penalised rather than rewarded. The set is 408 independently evaluated statements across 97 scenarios, near-balanced at about 50 percent TRUE per ability so that a residual TRUE/FALSE prior shows up as an accuracy-F1 gap, plus a separate 34-item rules-recall exam that separates knowing Avalon's mechanics from reasoning about minds. Twenty-eight models are prompted zero-shot with a fixed template. Three converging analyses then localise the failure on the open-weight Qwen3 family: logistic probes trained on last-token hidden states at every layer under five-fold cross-validation, compared against the same model's own chain-of-thought output on the identical items; behavioural injection of the ground-truth answer into the context; and causal writing, adding a TRUE-minus-FALSE class-mean difference direction at the best probe layer, signed per item by the cross-validated probe, with random and negated control directions. Whether the taxonomy carves anything real is itself tested, by decomposing per-cell difficulty and per-cell chain-of-thought benefit into main effects for each axis and assessing them with a permutation test that shuffles cell labels.

Results. The taxonomy passes its own test with a double dissociation: item difficulty loads on the epistemic-versus-motivational axis (-8.5 pp, p < 0.001) while the inference-versus-action axis has no significant effect on difficulty (-3.2 pp, p = 0.14), and the benefit of chain-of-thought shows the mirror pattern, loading on inference-versus-action (-7.9 pp, p = 0.008) with no epistemic effect (-1.2 pp, p = 0.71). What makes an item hard and what deliberation can fix are therefore controlled by different axes. Rule mastery is not social intelligence: nearly every model answers the rules exam well (mean about 83.3 percent, most at or above 88) while scoring far lower on items applying those same rules from a constrained viewpoint. Class means track capability -- 84.1 proprietary, 71.9 open reasoning, 66.5 open non-reasoning, with Gemini-2.5-Pro at 89.7 and GPT-5 at 88.5 -- and abilities order consistently as Intention Attribution 78.2 > Tacit Coordination 75.8 > Strategic Signaling 70.4 > Perspective-Taking 63.5, with a significant interaction (-9.3 pp, p = 0.027) making Perspective-Taking harder than the two main effects predict. The central finding is a representation-expression gap established three ways. Linear probes on hidden states recover 77-82 percent against the same models' own chain-of-thought outputs at 62-70 percent, with the answer linearly decodable already in middle layers and the gap largest on Perspective-Taking. Injecting the ground-truth answer into context raises accuracy by 12.6 pp averaged over all 28 models, largest again on Perspective-Taking at +16.3, and never backfires -- 26 to 28 of 28 models improve on every ability, so there is no sign of god's-eye over-claiming. Supervised steering at the best probe layer lifts output accuracy onto the probe ceiling at every size (Qwen3-1.7B 54 to 77, 4B 70 to 81, 8B 74 to 83) and raises True- and False-recall together, most strikingly un-skewing Qwen3-1.7B from a degenerate 10.7/98.0 TPR/TNR to a balanced 73.8/80.2, so the gain is surfaced content rather than a threshold shift; random control directions give about zero and negated ones give negative effects. Finally, test-time deliberation and training separate sharply: toggling chain-of-thought on the same Qwen3 weights moves overall accuracy by only +1.1 pp with the sign mixed across the size sweep (negative at 0.6B and 4B), while comparing base Qwen3-4B to its reasoning-trained variant gives +11.0 pp overall and up to +20.6 on Perspective-Taking. The small chain-of-thought average hides instability rather than stability: it flips the outcome on about 27 percent of questions, fixing roughly 16 percent and breaking 12 percent, so the net gain is fixes and breaks nearly cancelling.

Limitations. Stated, at length. Every item comes from one game, so whether the per-ability ordering and the represent-but-not-express gap transfer to negotiation, multi-party dialogue or other social-deduction settings is left open. Items are drawn from games the authors played, recorded and annotated themselves rather than from independent human play at scale, trading ecological breadth for label certainty, and a few labels rest on benchmark-specific conventions such as the sabotage-priority protocol, which the authors call internally consistent design choices rather than universal facts. The binary TRUE/FALSE format that makes labels unambiguous also compresses theory of mind into a verification task and does not measure generating mental-state-aware behaviour in open play. At 408 items across 97 scenarios with unequally sized dimensions the set is modest and, like any fixed benchmark, subject to contamination as it circulates. The mechanistic claims are scoped explicitly: probing and steering require internal access and run only on open-weight Qwen3 models, probes are correlational, and the steering result is described as an existence proof that the answer is linearly writable rather than a deployment recipe -- it needs a per-item sign from a supervised readout, and the fully label-free variant helps only the smallest, degenerate model. Ground-truth injection is what extends the expression-gap claim behaviourally to the proprietary frontier. Not stated but worth noticing: the probe-versus-output comparison is not quite like for like, since the probe is fitted with cross-validation on these items while the model's chain of thought is zero-shot, so some of the 77-82 against 62-70 gap is the probe having seen the task distribution.

Relevance. The archive's cleanest instance of the represent-but-do-not-express pattern, and unusually the claim is built three ways rather than one. The probe result alone would be weak -- a cross-validated probe beating a zero-shot chain of thought is partly the probe having seen the distribution -- but the paper adds two things that do not share that weakness. Injecting the ground-truth answer into context raises accuracy by 12.6 points across all 28 models and never backfires on any ability for 26 to 28 of them, which is a behavioural test needing no internal access and which is what carries the claim to proprietary models; and steering along a class-mean TRUE-minus-FALSE direction at the best probe layer lifts output accuracy exactly onto the probe ceiling at three model sizes, with random directions at about zero and negated ones negative. The recall table is what makes the steering result convincing rather than suggestive: Qwen3-1.7B moves from a degenerate 10.7/98.0 True/False recall to a balanced 73.8/80.2, so the intervention surfaced content instead of shifting a threshold -- exactly the control the archive's steering material keeps asking for, and one most steering papers omit. The paper is also disciplined about what steering does not show, calling it an existence proof that the answer is linearly writable rather than a recipe, on the ground that the per-item sign comes from a supervised readout and the label-free variant helps only the smallest model. That is the honest version of a result others would have reported as a method. Two further items transfer beyond the domain. The double dissociation established by permutation test -- difficulty loading on one taxonomy axis and chain-of-thought benefit on the orthogonal one -- is a template for showing that a proposed decomposition of a capability is real rather than a naming exercise. And the chain-of-thought finding sharpens something the archive holds loosely: a +1.1 point average conceals a 27 percent flip rate, 16 percent fixed and 12 percent broken, so a small net effect from added deliberation should be read as instability rather than as a small improvement, and reporting only the mean would have hidden that entirely.

Safety Cost of Steering Vectors Is Separable and Reducible

Yuxiao Li, Gjergji Kasneci · 2026-08-09 · arxiv

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08383>

Shows that the part of a steering vector which breaks a model's refusal behaviour is a separate direction from the part that produces the intended behavioural effect, and learns that direction by constrained optimization so it can be ablated without losing the steering.

Problem. Steering vectors are adopted because they are cheap -- one direction in activation space, computed from a small contrastive dataset, applied at inference with no retraining. But injecting one degrades safety: a vector built for a behaviourally unrelated purpose raises compliance with harmful requests, and no effective mitigation existed. The obvious fix does not work. Cosine similarity with the standard refusal direction predicts the degradation, yet ablating that direction from the steering vector fails to restore safety, because refusal is mediated by several mechanistically independent directions and the canonical one is not necessarily the one a given vector is damaging. Isolating the responsible component therefore cannot be done from any known direction, and making the model merely more cautious buys safety with false refusals.

Contributions.

  • The safety-degrading and effect-relevant components of a steering vector are geometrically separable, and in several configurations partially competing
  • CAST, a post-hoc constrained optimization that learns the direction to ablate rather than assuming a known one
  • A differentiable refusal proxy -- KL restricted to refusal-indicative tokens -- that makes safety optimizable without a non-differentiable judge
  • Generalization to three jailbreak families never seen during optimization

Method. The sanitized vector is the original with a learned subspace projected out, renormalized to the original length; the main experiments use a rank-1 approximation, so one unit direction is learned. Three objectives are in tension and are handled as a constrained problem rather than a weighted sum. The safety loss uses a differentiable stand-in for refusal: rather than full KL, it measures divergence restricted to a small set of refusal-indicative tokens, weighted by the base model's own probabilities so the quantity is dominated by refusal tokens the unsteered model would actually produce, and so that distributional shifts on non-refusal tokens -- which may be exactly the steering effect worth keeping -- are not penalized. Because that divergence can go negative, minimizing it does not merely restore refusal to baseline but can strengthen it past baseline when the other constraints permit. The effect constraint preserves the full output distribution of the originally steered model on prompts where the target behaviour is active, and the false-refusal constraint penalizes any increase in refusal on benign prompts with its tolerance set to exactly zero. The Lagrangian is solved by primal-dual updates: the direction moves by gradient descent while the two dual variables rise when their constraint is violated and decay when it is satisfied, so only the tolerances need setting by hand. Training uses small symmetric multipliers because in that regime the model's response is approximately first-order in the multiplier, which makes the safety-interfering component easier to isolate. Evaluated on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, on the three most reliably steerable alignment behaviours -- corrigibility, power-seeking, self-awareness -- with the direction learned against three template attacks and tested additionally on three optimization-based and adaptive ones it never saw.

Results. The negative result on the obvious baseline is the most useful part. Steering raises attack success rate on all three models, with worst-case increases exceeding 63 percent on Qwen-7B; ablating the standard refusal direction recovers at most about 8 points of mean ASR and leaves worst-case ASR largely unchanged on two of the three models -- 61.4 against 63.5 on Qwen-7B, 51.4 against 51.2 on Llama-8B, which is no recovery at all. CAST instead brings mean ASR below the unsteered baseline on both Qwen models (15.7 against a 24.5 baseline on 7B, 7.5 against 9.0 on 14B) and to within one point on Llama-8B. Broken out across the full multiplier range and all seven attacks individually -- 108 configurations -- no attack exceeds 4.2 points above the unsteered baseline after sanitization, the largest positive deviation is 4.6, and in more than two-thirds of cases the sanitized vector is actively safer than not steering at all. That holds on the three held-out attack families, so the learned direction captures a shared mechanism rather than an artifact of the templates it was fitted on. The separability claim is measured, not assumed: the sanitized vector's behavioural shift relative to the original averages 104.9 percent on Qwen-7B and 112.6 percent on Llama-8B, meaning removal of the safety-degrading direction typically increases the intended effect, which the paper reads as the two components being partially competing rather than merely separable. The one systematic attenuation is Qwen-14B under positive steering at about 75 percent, with its average across conditions at 87.6. False refusal does not pay for it: no measurable increase on general instruction-following prompts, Qwen models within about 1 percent on the harder borderline set, and Llama-8B's worst case around 3 to 4 points. The failure modes differ by family in a way a single-direction account would not predict -- both Qwen models degrade in the positive steering direction, consistent with directional alignment to the refusal direction, while Llama-8B degrades under both directions.

Limitations. The paper is explicit that the Lagrangian relaxation is nonconvex, so there is no guarantee of global optimality or exact feasibility, and reports that the constraints are satisfied only to close approximation. Three more a reader should weigh. The main results use a rank-1 subspace while the paper's own motivation is that refusal is higher-dimensional; the higher-rank study is a later section rather than the headline. The effect tolerance is chosen empirically as the smallest value that lets safety return to baseline while retaining the steering effect, so the trade-off point is tuned per setting rather than derived. And the direction is learned per model, per behaviour and per layer, which makes this a post-hoc correction applied to each vector before deployment rather than a property of the model -- the paper says as much in calling it a recipe, but the framing 'safety cost is reducible' is easily read as stronger. On measurement: both ASR and false-refusal rate come from LLM judges, which this archive treats as an instrument with its own reliability, and the training multipliers are small enough for the first-order approximation the method leans on while evaluation runs to 1.5.

Relevance. A clean instance of the archive's standing finding that how well a direction detects a property licenses no claim about what intervening on it does. Here the standard refusal direction demonstrably mediates refusal and its cosine similarity with a steering vector predicts the safety damage -- and ablating it recovers almost nothing, leaving worst-case ASR unchanged on two of three models. The direction that must be removed has to be learned bottom-up against the intervention's own objective, and it turns out to be partially opposed to the behavioural effect rather than merely orthogonal to it. That is a sharper statement than the archive currently holds: not only does a detection direction fail to transfer to intervention, the correct intervention direction is one no detector had named.

Reproducing and Stress-Testing Two Approaches to LLM Reasoning Reliability: Test-Time Probability Aggregation and Logic-Representation Editing

Minhan Cho, Jimin Kweon · 2026-08-09 · arxiv

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08514>

Independently reproduces two published reliability methods and stress-tests them across models and domains their authors never tried, finding one reproduces exactly but loses significance everywhere new, and the other rests on a decodable logic direction that a same-norm random direction matches exactly under steering.

Problem. Two recent methods attack reasoning reliability from opposite ends of the stack -- one aggregating token probabilities with self-consistency at inference, the other training projectors that split hidden states into content and logic and editing the logic part toward a valid region. Both were evaluated only by their own authors, neither was independently reproduced, neither was stress-tested across models or domains, and one shipped no public code. Validating such claims matters because reliability is precisely the property that has to hold outside the conditions it was demonstrated in.

Contributions.

  • An independent reproduction of two reliability methods that were evaluated only by their own authors, with one of them having shipped no public code, extended to models and domains the originals never covered.
  • The demonstration that an inference-time aggregation method reproduces its published numbers exactly while failing to reach significance against plain self-consistency on any new domain or public benchmark, with per-item paired tests rather than averages.
  • A direct test of the original authors' own explanation for a negative result -- insufficient sampling budget -- confirming its direction while showing the resulting advantage rests on 2 of 80 items and vanishes when the evaluation set is enlarged.
  • A mechanistic precondition for probability-aggregation methods: uncertainty and answer diversity are necessary but not sufficient, because the path probabilities must also carry information about which answer is correct.
  • A decomposition of the representation-editing method showing that its assumed logic-validity direction is real at one sub-layer (probe accuracy 0.82) and at chance across the layers the method edits (0.52), against a semantic control at 0.95 everywhere -- so representation editing works for semantic attributes and logic validity is encoded weakly and locally.
  • The null control the original lacked: a random direction of the same norm swept over the same strengths traces the same curve as the supervised direction, establishing that the probe-validated direction buys no controllability over noise.
  • A mechanical diagnosis of why a conditional steering gate is inert -- it fires on 98 percent of tokens because reference and task activations come from different distributions -- and a matched-distribution redesign producing the study's only positive steering result, by ablating a content direction rather than adding a validity one.
  • An explicit, non-accusatory account of a headline that cannot be reproduced because it depends on an unreleased component.

Method. Both methods are reimplemented and first checked against their original settings, then extended along the axes their papers did not cover: the aggregation method to four constructed domains and three public benchmarks with a single backbone, and the representation-editing method to four models of 7 to 8 billion parameters. Statistical discipline is applied uniformly and is the study's backbone -- per-item paired Wilcoxon tests rather than comparisons of averages, bootstrap intervals over items, multiple seeds for both sampling and projector retraining, and explicit reporting of how many individual items separate two methods. The sampling-budget hypothesis offered by the original authors for a negative result is tested directly by generating more paths and re-aggregating at growing budget. For the editing method the study goes past reproduction into diagnosis: a held-out linear probe measures whether a logic-validity direction exists and at which layers, with a semantic attribute on a separate corpus as a positive control; the additive intervention is swept over strengths with a norm-relative shift; the trained projector is decoded at the single best sub-layer; and a random direction of the same norm is swept over the same range as a null. A training-free alternative from the steering literature is then implemented faithfully, including a k-nearest-neighbour conditional gate with automatic layer selection, and finally a controlled syllogism task crossing formal validity with believability is built so that reference and task activations come from the same distribution.

Results. The aggregation method reproduces its original grid almost exactly, matching the published accuracy and calibration figures to two decimal places. Extended, it never significantly beats plain self-consistency anywhere: across four new domains no per-item difference reaches significance (paired Wilcoxon p at least 0.28 on every one), and across three public benchmarks it ties on one, edges another by 1.1 and trails a third by 2.0, again with nothing significant. One domain is diagnostic rather than merely null -- the model is uncertain and answer-diverse, the two preconditions the method needs, and it still only ties -- which yields the study's clearest mechanistic statement: uncertainty and answer diversity are necessary but not sufficient, because the path probabilities must additionally carry information about which answer is right, and on that task they do not. The budget hypothesis is then tested and half-vindicated in a way worth recording. Scaling from 8 to 32 sampled paths moves the method from below self-consistency to a 2.5-point lead that nearly halves calibration error, so the direction is as its authors predicted -- but at the larger budget only 2 of 80 items separate the two methods, the paired test does not reach significance, the bootstrap interval on the gap is [0.0, +6.3], and enlarging the evaluation set to 200 items erases the advantage entirely, turning it into -0.25 at p = 0.85. The editing method fares worse. Its headline effect rises on one of four models but not significantly (p = 0.56), and the same recipe significantly degrades two others (p below 0.001 and 0.002) and reduces the effect on a fourth; on a public logic benchmark it degrades even the one model it helped. Retraining the projector under five seeds shows that lone gain is stable across training rather than a single-run artifact, which the authors report in its favour, while noting it remains item-concentrated and per-item non-significant. The diagnosis is the valuable part. A held-out probe separates valid from invalid conclusions at 0.82 accuracy at the single best sub-layer -- so the direction the method assumes does exist -- but collapses to 0.52, chance, when pooled over the layer range the method actually edits, while the same probe on a semantic attribute reaches 0.95 across all layers. Steering along that direction then buys nothing: the additive intervention gives no consistent gain over a strength sweep, decoding the trained projector at the probe's peak layer leaves the effect unchanged on both models (p = 0.90 and 0.40), and a random direction of the same norm traces the same curve as the supervised one over the same strength range. So separability and controllability come apart -- the direction is decodable and is not a causal lever. The training-free alternative fails for a mechanically identifiable reason: its conditional gate fires on 98 percent of tokens and therefore behaves as static steering, because a classifier 0.88-separable on short reference sentences classifies nearly every task token as steer-worthy when reference and task come from different distributions. Removing that mismatch with a matched-distribution syllogism task produces the study's only positive steering result, and it is narrow in three ways the authors enumerate: it requires the matched distribution, it works by ablating the believability direction rather than by adding the validity one, and it only helps a model that already performs the task, since the other answers a constant no at chance. Their conclusion is that model capability rather than the intervention is the dominant factor.

Limitations. Stated and acted on. Evaluation sets are small and the authors repeatedly decline to claim effects their own tests do not support, including for the method they are most sympathetic to -- the 2.5-point budget-scaling lead is reported as at most a weak directional signal, and its disappearance on a larger set is put in the results rather than buried. One constructed corpus is flagged as a contiguous block rather than a random sample with citation-derived noisy labels; a benchmark's label parse rate of 74 percent is reported; one model's reimplementation is marked as not on the same scale as the others because it was run on different hardware and library versions, with only the direction of its effect treated as comparable. On the unreproducible headline the phrasing is exact and worth quoting: the original's most dramatic number relies on a discriminator that was not released, the authors' analogue is degenerate in that it marks every generation valid for one model, an auditable third-party judge shows no gain, and they state they find no evidence of fabrication but that the headline leans on an unauditable component and so cannot be reproduced as stated. Not stated but worth noticing: the study is one backbone for the aggregation method and four 7-8B models for the editing method, so 'model-dependent' is established over a narrow capability band, and several of the constructed domains are the authors' own.

Relevance. This is the strongest single instance in the archive of detection and control coming apart, and it is strong because of the control the original paper omitted. A held-out probe finds a logic-validity direction at 0.82 accuracy, which by the usual standard would license the whole intervention -- and then a random direction of the same norm, swept over the same strengths, traces the same behavioural curve as the supervised one. Not a weaker effect: the same curve. Decoding the trained projector at exactly the layer where the probe peaks leaves the target metric unchanged on both models (p = 0.90 and 0.40). The authors' phrasing is the one to carry: separability and controllability come apart, and a decodable direction is not a causal lever. Two further diagnoses generalise beyond this method. The probe's layer profile explains why the recipe fails rather than merely showing that it does -- 0.82 at the single best sub-layer, 0.52 at chance pooled over the layers actually edited, against a semantic attribute at 0.95 everywhere. So logic validity is encoded weakly and locally while semantic attributes are encoded strongly and globally, which predicts that representation editing transfers to the second kind and not the first, and gives a concrete reason to check a direction's layer profile before choosing where to intervene. And the conditional-gate failure is a mechanically identified distribution mismatch: a gate 0.88-separable on short reference sentences fires on 98 percent of task tokens, degenerating to static steering, because the references and the task come from different distributions. Any gated or conditional intervention in the archive should be checked for its firing rate, which is a one-line diagnostic that distinguishes a working gate from an inert one. The one positive steering result is instructive in what it required: a task where reference and steering distributions match, ablation of the content direction rather than addition of the validity direction, and a model that could already do the task -- with the authors concluding that model capability rather than the intervention is the dominant factor. Finally, the study is a model of how to report an irreproducible result: the original's headline depends on an unreleased discriminator, the authors' analogue is degenerate, an auditable judge shows no gain, and they state they find no evidence of fabrication but that the number leans on an unauditable component and cannot be reproduced as stated. That is the register such a finding should be written in. One further half of the study bears on this topic's neighbour and is worth recording here since it travelled under the same protocol: the inference-time aggregation method reproduces its published numbers to two decimal places on the original setting and then fails to significantly beat plain self-consistency on any of four new domains or three public benchmarks. Its precondition analysis is the transferable part -- uncertainty and answer diversity are necessary but not sufficient for confidence-weighted aggregation to beat majority voting, because the path probabilities must additionally carry information about which answer is correct, and one tested domain satisfies both preconditions while the confidence carries nothing. And the budget-scaling episode is the sharpest methodological lesson: the original authors attributed a negative result to insufficient sampling budget, the reproduction tests that directly, confirms the direction, and then reports that at the larger budget only 2 of 80 items separate the methods, the bootstrap interval on the 2.5-point gap starts at 0.0, and enlarging the evaluation set to 200 items turns the gap into -0.25 at p = 0.85. A scaling curve can move in exactly the predicted direction and still be nothing.

A temporal-horizon steering direction also separates abstraction, urgency, strategic scope and framing, so the intervention may be steering a broad answer register rather than the named variable, and a transfer result shows the construct is involved without identifying the direction's semantic basis. chain of thought faithfulness26Whether a stated chain of thought is an account of the process that produced the answer, and across 26 sources the archive's central negative result. The measurements are consistent: faithfulness varies substantially across tasks, with models sometimes relying heavily on the trace and sometimes largely ignoring it, and it decreases with model size and capability on most tasks studied -- so the earliest source's own conclusion is a conditional (traces can be faithful if size and task are chosen carefully) rather than an endorsement. Two structural problems recur. The standard test detects unfaithfulness only where an injected cue changes the answer, so it is silent whenever the answer is unchanged, and it intervenes on the prompt rather than on the trace. And the property is not simply desirable: a perfectly faithful agent executes a harmful action whenever its reasoning arrives at one, which makes the usual requirement self-contradictory -- faithful enough to be monitored, robust enough to override its own reasoning. One source measures that tension directly, finding the most faithful model follows tampered safe reasoning in 97.5 percent of cases while rejecting unsafe reasoning in only 12.3, higher faithfulness going with lower safety across seven models, and steering along the two directions trading them against each other by 5 to 9 points against magnitude-matched random vectors moving either by at most 1.1. The archive's operative position is that faithfulness is a relation between the trace and the answer rather than a property of the trace: monitoring works as a consistency check between the two, and a trace can be clean, correct and on-topic while the action taken is arbitrary.
chain of thought23The intermediate tokens a model emits before its answer, and across 23 sources an object the archive is careful to describe rather than to interpret -- one source proposes 'derivational trace' as a neutral stand-in precisely because reading these tokens as reasoning is unsupported by the available evidence and, it argues, actively harmful. What is established is largely formal and negative. Formally, generating intermediate tokens lifts the expressive ceiling of a bounded-depth transformer, with the number of steps tied to a circuit class; one archived construction realises graph traversal in two layers and a linear-step branching measure without layer normalisation, and another shows a sharpness-based bound growing linearly rather than exponentially in the relevant parameter for a parity family. Empirically the trace and the answer are separate information channels: content present in one can be absent from the other in both directions, traces can be swapped between problems or deleted with the answer unchanged, and post-training that improves accuracy need not improve the trace's semantic validity. The corpus's practical positions are that the trace is useful as a consistency check against the answer rather than as an account of computation, that its length is a compute knob with a bend in the curve, and that a large fraction of it is redundant -- with the caveat from a head-to-head comparison that redundancy is diffuse rather than located in any identifiable class of step.
detection versus control21Whether a direction that separates a property from its absence is also a direction that changes it when you push along it -- and across 21 sources the assumption that the two coincide is the one the archive has most thoroughly falsified. The assumption is load-bearing rather than incidental: it is what licenses selecting a steering direction by its discriminability, which is exactly what the foundational method does when it picks the most discriminative attention heads. The direct refutation is quantitative. Inverted vectors -- high discriminability with a strongly negative relationship between steering strength and concept score -- are mined systematically rather than found by chance, three per model-concept pair across 15 pairs, one reaching an AUC of 0.97 with a Spearman correlation of -1. So alignment with positive representations does not determine even the sign of the steering effect, and the worst untreated case moves a concept backwards (promotion score 1.50 against an unsteered 1.75, corrected to 3.57). The authors read this as the explanation for the variability in steering efficacy reported across model-concept pairs in prior work. Several sources have absorbed the lesson into their methods rather than their conclusions: one reports its difference-of-means family as the weakest of four classifiers and declines to pick the intervention by classification accuracy; another finds probe-steering agreement near zero in early layers (-0.03 to 0.07) rising to about 0.91 at the final layer, and derives an early-layer waiver from that curve rather than from convenience. Two further asymmetries appear. Detection generalises where control does not, and control generalises where detection does not: a temporal-horizon direction trained on binary answers transfers to a monetary task with a different format, while a defect direction fitted on controlled pairs scores 83.3 percent held out on those pairs and 19.6 to 30.0 percent on real benchmarks. And a steering effect can be damage: the archive's recurring demand is a matched null -- a random direction of comparable activation volume -- without which suppressing a feature and observing degradation reports that something changed rather than that the right thing did.
pass@k21The probability that at least one of k sampled responses is correct, used across 21 sources as the archive's measure of what a model can reach as opposed to what it reliably produces. Reading it correctly is most of what the corpus has to say. Small k measures sampling efficiency and large k probes the boundary, and the two can move in opposite directions within one training run -- pass@1 rising across steps while coverage at pass@256 falls, and in one distillation study the curve crossing between k=1 and k=1024 in every setting measured. A rising curve is also compatible with within-question stability collapsing, so reporting only coverage hides that the same model now answers inconsistently. Two corrections to naive use are established. Scoring the chain as well as the answer reverses the verdict about reinforcement learning at every k, because base models win plain pass@k partly by producing wrong chains that land on right answers. And for candidate banks produced by adaptive search or dependent procedures the usual estimator is a descriptive summary of what was returned rather than an unbiased probability -- one source states this and notes most papers reporting the metric do not. The archive's practical demand is that a method report pass@k across a range rather than at one budget, since the property worth having is competitiveness with the base model uniformly across k rather than a win at either end.
test-time compute21Computation spent at inference rather than training, and across 21 sources the resource the archive's scaling literature is about. Its foundational result here is a trade against parameters: a compute-optimal allocation strategy improves scaling efficiency more than fourfold over a fixed best-of-N baseline, and in a FLOPs-matched evaluation lets a smaller base model outperform a 14 times larger one on problems where it already has non-trivial success rates -- with that last clause being the condition the archive keeps finding attached. The corpus's accumulated qualifications: the same nominal budget buys different things under different regimes, so a single scalar makes results incomparable; more of it is not monotone in accuracy because the aggregation rule is amplified rather than averaged; and its value is conditional on the instance, with budget increases that pay on a feasible instance changing nothing on an infeasible one. One source separates it from a confound worth noting -- an improvement attributed neither to added compute alone nor to information encoded in phrasing.
monitorability18How much an observer can learn about what a model is doing by reading what it emits, and across 18 sources the safety-adjacent property the archive treats as a relation between task, access and monitor rather than a property of a model. The measured findings: reasoning models are consistently more monitorable than their instruction-tuned counterparts on both axes across all four model pairs tested, with the highest at 78.3 percent average and the largest gaps on the cue where concealment would matter most; difficulty is inversely related to it, with the hardest science benchmark lowest; and scores are similar across all four trace-transition types, which is that source's argument that marking non-switch cases faithful by default throws away most of the available signal. Three limits are stated by the sources themselves. It is almost always evaluated where the prompt directly incentivises concealment, which is not the deployment case. Detection depends on the monitor, so a number bounds that monitor rather than monitorability in general. And where the trace is latent there is no trace to read, so monitoring must fall back on probing or verbalising the latent states, and how much is retained that way is unknown. The archive's own position, from its findings, is that monitoring works as a consistency check between a trace and the answer it accompanies rather than as anomaly detection within the trace.
residual stream17The additively updated activation vector each transformer layer reads from and writes to, and in this archive the substrate every interpretability method operates on rather than an object studied in itself. What the 17 sources establish about it is mostly negative and methodological. It has no privileged basis: individual neurons are polysemantic and there is no reason to expect features here to align with the neuron basis at all, which is the premise the sparse-autoencoder literature is built on -- and that literature's own numbers show the cost, with automated interpretability scores declining from about 0.30 at layer 0 to 0.15 by layer 5 and reconstruction raising perplexity from 25 to 40 when substituted back. The causal information is often compact: replacing the whole stream at a site has a comparable effect to a low-rank patch, so the relevant subspace is small. And one source names the interpretive gap directly -- aligning explanations to a separately learned codebook answers what a direction means in a proxy space while leaving implicit the rule that actually writes into the stream. Practical scope caveats recur: methods tuned for it do not robustly transfer to intermediate MLP layers, and attention heads are typically untouched.
calibration16Whether a stated or implied probability matches the frequency it is right, used across 16 sources in three different senses that are worth keeping apart. As a property of a model's own confidence it is the failure the archive keeps finding: chain-of-thought supervision reduces composition errors without fixing it, a model scores 52.4 percent when the correct option is A and 31.6 to 33.6 for other positions on a position-balanced set, and internal-state statistics differ sharply in calibration error under the same conditions (0.1216 and 0.0858 for one against 0.2451 and 0.1764 for another). As a property of a critic it has direct consequences for training, since a miscalibrated value estimate produces systematically asymmetric advantages -- one source tracks Brier 0.2031 to 0.1637 and expected calibration error 0.2612 to 0.2131, with the largest gap exactly where overestimation inflates negative advantages. As a fitted correction it inherits whatever defines its calibration set, and one source notes it needs paired truthful and hallucinated representations with no sensitivity analysis over set size or composition reported. Two methodological findings are transferable. The measurement is often achievable from the first 12 of 28 layers, so no full forward pass is needed. And a calibration improvement is only evidence if the metric and the objective are different measurements -- one archived method optimises answer-token confidence and is scored by an error computed from the same quantity, which makes the improvement partly a check that optimisation succeeded. One source names the design squeeze directly: models weak enough to have headroom lack the signal, and models with the signal lack the headroom, so the space where self-correction of calibration can be measured cleanly is nearly empty.
entropy collapse16The fall of policy entropy to near zero within a few hundred steps of reinforcement learning on reasoning tasks, and across 16 sources one of the archive's best-quantified training phenomena. It is front-loaded: across 11 runs of 2,400 gradient steps, 73 percent of entropy consumption and 76 percent of performance gain occur in the first 200 steps and the first 800 account for over 93 percent of gain with 94 percent of entropy loss, so more than two-thirds of training yields marginal returns. It coincides with the highest training reward, which several sources read as premature exploitation of a narrow set of high-reward patterns. The interpretive question the corpus takes seriously is what collapsed. A measured fall is compatible with the policy having calcified merely stylistic choices or with its having pruned genuinely distinct inferential pathways, and the source that separates them finds the collapse significantly stronger in a semantic contrast than a syntactic one -- so it is inferences rather than phrasing. That same source shows the compensating gain and the cost in one measurement: the trained policy's entropy collapses correctly where only one continuation is legal, and with illegal continuations masked away the flatter distilled policy outperforms it by about 60 percent. Many mitigations exist -- clipping variants, covariance-based penalties, entropy regularisation, strict on-policy updating, and restricting updates to high-entropy tokens -- and the corpus's caution about them is that one paper's largest reported share of the gain came from switching to strict on-policy training rather than from the entropy mechanism it credits, and that one theoretical account depends on the advantage of tokens never sampled, which most algorithms cannot estimate.
latent reasoning16Keeping intermediate computation in continuous hidden states instead of emitting tokens, which removes the token cost of a chain of thought and, by construction, removes the readable trace with it. Across 16 sources the corpus reports both sides of that trade and one uncomfortable null. The gains are real where the ablation is controlled: one source's continued-training control -- same checkpoint, same epochs, no latent replacement -- costs 0.65 to 1.63 mean F1 at three scales, so the effect is the latent mechanism rather than extra training, and another cuts 268.56 rationale tokens to 1.60 with an 8.9-fold speedup. Effort scales monotonically in one system (21, 27, 29.5 percent at low, medium and high) though its own minimum-effort comparison is statistically unresolved. The null is the sharpest result: on off-the-shelf models, continuous-state decoding is functionally indistinguishable from discrete decoding -- layer-wise entropy profiles coincide, KL between the two states reaches about 1e-4 in middle layers, cosine similarity 0.996 to 0.998 -- so a latent method that is not trained for it may be doing nothing latent at all. Two design findings recur: latent budgets are usually fixed per question when what a question needs varies, and reasoning latents without rationale supervision are worse than no reasoning latents at all. The inspectability cost is named directly by the one source applying it where decisions must remain auditable.
prompt difficulty15How hard a problem is for a given policy, and across 15 sources the variable that both training and inference allocation turn on. Its central property is that it is policy-relative and moving: a prompt is easy when every rollout succeeds and hard when none does, and both cases produce zero group-relative advantage, so the useful band is the middle -- one method shifts 82.7 percent of its gradient budget into the 0.2-0.9 pass-rate band against a baseline's 58.9. It also moves during training, which is why one source tracks per-task reward trajectories across 20 epochs and classifies tasks into six profiles including forgetting and stubbornly-unlearned rather than treating difficulty as a static label. At inference the same variable governs allocation, with several methods conditioning the thinking budget on it, and one finding the saving concentrated on the easy tail -- 53 to 81 percent token reduction for the top decile and percentile. Two cautions the archive attaches. Difficulty defined by a baseline's own pass rate selects for that baseline's failures, so part of any tiered improvement is regression toward the mean. And benchmark difficulty is only an approximation of what a particular policy can answer, which is why the better sources report tiers by measured pass rate rather than by benchmark label.
reasoning redundancy15The part of a chain of thought that does no work, and the quantity every efficiency method in this archive is trying to identify. Fifteen sources locate it differently — after the answer is derived, where double-checking continues; in tokens with negative marginal log-probability contribution to the correct answer; in segments the model's own likelihood landscape marks as extraneous; in the low-entropy convergence region after a sharp two-phase transition; in review nodes of a dependency graph that have too few descendants or sit too late; in steps receiving little attention from the reasoning-termination token; in later alternative solutions, argued to be actively harmful rather than merely wasteful; and in structure inherited from a teacher whose capacity did not match the student's. This note previously recorded that no source compared these criteria on the same trace. One now does, and the answer reframes the disagreement rather than settling it: at step granularity three importance criteria overlap 70-80% on which steps to preserve while diverging on which to delete, so the criteria converge on a shared reasoning backbone and differ only over interchangeable filler; at token granularity the agreement collapses, and only symbol-aware scoring avoids deleting operators and numbers. That study also refutes the premise several archived methods rest on, reporting that pruning which deliberately targets reflective statements performs no better than pruning that ignores them, because redundancy in long traces is diffuse — the skeleton is repeated and rephrased throughout rather than concentrated in a nameable class of step. Two caveats keep the question open: the comparison covers three generic scoring functions in a distillation setting, so the reasoning-specific criteria above are still untested against each other, and the 70-80% figure is a light-compression number that falls by half at aggressive ratios. Reported reductions run from roughly 40% to 87%, sometimes with accuracy gains.
localization14Attributing a behaviour to a specific part of a model — a layer, a head, a neuron, a direction, a parameter region — and the organizing question of this archive's interpretability work at fourteen sources. The sources agree it is possible and disagree about what a located component means. Granularity changes the answer: on propositional logic, four families of attention heads execute a sequential circuit, while on arithmetic the mechanism is an unordered bag of heuristic neurons, and no source tests whether a computation modular at head level is heuristic inside each head. Method choices change the answer too — how prompts are corrupted, which metric scores the effect and whether layers are patched singly or in windows all shift what activation patching reports, and single-component tracing cannot see components that matter only jointly. Two cautions recur. Being encoded is not being used: a concept can be linearly recoverable while having no influence on the output, and sparse autoencoders improve the first while attenuating the second. And what is located may be a state rather than a property, since memorizing and generalizing circuits compete during training.
out-of-distribution generalization11Whether a trained improvement survives a change in the input distribution, and across 11 sources the axis on which the archive's supervision results most often separate. Two patterns recur. Rationale supervision is worth little in domain and a great deal out of it -- 20 to 28 points across four models on a contamination-audited external benchmark, against nothing measurable in domain for strong backbones -- and a controlled human study attributes the measurable effect to language adherence and inspectability rather than to better reasoning. And reasoning helps most where the recovery is needed: one self-correction method's out-of-domain gains reach 10.9 and 8.8 points where its in-domain gains average about one, and removing the reasoning step costs 8.7 percent in distribution and 55.6 percent out. The corpus's caution is that out-of-distribution is a direction, not a place: transfer to three related languages is near-identical in average between two methods while distributed differently across the three; a defect direction fitted on controlled pairs scores 83.3 percent held out on those pairs and 19.6 to 30.0 on real benchmarks; and a correctness predictor falls from 0.87 to 0.60 across three shifts while the structural geometry it was computed from transfers intact.
reasoning trajectory11A reasoning trace read as a path -- through hidden-state space, through answer space, or through a sequence of committed states -- rather than as a set of independent steps. Across 11 sources it is the framing behind a cluster of methods that share one argument: static activations let probes latch onto lexical surface patterns, so layer-to-layer or step-to-step displacement is the more reliable signal. The archive's results from that framing: error detection from residual-stream motion combined with a region reader and a direction reader; verification without training by comparing a trace's start-to-end hidden-state delta against class centroids; correctness read from step-specific geometry that transfers across tasks while the correctness predictor computed from it does not; and two-dimensional projections of distance-to-answer-choice features that make visible how incorrect trajectories converge earlier than correct ones. Two failure modes are located on the trajectory rather than in it: the sentences that commit a model to agreeing with an incorrect user suggestion, and the step at which a model that correctly flagged a query as harmful overrides that judgement. The corpus's caution is that trajectory geometry's advantage over an ordinary probe on the final state is within a few thousandths of AUROC on several benchmarks, so the general claim is carried by a minority of them.
superposition11Representing more features than there are dimensions by assigning them to an overcomplete set of non-orthogonal directions, which is viable only when features activate sparsely -- the hypothesis that explains why individual neurons are polysemantic and why a learned dictionary is needed to recover features at all. Across 11 sources it is the premise the archive's interpretability work rests on rather than a claim it tests, and the corpus's contribution is mostly about what follows from it. If features are in superposition then no privileged basis exists, so a decomposition depends on the procedure that produced it -- which is the identifiability problem the sparse-autoencoder entry records empirically, with different seeds and widths recovering materially different features from the same activations. One source in the corpus questions whether the latent-thinking phenomena attributed to it are superposition at all, finding continuous and discrete decoding functionally indistinguishable on off-the-shelf models.
hallucination10Producing content unsupported by the evidence, and across 10 sources a category the archive's better work refuses to treat as one thing. The most useful decomposition separates reasoning hallucination, where a flawed inference step propagates into a wrong conclusion and the model could in principle have got it right, from knowledge hallucination, where the model lacks the facts and no amount of further reasoning supplies them -- worse, repeated refinement can make an unsupported answer more convincing. That paper's ablation shows the two remedies are not interchangeable: reflection alone leaves coverage at exactly 100 percent while abstention alone leaves the hallucination proxy essentially unchanged at 20.0, because refusing on unanswerable prompts does nothing about errors on answerable ones. A second decomposition, in vision, splits by whether the failure survives masking the attended region -- masking works for the uncertainty type and is useless for the prior type by construction. Two measurement lessons recur. A hallucination number must be read with coverage: one system's 28.5-point reduction in its proxy costs 9.6 points of coverage, and its reflection-depth sweep is monotone in opposite directions on the two axes, which the authors use to justify keeping reflection shallow rather than to claim a best setting. And a mitigation that reduces mentions may be suppressing output rather than grounding it, which is why the stronger sources report word count, correct-object coverage and perplexity alongside the reduction.
meta-evaluation10Evaluating the evaluation — asking whether a benchmark, metric or judge measures what it claims. Ten sources practise it, and the recurring result is that the validation layer is weaker than the thing it validates. Exact-match agreement with human labels, the standard way to certify an LLM judge, is shown insufficient over roughly 541,000 judgments; forcing annotators to pick one answer on tasks admitting several defensible readings biases that validation badly; and judge preferences track style rather than the properties they are supposed to measure. Benchmarks fare no better under the same scrutiny: decomposing a task by cognitive dimension, ablating the modality the task supposedly requires, or scoring intermediate artefacts separately each reveal that the headline number was carrying something else. The archive also holds a call to apply this to interpretability itself, after two papers reached opposite conclusions on one behaviour and a third found both partly right and incomparable.
effective depth9How much of a network's depth a computation actually uses, and across 9 sources a quantity that appears in three different literatures with the same shape. Theoretically it is the thing chain of thought buys: a bounded-depth transformer is limited by its layers in one forward pass, and emitting intermediate tokens converts sequence length into effective depth, which is why the number of steps maps onto complexity classes. Empirically it appears as depth-localisation -- grounding detection climbing through early layers, peaking mid-network and falling toward the output; the useful read-out band being narrow and mid-network; and reasoning-relevant signals concentrated in particular layers rather than spread. And as a mechanism it appears in the counting work, where latent counts are stored, transferred through dedicated heads and aggregated at the final stage, which is a depth-structured computation rather than a distributed one. The archive's practical consequence is that pooling across depth dilutes rather than aggregates, and that a claim about where a computation lives needs the layer sweep rather than a single site.
monosemanticity9Whether a unit responds to one interpretable thing or to several unrelated ones -- the property a feature decomposition is trying to recover and the property individual neurons lack. Across 9 sources it functions as the motivating premise for dictionary learning: reverse engineering requires units analysable in isolation, neurons activate across semantically unrelated contexts, and for the residual stream there is no reason to expect features to align with the neuron basis at all. The archive's evidence about how well it is achieved is mixed and quantitative: automated interpretability scores for sparse coding run about 0.30 at the first layer and decline to 0.15 by the fifth, against 0.14 for an independent-components baseline and 0.06 to 0.08 for random directions -- so the improvement over chance is real and shrinks with depth. The standing caution is that a monosemantic-looking feature is a property of a particular dictionary rather than of the model, since feature splitting and absorption move the boundaries between features as the dictionary changes.
memorization7Reproducing training content rather than computing an answer, and across 7 sources a hypothesis the archive tests rather than assumes. Its cleanest separation is the loose-versus-strict metric under symbolic variabilisation: a base mathematics model loses 38.5 percent even under partial credit, consistent with heavy reliance on memorised patterns, while reinforcement-trained models lose 2.6 to 7.2 -- so the two metrics together separate memorisation from mere inconsistency. A mechanistic source rules it out for arithmetic specifically, finding neither a learned algorithm nor memorisation but an unordered collection of sparse heuristic neurons voting. And a benchmark source finds the opposite asymmetry for proverbs, where completion is easy and choice is hard. The archive's related material covers the unlearning side and the contamination-detection side, where the standing result is that post-training suppresses the signals memorisation detectors rely on.
reproducibility7Whether a reported result can be obtained again, and across 7 sources a property the archive treats as scarce enough to be worth measuring. Its instances divide by what fails to reproduce. Method-level: an independent reproduction matches a published grid to two decimal places and then finds the advantage vanishing on every new domain, with no per-item difference reaching significance across four of them. Instrument-level: if two runs on the same activations give different dictionaries, a claim about a feature is a claim about a run -- which is the sparse-autoencoder identifiability problem stated as a reproducibility one. Protocol-level: measured performance depends on template, decoder, controller, reducer, verifier, stopping rule and numerical settings, so accuracy reported without the inference protocol is not reproducible in principle; one source prescribes declaring budget, temperature, evidence map, stopping and extraction rules before outcomes are inspected. Numerical-level: one source studies sources of nondeterminism in inference itself. The archive's practice, where sources model it well, is byte-identical prompts verified by hash, fixed seeds, and pre-registration of thresholds before the confirmatory analysis.
benchmark design6How an evaluation is constructed, and across 6 sources a subject the archive treats as consequential rather than clerical. Three design decisions recur with measured consequences. Generating answers by construction rather than by labelling removes a model from the loop entirely -- one benchmark builds items from executable computation trees over real tables so answers are correct by construction, and exposes four independently controllable difficulty axes, which is what lets it show unit and scale perturbations collapsing a frontier model from 98.4 to 3.0 percent. Deciding what counts as the same problem is a judgement that then defines the result: a symbolic variabilisation is hand-built by experts, so the feasible domains encode a view about identity, and a loose metric granting partial credit narrows the reported drops by an order of magnitude while separating memorisation from inconsistency. And model-mediated construction is pervasive and worth stating -- one benchmark's candidates are filtered, reviewed and audited by models throughout, so what it calls a natural request means what those models judged to be one, with human involvement confined to adjudicating disagreements. The archive's oldest source in this set is the one that introduced a benchmark and its verifier together, which remains the pattern the corpus rewards.
implicit reasoning6Multi-step inference carried inside the forward pass or in continuous latent states, without emitting the intermediate steps as tokens. The six sources agree it is real and disagree about what it costs. Mechanistically, transformers acquire it only through grokking — training far past overfitting — and the resulting circuit generalizes out of distribution for comparison but not for composition; at scale a sparse modular circuit is recoverable for propositional logic. Practically, latent variants switch to soft embeddings at low-confidence steps or add an abstract latent as a pretraining target, and both have to fight the tendency of soft embeddings to collapse toward the top token. Theoretically, one entry proves internalization need not cost sample efficiency: a curriculum deleting thinking tokens in geometric chunks learns k-parity with polynomially many samples and only logarithmically many stages — though the layers required grow with the chain being absorbed, so inference compute is relocated into the architecture rather than removed. On monitorability the archive's finding is that what a monitor can catch depends more on the task and on access to internals than on whether the reasoning was written down at all.
jailbreak6A prompt that induces a model to produce output its alignment training was meant to prevent, and across 6 sources studied here mainly through what defences transfer. The archive's clearest result is that pattern-keyed alignment does not: a model trained on refusals learns which inputs look dangerous rather than what makes them dangerous, and one defence leaves gradient-optimised suffix attacks at 29.50 percent against a base rate of 29.75 -- no effect at all -- while defences supervised on the mechanism of an attack reach 1.25. The two ablations that carry that argument are worth keeping: seeds that actually defeated the teacher are worth about 14.2 points over raw harmful prompts with everything else fixed, and pairing the same successful jailbreaks with a refusal rather than an analysis moves average attack success by 4 points against the analysis's 60. Two cautions. Evaluation is judge-mediated throughout, with one paper's own audit finding two judges disagreeing on 42 percent of the class that determines the score. And a defence that reduces success without closing it should be read as raising cost -- one agentic attack still reaches 18.00 percent while needing nearly twice as many turns.
linear representation hypothesis6The claim that concepts are accessible as directions in activation space, so that separability implies a usable axis. Across 6 sources it is the assumption underneath probing and steering alike, and the archive's material is largely about where it fails to license what it is used for. Its steering form is the strong one -- if texts exhibiting a concept are linearly separable from those that do not, translating toward the positive half-space should promote the concept -- and that is exactly what the inverted-vector result falsifies, since directions with high separability can move the concept backwards. Its probing form survives better: a great deal is linearly decodable, often earlier in the network than the model's own output states it. The corpus's operative distinction is therefore that linear accessibility is well supported for reading and not for writing, and one source notes that applied work continues to build measures on outputs or embeddings even where the internal direction is available.
mechanistic interpretability6Explaining a model's behaviour in terms of its internal components, and across 6 sources a field the archive reads with its methodological problems foregrounded. Three appear repeatedly. Comparability: the literature has no convention for corruption methods or evaluation metrics, so results from different papers are frequently not measuring the same thing. Identifiability: where units come from a learned dictionary, different seeds and widths recover different features, so a claim about a feature is partly a claim about a run -- and one source argues the identifiable object and the human-legible object cannot be the same object. Auditability: one source calls for guidelines developed by continuous collaborative reviewing, which is the field-level version of the archive's own practice of asking for nulls and controls. The corpus's positive contributions are tooling and surveys -- a library bringing patching and attention analysis to vision-language models, and a survey organising training, inference and failures for reasoning models.
representation versus readout6The gap between what a model's internal states contain and what its outputs express, and across 6 sources one of the archive's most reproducible findings. Its canonical form is a probe recovering a property well above what the model itself says -- one cross-model study finds cultural knowledge represented in the residual stream while the outputs do not decode it, and a theory-of-mind study finds probes at 77 to 82 percent against the same models' own chain-of-thought outputs at 62 to 70. The consequence the archive draws is diagnostic rather than optimistic: a failure to express is a readout failure, which more data cannot fix and a decoding or steering intervention might, whereas a failure to represent needs different training -- and the two predict opposite remedies, which is why the distinction is worth naming. Two adjacent results bound it. Readout can be sufficient without being necessary: a direction whose removal from either module fails to change generation is decodable and not load-bearing. And a decoder built to make a latent computation inspectable can be anchored far more by the source text than by the latent states it is supposed to inspect, so an interpretable readout is not evidence that the readout reflects the computation.
training dynamics6How quantities move over the course of training rather than where they end, and across 6 sources the view that repeatedly separates effects a final-checkpoint table conflates. The archive's instances: entropy consumption and performance gain are both front-loaded, with 73 and 76 percent in the first 200 of 2,400 steps; pass@1 and large-k coverage move in opposite directions across the same steps, and in one distillation study the large-k degradation appears by around step 80 and fluctuates while the small-k gain accrues stably; the exact-tie rate rises across epochs as the reward saturates; and per-task reward trajectories across 20 epochs sort into six profiles including forgetting and stubbornly-unlearned, which a final-accuracy number cannot show. One theoretical source in the corpus proves a training-dynamics result rather than measuring one, on how a transformer comes to learn a chain-of-thought procedure with length generalisation. The operative point for reading the archive is that a method compared at a fixed step count and a method compared at its best checkpoint are answering different questions, and that a degradation appearing before convergence is evidence about the objective rather than about overtraining.
uncertainty quantification6Estimating how much a model's answer should be trusted, and across 6 sources a family whose members disagree about what to compute it from. The corpus's options are token-probability statistics, entropy over sampled answers, internal-state geometry, and explicit verbalised confidence -- and its findings are mostly about their failure to interchange. Statistics computed coordinate-wise from hidden states give calibration error of 0.1216 and 0.0858 in two prompt orderings against 0.2451 and 0.1764 for a trained last-token head, and both are competitive using only the first 12 of 28 layers. Verbalised confidence and answer frequency both fail in multimodal settings for the same reason -- neither tests whether an answer depends on the image, so a grounded answer and a confident language-prior guess look identical. And the archive's sharpest result bounds all of them on hard problems: in the highest-agreement bin the plurality is correct about half the time for one model and less often than its own lowest bin for another, so no verifier-free statistic derived from the model's own samples carries the information a gate needs. One source turns a latent estimate into an actionable critique by naming the offending parameter rather than emitting a scalar, which is the direction the corpus suggests when the number itself is untrustworthy.
circuit complexity5The complexity-class characterisation of what a transformer can compute, and across 5 sources the formal backbone of the archive's chain-of-thought material. The established results: a single forward pass of a bounded-depth transformer sits in a low parallel class, generating intermediate tokens lifts that ceiling, and the number of steps maps onto classes -- a linear number of steps reaching NC1, with polynomially many steps reaching further. One source gives an exact fixed-step characterisation in terms of a rank measure of the function; another shows chain of thought lets transformers solve inherently serial problems; a third supplies a concrete witness by constructing graph traversal and a linear-step branching measure in two layers without layer normalisation. Two boundaries the archive should keep. These are representational results and say nothing about what gradient descent finds -- one source in the set proves a learning result instead, on how a transformer comes to learn a chain-of-thought procedure with length generalisation. And they are usually proved for hard attention, an idealisation adopted because softmax reintroduces difficulties known from sigmoidal networks.
degenerate generation5Output that satisfies a training or selection criterion while being useless -- repetition to the token limit, unterminated generation, loops that maximise agreement. Across 5 sources it is the concrete form the archive's local-metric warning takes. Its instances: a student reaching near-perfect token agreement with a teacher by looping while the response as a whole is broken; a reverse-KL objective collapsing by progressive loss of termination, with generations growing from 2.7 thousand characters to the token ceiling and parsable answers falling from 99 to 33 percent; a policy emitting repetitive tokens to an 8192-token limit under an unconstrained length reward; and a model whose most active reasoning features are suppressed responding by generating 454 percent more tokens of lower-diversity text rather than stopping. Two methodological consequences. Degenerate rollouts must be counted separately from incorrect ones, which one exploration metric does explicitly, because entropy and pass@k cannot distinguish exploration from degeneration. And a terminal check on the whole output is what separates degeneration from success, since every local metric in these cases was satisfied.
distribution shift5A difference between the distribution a model was trained on and the one it meets, used across 5 sources in two distinct senses the archive keeps apart. The training sense is the train-inference mismatch that on-policy methods exist to remove: learning from a teacher corpus leaves the student unprepared for its own prefixes, and supervised self-correction suffers it in a specific way, biasing the model toward following correction templates rather than selectively revising real errors. The evaluation sense is what a deployed model meets, where the archive's material is about detecting the shift rather than surviving it -- guarded captions retaining 95.4 percent of vanilla word count with only a small distribution shift is how one source rules out suppression as the mechanism for its improvement. One further instance from the corpus: flow-based methods that swap a deterministic sampler for a stochastic one during training optimise samples the test-time sampler does not produce, which is the same problem stated in continuous space.
generalization5Whether what a model learned in one setting holds outside it. With five sources the archive now carries both the behavioural claims and the theory that prices them. Behaviourally it records a recurring gap: a reasoning-structure intervention trained on 1K examples transfers across tasks, while arithmetic reverse-engineered to the neuron turns out to be neither an algorithm nor memorization but a bag of sparse heuristics — so benchmark transfer is evidence about coverage rather than about an algorithm having been learned. Three theoretical entries bound the question rather than illustrate it. A PAC-Bayes analysis on boolean functions makes the generalization gap scale with Fourier sparsity times squared degree, and shows chain of thought turning an exponential dependence on reasoning length into a linear one. A VC-dimension result prices chain-of-thought supervision at depth times parameters times a logarithm, with input length and reasoning length entering only inside that logarithm and only through their sum. And a negative result proves that for transformers of depth two or beyond no computable bound exists on how long the training inputs must be. Together they say the behavioural failures are not merely unlucky: in the general case no procedure can tell you in advance.
grounding5Whether an output rests on the evidence it cites or claims to use, and across 5 sources a property the archive insists be verified causally rather than by construction. The standard the corpus sets: masking the evidence a system cites flips 82.8 percent of correct answers while masking uncited evidence changes 9.6 -- a claim about grounding that a rule-based integrity check, near-saturated at 99.5 and 99.6, cannot make. Two measurement findings recur. Grounding is depth-localised: detection climbs through early layers, peaks mid-network and falls toward the output where representations specialise for next-token prediction, with the same shape on every backbone, so pooling across depth dilutes rather than aggregates. And explicit intermediate grounding is worth measuring against implicit attention -- one system finds pairing every reasoning step with a predicted image region worth 8.78 points over letting the model attend implicitly. The caveat one source states about its own work applies generally: a detector trained supervised on hallucination labels is a learned classifier over internal states, so a layer sweep is suggestive about where grounding lives without being an interpretability result about how it is represented.
interpretability illusion5A finding that looks like a discovery about a model and is a property of the measurement, and across 5 sources the archive's most consistently supported methodological result. Its documented forms. Uncalibrated units: an apparent emergence of concept steerability with scale is produced entirely by raw intervention units, the readout metric and a fixed operating point, with correcting any one of the three removing the trend. Recipe dependence: a published internal signal for code correctness has no configuration that is best anywhere, with the benchmark deciding which extraction choice wins and a mismatched fitting source driving the signal below chance. Missing nulls: a decodable logic direction is matched exactly under steering by a same-norm random direction, so the reported effect is magnitude rather than content. Quantity confusion: patching and weight-space ablation measure different things that neither bounds, so a result from one does not transfer to the other. And identifiability: the object a method recovers may be determined by the procedure rather than by the model. One source in the set withdraws its own clean empirical separation when it fails out of sample, which the archive treats as the model behaviour for this category.
adversarial robustness4Whether a capability or a defence survives an input chosen to defeat it, and across 4 sources measured in a way that repeatedly separates methods that look equivalent. The archive's instances: two intent classifiers matched on accuracy diverge entirely under adversarial rephrasing, where statistical summaries survive and trained heads collapse to zero -- so the trade is not accuracy but where each fails; unit and scale perturbations on a financial benchmark collapse the strongest model from 98.4 to 3.0 percent; and a self-reflection safety mechanism holds under a specially designed adaptive attack without targeted adversarial training. One image-safety method makes the cost structure explicit by triggering only from an intermediate estimate where a violation appears, so the extra compute lands on unsafe inputs while benign latency stays flat. The corpus's standing point is that robustness is a property of the pairing between a system and an attack class, and that a defence untested against an attack with a different structure -- gradient-optimised suffixes rather than semantic jailbreaks -- has not been shown to transfer.

GRPO62Sampling a group of rollouts per prompt, standardising their rewards within the group to form the advantage, and optimising a PPO-style clipped surrogate with a KL penalty -- so no value network is learned. Across 62 sources it is less a method than the substrate everything else is built on ('the implementation is a few lines on top of GRPO'), and its properties are consequences of the group normalisation rather than of the reward. Four are documented repeatedly. Prompt weighting: for binary rewards the induced weight is one over the square root of p(1-p), which diverges at both ends, and the realised consequence has been measured -- 36.4 percent of one benchmark's token-level gradient budget spent on prompts already above 0.9 pass rate. Silent groups: a prompt whose rollouts all agree has zero advantage for every member and contributes nothing, reported at 63 to 68 percent of training groups in one study and rising across epochs in another as the reward saturates. Relative spacing only: what reaches the optimiser is the ordering and gaps of rewards within the group, so a linear rescaling is cancelled while a bounded nonlinear one is not, an unbounded reward lets one outlier inflate the group standard deviation and flatten every near-optimal sibling, and a difficulty-dependent reward multiplier interacts badly with the normalisation -- weakening a token penalty precisely where outcome variance is highest. Entropy: policy entropy falls from about 0.25 to near zero within roughly 50 steps, with the highest training reward coinciding with the lowest entropy, which several sources read as premature exploitation; a large family of clipping and covariance variants exists to slow it, and one source finds switching from approximate to strict on-policy updating worth 4.6 and 5.3 points, more than the entropy method layered on top. On what it does to reasoning the sources split. One gives a theoretical account in which the gradient raises the probability of correct chains even though the reward sees only the answer, once pretraining supplies priors separating them; another finds accuracy improving without the traces becoming more semantically valid, which it calls unsurprising for an outcome reward. Two failure modes recur: on subjective judgement tasks mean completion length collapses from about 300 tokens to 170 after roughly 70 steps because guessing the label earns the same reward more cheaply, and pass@1 rises across training steps while coverage at pass@256 falls, so the reachable set narrows as reliability improves. A brief run of it also suppresses the signals benchmark-contamination detectors rely on, which one source attributes to PPO-style importance sampling and clipping rather than to anything specific here. Practical cautions from the corpus: it is dominated in cost by rollout generation, and adaptive allocators beat full-group training substantially under a matched token budget; against PPO under an identical reward it wins 81.2 to 72.6 where correctness is localised to a few tokens, which the authors attribute to group-relative rather than batch-level advantage normalisation; and it is frequently an under-tuned baseline, gaining under 4 points from best-checkpoint selection where a supervised baseline gains 27, and in one case scoring below its own zero-shot starting point on a benchmark. Reported margins over it are commonly 0.75 to 2.6 points with no seeds or variance.
supervised fine-tuning54Maximising the likelihood of curated target sequences -- usually a reasoning trace followed by an answer -- and across 54 sources the stage whose role is most often asserted and least often measured. Its dominant use here is as a cold start that installs a format before reinforcement learning, and where that has been ablated the effect is categorical rather than incremental: a tool-using policy trained by reinforcement learning alone cannot reliably emit parsable tool calls at all (50.6 percent format compliance against 100), while supervised fine-tuning alone reaches 99.8 percent and comes within 2 to 3.6 points of the full pipeline. The sources disagree sharply about whether it is needed. Several find it indispensable and then surpassed -- it beats the base model and loses to every reinforcement-learning method applied over it, or plateaus at 89.74 percent where the full pipeline reaches higher. Others find the opposite: reinforcement learning without any cold start is the best configuration on closed-ended medical questions on all three benchmarks by 8 to 20 points while losing every open-ended split, one hybrid-reasoning method removes the stage deliberately as computationally costly, and one from-scratch study finds group-relative reinforcement learning losing to supervised fine-tuning on two of three mathematics benchmarks (27.37 against 36.69 Pass@8 on one) with the combination beating both by 31 to 175 percent. Two systematic reasons the comparison keeps coming out differently are documented. It is routinely under-tuned as a baseline: giving each method its best validation checkpoint rather than a fixed epoch budget is worth 27 points of end-to-end accuracy to supervised fine-tuning and under 4 to reinforcement learning, which reverses the reported ordering. And the comparison usually confounds the training method with the trace source, since the supervised arm typically learns from a larger distilled teacher while the reinforcement-learning arm has none. On what the traces themselves buy, two controlled studies agree that the effect is out of distribution rather than in it: rationale supervision buys nothing in-domain for strong backbones and 20 to 28 points out of domain, and domain-specific chain-of-thought supervision lifts one 9B model from 37.4 to 89.0 percent where answer-only supervision reaches 75.1. Quantities are small throughout -- 1,000 curated traces for test-time scaling, 1,000 examples for a safety-alignment structure change, 800 to install or destroy a habit of verbalising uncertainty (suppressing it halves pass@1 on five models despite every training trace being correct). Two sources note where the stage is dangerous rather than useful: a triggered backdoor whose visible reasoning stays clean and correct is installed this way, and benchmark contamination introduced here is laundered by a brief reinforcement-learning run afterwards, so a pipeline ending in reinforcement learning conceals what its supervised stage absorbed. One source places more alignment weight on this stage than the preference-optimisation literature assumes, identifying data scaling and prompt diversity as the drivers.
LLM-as-a-judge49Using a language model to score another model's output, and across 49 sources the archive's most pervasive dependency -- it appears as an evaluation metric, a training reward, a data filter, a contamination detector and an annotator of the archive's own probing labels. The corpus is largely a catalogue of what it gets wrong. Validity: judge preference scores do not correlate with concrete measures of safety, world knowledge or instruction following, because judges systematically prioritise style over factuality; judges also produce characteristic scores regardless of the text evaluated. Reliability without validity: one large-scale evaluation finds generational progress legible on one judging benchmark (0.309, 0.487, 0.606) and nearly invisible on another (0.451, 0.451, 0.457). Instrument dependence: a standard prompt turns out to be specialised to the model it was authored against, with every other model including that model's own newer sibling performing significantly worse under it, so a prompt is not a neutral instrument carried between judges. Disagreement concentrates where it matters: an independent audit of 400 responses finds 90.25 percent overall agreement between two judges and a 41.94 percent false-negative rate on the small subset the reference judge calls unsafe -- exactly the class that determines the reported score. As a reward it is expensive and fragile: one system issues a judge call per rubric per rollout per group per step, another finds a judge-based alternative costing 62 percent of per-step wall clock against 6 percent for a structural rule, and a third falls below its plain baseline when the judge is weakened. Two structural cautions recur across papers rather than within them. A judge drawn from the same family as one of the systems it scores is not an independent instrument, which several sources note about their own evaluations. And validating a judge against human annotation performed under the judge's own criteria does not establish independence -- one paper reports 95.6 percent agreement and says so explicitly. Where the corpus is positive it is about ensembling and validation rather than about judges as such: a jury of different models beats any member, and the practice several sources model is to report agreement, kappa and a hand inspection of disagreements before concluding anything from the labels.
linear probe32Fitting a linear classifier on hidden states to test whether a property is linearly decodable, and across 32 sources the archive's most-used and least-standardised instrument. What it reliably shows is that a great deal is decodable and often earlier than the model says it: probes recover 77 to 82 percent on a theory-of-mind task against the same models' own chain-of-thought outputs at 62 to 70, with the answer available already in middle layers; probes detect sycophantic anchors at 74 to 85 percent balanced accuracy above text-only baselines. Four cautions recur and are the reason the archive treats a probe result as a lower bound on a claim rather than the claim. Probes learn what is easiest: activations are saturated with polysemantic features, so a probe can succeed for surface lexical reasons rather than for the structure it was meant to find, which is why null and permutation controls appear in the better sources. Extraction choices dominate: no configuration of read-out location, pooling and framing is best across benchmarks, and a pooled variance decomposition can rank a factor below one percent because its opposite effects on two benchmarks cancel. Decoding licenses nothing about intervening: the difference-of-means family used for steering is sometimes the weakest of four classifiers on the same data, and one source declines to pick its intervention by classification accuracy for exactly that reason. And the margin over the obvious simpler control is often small -- on three of six benchmarks in one study the advantage of trajectory geometry over an ordinary probe on the final hidden state is within a few thousandths of AUROC, so a general claim rests on two benchmarks. Sources also note the linear restriction itself makes their reported correlations lower bounds.
chain-of-thought prompting31Eliciting step-by-step intermediate tokens from a model that would otherwise answer directly, usually by few-shot examples drawn from the dataset -- which across 31 sources remains the standard baseline in official model releases and in nearly every comparison the archive holds. Its status here is almost entirely that of a reference point rather than a subject: methods report 6.6 points over it averaged across 30 settings, or 74 percent against its 4 percent on a search-structured puzzle, and it is the arm every latent-reasoning, self-consistency and structured-prompting paper must beat. Two qualifications recur across the corpus. The prompt is an experimental variable rather than a fixed point, so a comparison against it measures the template as much as the method -- one source holds the template constant across arms precisely so that only the diversity source differs. And what it improves is the answer, not necessarily the reasoning: the archive's faithfulness sources find extrinsic prompting and supplied cues both helping while neither is internalised, leaving reasoning that proceeds disconnected from the evidence it claims to rest on. One source in the corpus explains its accuracy effect mechanistically; the others use it without one.
self-consistency28Sampling several chains and returning the answer they mostly agree on, and across 28 sources the baseline every test-time-scaling method must beat, the aggregation rule most of them replace, and increasingly the method the archive can say is unsafe on hard inputs. Three lines of evidence. It is beaten by reweighting rather than by more sampling: entropy-, instability- and geometry-based weightings all report margins that grow with the sample count rather than closing, and the mechanism offered is that incorrect trajectories converge to their wrong answer earlier than correct ones converge to the right one, so counting votes rewards the failure mode. It is not monotone: scaling it can amplify spurious consensus, with 64 rollouts reaching 34.42 against 34.66 at 16 in one label-free training setting, and on a hard science benchmark it lowers per-problem accuracy on 56.6 and 65.7 percent of problems for two small models while the aggregate barely moves. And it is a strong control that absorbs many proposed alternatives -- one study finds confidence-weighted variants tracking a matched control within noise, and a reproduction finds a published improvement never significantly beating it across four new domains, gaining 2.5 points at a larger budget on a set where only 2 of 80 items separate them, and losing the advantage entirely when the evaluation set is enlarged to 200. The precondition the corpus identifies is calibration: agreement must track correctness, and where the highest-agreement bin is right about half the time -- or less often than the lowest bin -- no verifier-free reweighting can repair it.
RLVR27Reinforcement learning against an automatic correctness check -- a test suite, an exact-match verifier, a deterministic evaluator -- and across 27 sources the dominant post-training method here and the one the archive has argued about most. The central dispute is whether it expands what a model can reach or only sharpens what it already could, and the corpus's contribution is that both are true of different sets: branch-preference entropy of a trained policy is below its distilled counterpart on 95.5 to 100 percent of sampled branches across four model families, with the collapse stronger for semantically distinct continuations than for syntactic variants, while backtracking probability at dead ends and recovery from injected distractors both rise sharply. The synthesis one source offers is that it buys sample efficiency by learning what is invalid and pays for it by pruning what is valid but different. Two scope conditions recur. It works where the pretraining corpus is dense in step-by-step derivations and a checker is clean; outside that -- subjective judgement, open-ended generation -- the same objective produces degeneration, with completion length collapsing because guessing earns the same reward more cheaply. And gains are brittle to how a question is worded: paraphrase and template change degrade them, traced to a binary verifier that cannot separate a wrong answer from a misformatted one. Its label-free variants replace the gold answer with the majority answer among rollouts, which inherits the failure modes of consensus. One further consequence the archive records: a brief run of it suppresses the signals benchmark-contamination detectors rely on.
activation steering21Adding a direction to a model's activations at inference to change its behaviour, and across 21 sources an instrument whose defaults do most of the damage attributed to the technique. Three defaults are named. The direction: choosing it by discriminability -- what the foundational method does -- can install the opposite of the intended effect, since vectors combining high separability with a strictly negative dose-response relationship are minable systematically. The site: injection layers are fixed globally per behaviour with which and how many treated as configuration, and one source shows the useful band is narrow, mid-network, and different for each model and each benchmark. The units: an apparent emergence of steerability with scale is produced entirely by raw activation units, an uncalibrated read-out metric and a fixed hand-picked operating point, and a factorial decomposition finds no significant trend in any of four cells. What the technique does establish, where controls are run, is causal direction rather than magnitude -- reducing steering strength consistently reduces reasoning token count and accuracy while leaving answer length unaffected, which is the shape of evidence the archive treats as usable. Two standing requirements follow from the corpus: report a matched null of comparable magnitude, since steering is an off-distribution intervention at a conventionally chosen strength and a degradation under it can be damage rather than mechanism; and do not read a probe's accuracy as a prediction about what steering along the same direction will do.
activation patching19Replacing an activation at a chosen site with the activation a different input produced, and measuring what the output does -- the standard localisation tool in mechanistic interpretability. Across 19 sources its recurring problem is not the idea but the absence of a convention: the literature contains many variants with no consensus on hyperparameters, each paper using its own corruption method and its own evaluation metric, so two patching results are frequently not comparable. One theoretical source sharpens this into a claim about what is measured: patching and weight-space ablation measure two different quantities -- a carrier's donor-receiver contrast versus its absolute level at the receiver -- and neither bounds the other, with an exact criterion for when ablating a subset collapses a conditional onto one branch. The archive's practical requirements follow. A patch needs a control pair rather than a single corruption, since a change under an arbitrary substitute shows that something moved. And where a compact subspace suffices -- replacing the whole residual stream at a site having a comparable effect to a low-rank patch -- that is the informative result, because it bounds where the causal information sits rather than merely confirming it is somewhere upstream.
knowledge distillation15Training a student on a teacher's outputs or distribution, and across 15 sources a method whose success is governed less by the objective than by three properties of the pairing. Capacity floor: the same jury distillation that lifts a 12B student above every open reasoning-model judge lowers a 4B one (system-level 86.50 to 82.48), a limit the source scopes around rather than states. Idiom match: a safety-alignment pipeline gains across one model family and loses utility across another at small scale, which the authors attribute to a reasoning-style mismatch with the teacher's family, and a language-specific 9B model gains least from further rationale distillation while the weakest general backbone gains most. Mismatch regime: one method's advantage over standard on-policy distillation is 1.0 point where the student is close to the teacher -- inside the spread of three other baselines -- and 13.4 points where it is far below, and the strong-mismatch baseline is a model emitting 22K-token unterminated responses, so much of what is demonstrated is repair of breakage rather than transfer of reasoning. Two objective-level findings recur. Token agreement is not the goal: a student can drive agreement to near-perfect by looping while the response is globally broken, so the metric is satisfiable by degeneration. And preferences established under gold labels understate themselves under noisy ones -- full-vocabulary divergence beats sampled-token distillation by 17.8 and 7.2 percent under pseudo-labels against 2.0 and 2.7 under gold, while truncating to the teacher's top-100 entries matches the full vocabulary and is therefore free. The corpus's standing caution is that a distilled result is a statement about one teacher: several sources note their rationales, filters and quality audits all come from proprietary models, so what rationale supervision buys is what that teacher's rationales buy.
sparse autoencoder15Learning an overcomplete dictionary on model activations under a sparsity penalty, so that a polysemantic activation vector is decomposed into features that admit individual interpretation. Across 15 sources it is the dominant tool for this and the one the archive holds the most evidence against as a source of stable facts. The core problem is non-identifiability, and it is empirical before it is theoretical: different training seeds and dictionary widths recover materially different features from the same activations, dictionaries absorb and split features, and residual-stream components remain uncaptured by any known variant -- so two studies using different dictionaries are not measuring the same object. The theoretical companion in the corpus argues the failure is structural rather than algorithmic, since a reconstruction-and-sparsity objective does not enforce the invariance identifiability would require. Quality is also lossy and depth-dependent: automated interpretability scores decline from about 0.30 at layer 0 to 0.15 by layer 5, against about 0.14 for an independent-components baseline and 0.06 to 0.08 for random directions, and substituting reconstructed activations back raises perplexity from 25 to 40. Two scope limits recur: it must be trained per layer per model, which makes it costly and model-specific rather than endogenous, and it operates at the token level, which mismatches the unit reasoning happens in. The archive's practical position is that a sparse-autoencoder feature is a hypothesis about a direction rather than a measurement of one, and that any claim built on a specific dictionary should state the dictionary and, ideally, be checked against a second.
LoRA14Training low-rank adapter matrices while the base weights stay frozen, used across 14 sources as the default cheap fine-tuning method and, in several, as the thing that bounds what a study establishes. Configurations in the corpus cluster tightly -- ranks 8 to 256, alpha typically twice the rank, dropout 0.05, one to two epochs -- and one source's own sweep peaks at rank 128 while it reports rank 256, attributed to the sweep's smaller training set. Two findings are worth carrying. It is not always worth its cost: in one forecasting system it and full fine-tuning cost 8.21 and 15.21 times the training time to move the headline error by at most 0.001, which is the clearest instance in the archive of a backbone that is close to inert for the task. And several sources name it as a scope limit on their own conclusions -- training on 1,000 problems for one epoch on a single GPU bounds what a comparison establishes about full fine-tuning at scale, and one contamination study notes its clean reference models are themselves adapters at one hyperparameter setting. It is also the mechanism by which several archived methods keep a frozen model intact while adding a capability alongside it, restructuring the residual stream through an adapter rather than editing the model.
PPO14Policy optimisation with a clipped surrogate objective, a learned value function and a KL penalty -- the generation before the critic-free group-relative methods that dominate this archive, and across 14 sources still the source of the machinery those methods keep. The corpus's most useful content concerns its critic. Scalar regression is statistically consistent for exactly the quantity needed, since with binary terminal rewards the return from an intermediate state is Bernoulli and its conditional mean is the probability of eventual success -- one source is explicit that scalar critics are not representationally invalid. The failure is calibration rather than representation: because the critic enters the actor update as a subtraction, a modest overestimate gives failed rollouts large negative advantages and successes small positive ones, measured at a 2.63 ratio and growing more skewed over training, so the update spends most of its magnitude on failures. A distributional critic brings that ratio to 1.02 and recovers pass@256 from 38.48 to 48.06 -- and the archive should note the baseline number, since 38.48 sits below the untrained base model's 40.74, so unmodified training narrowed the boundary. Two comparative findings: against group-relative optimisation under an identical reward it loses 72.6 to 81.2 where correctness is localised to a few tokens, attributed to batch-level rather than group-relative advantage normalisation, and token-level reward weighting that helps the group-relative method severely destabilises it. Its clipping and importance sampling are also the mechanism one source blames for post-training concealing benchmark contamination.
causal intervention13Changing something inside a model and measuring what the output does, as opposed to reading a correlation off its states -- across 13 sources the standard the archive holds interpretability claims to, and the standard most of them are shown to miss. Two structural results bound what an intervention establishes. Different interventions measure different quantities: activation patching measures a carrier's donor-receiver contrast while weight-space ablation measures its absolute level at the receiver, and neither bounds the other, so a result under one does not transfer. And a strong intervention can produce a strong effect for the wrong reason: suppressing a top feature and observing degradation shows damage unless a matched null of comparable magnitude is reported, and a decodable direction can be matched exactly under steering by a same-norm random direction. The corpus's positive instances share a shape -- the intervention comes with a control that should not produce the effect. Masking the evidence a system cites flips 82.8 percent of correct answers while masking uncited evidence changes 9.6. A scratchpad edit is checked against a move-specific gap of +0.57 and +0.68 so that the model is shown to combine the edited state with the supplied move rather than emit a fixed answer. And a steering result is reported as inverted precisely because the intervention's sign was measured rather than assumed.
steering vector13A single direction in activation space, computed from a small contrastive dataset and added at inference with no retraining -- cheap, which is why it is adopted, and unreliable in ways the 13 sources here document specifically. It rests on the linear representation hypothesis: if texts exhibiting a concept are linearly separable from those that do not, translating toward the positive half-space should promote it. The archive's strongest result is that this fails for the sign, not merely for the magnitude: inverted vectors combining high discriminability with a strictly negative relationship between steering strength and concept score are mined systematically, three per model-concept pair across 15 pairs, one reaching AUC 0.97 with Spearman -1 -- so choosing a direction by how well it discriminates, which the foundational method does, can install the opposite of the intended effect. A second failure is collateral: the component that damages refusal behaviour is a separate direction from the one producing the intended effect, cosine similarity with the canonical refusal direction predicts the damage, and yet ablating that direction fails to restore safety because refusal is mediated by several mechanistically independent directions and the canonical one need not be the one a given vector is harming. Scope caveats from the sources: the inverted-vector evidence covers translation-based steering with vectors mined from attention-head outputs on three models and five concepts, so affine, non-linear and optimised pipelines and other steering sites are untested; and steering is an off-distribution intervention at a conventionally chosen magnitude, so a degradation observed under it may be damage rather than mechanism unless a matched null is reported.
component ablation12Removing one part of a method at a time and reporting what each removal costs, and across 12 sources the archive's default evidence that a design is more than the sum of arbitrary choices. What the corpus establishes is mostly how to read one. Removals frequently improve some benchmark while degrading others -- one source reports every single component ablation gaining on at least one of three sets, with removing a prioritisation term gaining 0.4 on one and losing 1.8 and 2.0 on the others -- so the honest conclusion is that the full configuration is best on average rather than everywhere, which those authors state and others do not. Sequential ablation reports ordered marginals, not interaction-free contributions, since a different addition order gives different numbers; one source says so explicitly while presenting a monotone sequence. And a removal that hurts establishes that a component carries information, not that the method uses the property it was designed around -- for that the archive's standing requirement is a control that preserves the component and breaks only its alignment, or a random alternative of matched cost. The strongest instances in the corpus are those where the ablation isolates a mechanism rather than a number: restricting a method to the subpopulation it claims to act on and recovering 84.7 percent of the gain, or removing two control actions separately and finding both load-bearing and not interchangeable (15.31 and 13.06 average F1).
ablation10Removing something -- a feature, a direction, a component, a view -- and reporting what breaks, used across 10 sources both as evidence and as the thing being argued against. The corpus's most useful content is about when it misleads. One source argues explicitly against it for locating a representation, on the grounds that when a representation is split or partially redundant across latents, removing one produces false negatives, and steers along the decoder direction in both signs instead. A second shows removal answering a different question from the one asked: removing a direction from the trunk, from the diffusion module, or from both fails to reduce the generated content, so the trunk signal is sufficient for decodability and not necessary for generation. A third bounds its own claim in the sentence that makes it, noting that a suppression experiment establishes a representational effect on reconstruction and not a causal effect on receiver behaviour, because downstream outputs were never measured after the intervention. And selectivity is fragile: in one cross-model study a language-selective ablation hurts the target language significantly more in all 17 models while selectivity for the other language is statistically indistinguishable from zero in 12 of 17, with the direction of the asymmetry reversing under a different attribution method. Where the corpus is positive it is about ablations that are matched and exhaustive rather than single: one reports every removal degrading both metrics in essentially every setting and reads the ordering, and several note that a complete or near-complete suppression without a matched null -- a random component of comparable activation volume -- shows that something changed rather than that the right thing did.
process reward model10A learned scorer for intermediate reasoning steps, and across 10 sources the standard route to dense supervision -- with the standing objections that it needs step annotations, a separate training run, or online judging, all of which the archive's cheaper alternatives are designed around. Two findings worth carrying beyond the cost argument. Its ranking for candidate selection does not carry over to its use as a policy model: in one comparison only the weakest selection variant improved policy performance, a disconnect that source reports without explanation even though the two uses are what such a model is for. And the training cost differs by more than an order of magnitude between constructions -- one rollout-based method requires 38.6 to 38.8 times the FLOPs of a cross-entropy implicit variant, and 21.3 to 146.5 times a preference-based one depending on rollouts. It also appears as the verifier in compute-optimal test-time allocation, where the allocation rather than the scorer is what buys the fourfold efficiency.
circuit analysis9Identifying the subgraph of components a model uses for a task, and across 9 sources a method whose reach the corpus is explicit about. Its scope has been limited to partial evidence or to small models -- three-layer transformers and GPT-2-scale networks -- and the sources that extend it beyond that do so in controlled synthetic settings, which is what makes the analysis and the in-distribution/out-of-distribution split possible while leaving open whether the same structure arises during realistic pretraining. Two self-imposed caveats recur. The analysis often operates at the level of attention patterns, and the authors of one source say it would be better strengthened by embedding-level analysis. And an architectural or mechanistic suggestion that follows from a circuit is a hypothesis rather than a tested claim; several sources mark that boundary themselves. The archive's related material bears on it from the identifiability side: a decomposition that is not invariant to the procedure that produced it is not comparable across studies, which is a question circuit work inherits whenever its units come from a learned dictionary rather than from the weights.
contrastive activation addition9Computing a steering direction as the difference of mean activations between contrastive prompt pairs, then adding it at inference -- the canonical activation-steering recipe and, across 9 sources, the one whose defaults the archive has repeatedly found responsible for its failures. Three are documented. The direction can be inverted: choosing by discriminability, as the foundational method does when it selects the most discriminative attention heads, admits vectors that separate a concept well and move it backwards, mined systematically at three per model-concept pair across 15 pairs. The site is arbitrary: injection layers are fixed globally per behaviour, while the useful band is narrow, mid-network and different per model and per benchmark, and per-instance selection outperforms any fixed set. And the units are uncalibrated: an apparent scale trend in steerability dissolves when activation units, the read-out metric and a hand-picked operating point are corrected. Its collateral effect is separable -- the component that damages refusal is a different direction from the one producing the intended effect -- but not removable by the obvious route, since ablating the canonical refusal direction fails to restore safety when several independent directions mediate refusal. The archive's operative reading is that the recipe works when the direction, the layer and the magnitude are each chosen with a control rather than inherited as configuration.
Monte Carlo tree search9Building a search tree by simulation, using visit counts and value estimates to decide where to expand, applied across 9 sources to reasoning traces, report generation and physical test planning. Two structural properties transfer beyond any one application. Progressive widening decouples the search from the size of the action space: across grids spanning a 36-fold range in action cardinality the realised tree is identical at 500 nodes and 30 root children, because children expanded at a node are bounded by its visit count -- so refining the action resolution raises attainable quality at no combinatorial cost. And the budget curve carries a condition: yield rises smoothly and sublinearly with iterations on a feasible instance while a fourfold increase changes nothing on an infeasible one, so per-instance sensitivity rather than the aggregate scaling curve tells you which regime you are in. The archive's related material supplies the counterweight for language settings: a tree search over reasoning branches can lose badly to a single chain when branches interpret the problem inconsistently and the search has no scorer able to adjudicate between them.
selectivity control9A comparison establishing that an intervention affects the thing it names and not a neighbouring one -- a matched item the effect should not touch, a random direction of the same magnitude, a permuted assignment, a model that never produced the data. Across 9 sources it is what separates the archive's usable causal claims from its suggestive ones. Its positive form is narrow and quantitative: a scratchpad-editing study reports a move-specific gap of +0.57 and +0.68, establishing that the model combines the edited state with the supplied move rather than emitting a fixed answer. Its absence is what the archive most often flags -- an intervention that suppresses a feature and observes degradation, with no random feature of comparable activation volume and no orthogonal direction reported, has shown that something changed rather than that the right thing did. Three forms recur and are not interchangeable. A removal answers whether a component carries information. A permutation that preserves the component and breaks only its alignment answers whether the method uses the alignment, which is usually the claim. And a baseline instrument that could not have the property -- a probe trained on a model that never produced the responses, reaching 0.72 where the real probe reaches 0.86 -- bounds how much of a result is generic. Selectivity itself can be fragile: in one cross-model study a language-selective ablation is significant in one direction for all 17 models and statistically indistinguishable from zero in the other for 12 of 17, with the asymmetry reversing under a different attribution method.
logit lens8Decoding an intermediate activation through the output unembedding to see what token it would produce, used across 8 sources as a cheap read-out of what a layer is holding. The corpus is consistent about its status: it gives a relative ordering rather than a calibrated probability, since intermediate states are not calibrated for direct decoding, and one source keeps it in raw form deliberately for cross-model comparability. That caveat has a direct methodological consequence the archive should carry -- a region or state that fails to decode may be one whose content is not yet expressed in the unembedding basis rather than one that lacks the content, so a negative logit-lens result is weak evidence. Its productive uses here are comparative rather than absolute: agreement with the sampled token rising gradually across depth in one model and jumping over three layers in another; regions attended for real objects decoding to the target token while regions attended for hallucinated ones do not; and, at scales where no full-rank reference can be trained, a trained alternative catching attacks the raw lens misses (0.80 against 0.68).
attention analysis7Reading attention weights as evidence about what a model is using, across 7 sources both a productive signal and a repeatedly qualified one. Its productive uses here are comparative rather than absolute: attention paid to the end-of-thinking token from that position ranks which reasoning steps matter, validated by ablation showing that pruning the highest-ranked steps spikes the answer's perplexity while pruning the lowest raises it marginally and random pruning falls between; and cross-phase routing focus separates reasoning hallucinations. Its recurring qualification is that attention says where a model looked, not what it found -- one hallucination study finds attention alone does not separate real from hallucinated objects and only what the attended region decodes to does, and another notes that a circuit analysis operating mostly at the level of attention patterns would be better strengthened by embedding-level analysis. The archive's standing note applies: high attention correlated with a downstream quantity is a correlation between one position and one measurement, not evidence that information is routed through that position causally.
budget forcing7Controlling how long a model thinks by cutting generation off at a limit or by appending a continuation cue such as 'Wait' to extend it, introduced as the second of two ingredients in a minimal test-time-scaling recipe alongside supervised fine-tuning on 1,000 curated traces. Its notable property is extrapolation beyond the model's own unmanaged behaviour -- 50 to 57 percent on one competition benchmark for a 32B model. The archive's caveat, stated in one source, is that it works by appending a specific token, which makes it a property of a model that has learned to treat that token as a cue rather than a general control mechanism -- and the archive's related material supports reading it that way, since control over thinking length turns out to be concentrated in a single special token rather than distributed over the trace.
reasoning distillation7Training a smaller model on long reasoning traces from a larger one, and across 7 sources both a widely used capability transfer and the comparison that complicates the archive's reinforcement-learning results. Two findings. It does transfer capability where on-policy methods do not: evaluated identically across sampling budgets, off-policy distillation is higher on both coverage and average correctness at every budget while on-policy distillation's advantage reverses at large budgets. And it is the right control for several claims: one exploration study's controlled comparison is verifiable-reward training against long-chain distillation from the same base rather than against the base model, so 'reduces exploration' there means relative to a distilled policy trained on the same data. The corpus's caution is about attribution -- one source's strongest supervised arm learns from a 14B distilled reasoning model while its reinforcement arm has no teacher at all, so a headline supervised-versus-reinforcement comparison confounds the trace source with the training method. Several archived efficiency methods distil pruned or compressed traces rather than raw ones, and one head-to-head finds step-level importance criteria agreeing on what to keep while disagreeing on what to cut, because redundancy is diffuse rather than located in an identifiable class of step.
Coconut6Feeding a model's last hidden state back as the next input embedding so that reasoning proceeds in continuous space rather than through emitted tokens, and across 6 sources the reference implementation of latent reasoning -- along with the archive's clearest evidence about what such methods do and do not achieve. Its measured trade is domain-dependent: on grade-school arithmetic explicit chain of thought reaches 42.9 percent at 25.0 tokens against its 34.1 at 8.2, while on a structured logic task it reaches 97.0 percent at 14.2 tokens against explicit chain of thought's 77.5 at 49.4 -- so it wins where search over a constrained space is the bottleneck and loses where the reasoning is arithmetic. Two negative results matter more. It is fragile to train: removing the curriculum collapses it to at or below the no-reasoning baseline on two of three tasks. And in the fine-tuned regime its latent tokens can be inert -- deleting every one of them changes accuracy by at most 1.0 point across four models, with entity probing showing the final answer dominating the belief distribution from step 0 with no progression, against a discrete baseline where the target takes over only at the last step.
difference-in-means direction6Taking the difference between mean activations over two contrasting sets as the direction representing what distinguishes them -- the simplest construction in the archive and the one most steering results are built on. Its recurring weakness is that the direction is only as clean as the contrast: pairs that differ in the intended property also differ lexically and in register, so the recovered axis mixes them, and one source states this about its own contrasts directly. Two measured consequences. As a classifier it is often the weakest of the families compared -- one study reports it slightly behind three alternatives on the same data and declines to select its intervention by classification accuracy for that reason. And as a correction it is insufficient where the structure is not a constant offset: subtracting the empirical mean difference between truthful and hallucinated representations yields only modest improvement, which the source takes as evidence that hallucination is not primarily a global bias and that covariance structure is doing the work.
PCA6Projecting activations onto their directions of greatest variance, and across 6 sources the baseline that decomposition methods must beat and frequently do not by much. Two careful comparisons. On automated interpretability across residual-stream layers, sparse coding scores about 0.30 at the first layer declining to 0.15 by the fifth, against 0.07 to 0.13 for principal components and 0.06 to 0.08 for random directions -- so the ordering is right and the margin narrows with depth. On causal transport, ablating the top-j modes of an alternative decomposition moves a behavioural readout more than j random directions and less than j principal directions (at j=8, principal components remove 60 percent against the modes' 25), with the gap decaying 4.1-fold in depth-distance and essentially gone by about 8 -- read by those authors narrowly, as principal components ceasing to be the better transport basis without the alternative being shown to be one. The archive's use of this pair is as a template: report the simple baseline and the random null together, and let the margin between them bound the claim.
sparse dictionary learning6Finding a set of directions such that every activation vector is a sparse linear combination of them -- the formal statement of what a sparse autoencoder approximates, and the method the superposition hypothesis implies if features are assigned to an overcomplete set of non-orthogonal directions. Across 6 sources the archive's evidence is the same as for its dominant implementation: it beats the alternatives on automated interpretability (about 0.30 at the first layer against 0.14 for independent components and 0.06 to 0.08 for random directions) and declines with depth to about 0.15 by the fifth layer, its reconstruction is lossy enough to raise perplexity from 25 to 40 when substituted back, and its solutions are not identifiable, with different seeds and widths recovering materially different features from the same activations. One source in the corpus argues the non-identifiability is structural rather than algorithmic, since a reconstruction-and-sparsity objective does not enforce the invariance identifiability would require.
VeRL6The reinforcement learning library these sources build their training pipelines on. None describes its design; it appears as the implementation substrate, cited to establish that a comparison against a baseline is like-for-like — that a method differs from its GRPO baseline only in the token-selection step with loss and optimizer identical, or that it follows the DAPO recipe inside the same codebase. Its recurrence across five independent entries is itself a fact worth recording about the archive: much of the entropy literature here is measured on one framework, so an artefact of that framework would not show up as a disagreement between them.
beam search5Keeping a fixed number of partial candidates and extending the best of them, and across 5 sources the search baseline in test-time-scaling comparisons -- generally beaten by methods that reduce completed candidates rather than search over unfinished ones. Its distinctive property in the archive's taxonomy is that it operates on partial states, which is a different statistical object from sampling completed candidates and reducing them, and one source's argument is that reporting these under one scalar budget makes results incomparable. Where it is adapted rather than used as a baseline, the adaptation is about what the freed capacity does: a thought-level variant treats pruning as zero-sum, immediately re-spending the capacity released by dropping a weak trace on branching from a high-scoring prefix, which is what makes prefix caching load-bearing for it.
bootstrap confidence intervals5Resampling to put an interval on an estimate, and in this archive the practice that separates the sources whose numbers can be read from those whose cannot -- most archived papers report no variance at all. The corpus's instances show what the interval buys. Problem-level resampling at 1000 iterations with a fixed seed underwrites a pre-registered backfire rate of 56.6 percent with interval [49.5, 63.6]. Hierarchical resampling that jointly resamples seeds and problems gives [+3.92, +5.78] on a 4.85-point gain, which is what licenses calling it real; a paired bootstrap stratified by problem and seed gives [2.592, 8.333] on a 5.617-point gain. And the reverse case is equally instructive: a reproduction finds a published 2.5-point advantage with a bootstrap interval of [0.0, +6.3] that vanishes entirely when the evaluation set is enlarged. One source uses question-cluster bootstrapping on a two-annotator expert study to give [6.6, 19.1] on a 12.5-point relevance gain. The archive's operative rule is that resampling must match the dependence structure -- resampling problems alone when seeds also vary understates the interval, and neither is meaningful for banks produced by adaptive search.
causal mediation analysis5Measuring a component's causal contribution to an outcome by intervening on it while holding the rest of the computation fixed — the framework activation patching instantiates. The archived sources have moved from citing it as lineage to working with it. Two use it as the method proper: one recovers a sparse propositional-logic circuit in models up to 27B and decomposes it into four families of attention heads whose roles are verified by targeted counterfactual patching, and one traces the mechanism behind a System-2 decomposition of counting. One generalizes the technique, relaxing the combinatorial search over which components to intervene on into a continuous one over soft interventions so subsets can be chosen jointly rather than one component or layer at a time. One is a methodological audit finding that the choices inside a patching experiment — how the prompt is corrupted, which metric scores the effect, whether layers are patched singly or in sliding windows — each change the conclusion. None works with the formal decomposition of counterfactual effects into direct and indirect parts.
permutation test5Rerunning an entire analysis with the labels or assignments shuffled, to measure what the pipeline scores when the structure it depends on is destroyed. Across 5 sources it is the archive's canonical null control, and its uses divide by what is permuted. Labels: shuffling and rerunning 200 times per layer establishes what a probing pipeline scores on meaningless labels, which is what licenses reading a held-out accuracy at all when the feature dimension exceeds the sample count by two orders of magnitude. Correspondence: permuting per-turn multipliers within a trajectory, or shuffling privileged trajectories across problems, preserves the signal's distribution and destroys only its alignment -- which is the experiment that separates a method exploiting matched correspondence from one benefiting from the signal's marginal properties. Deployment: one source builds a permutation test into a two-stage planner that resamples score laws at each candidate sample size, reporting it as uniformly conservative with 0.785 to 0.92 power and abstaining on the one channel whose probe does not transport. The archive's finding is that removal and permutation are different experiments and that where both are run, the ordering between them is itself the measurement.
activation probing4Reading a property of a model's computation off its internal activations with a small auxiliary predictor, rather than from its output. The sources use it as a monitoring and verification tool: to detect hint-reliance when the reasoning trace is latent, to distinguish sound from flawed reasoning, and to predict answer correctness. They disagree on what to probe. Two argue that static single-layer activations invite the probe to latch onto surface lexical patterns, and read cross-layer displacement instead; a third restores restricted location information on the grounds that displacement alone discards too much; a fourth reduces the whole trace to one start-to-end delta and still beats single-layer baselines. All four report gains over single-layer probing, on different benchmarks and with no shared model.
causal tracing4Intervening on internal representations to quantify the pathways linking inputs or computations to a metric of interest. Across 4 sources the archive's material is mostly about extending it past its usual one-component-at-a-time form: one source relaxes the combinatorial search over subsets of components into a continuous one over soft interventions, so that components can be selected jointly rather than swept singly. Its application in the corpus recovers sparse circuits -- one propositional-logic study decomposes a recovered circuit into four families of attention heads executing rule locating, rule moving, fact processing and decision making as sequential steps across models from 7B to 27B. The archive's standing caveats for this family apply: no convention exists for the corruption method or the evaluation metric, so results are frequently not comparable across papers, and an analysis conducted at the level of attention patterns is weaker than one carried to the embedding level, which one source says of its own work.
chain-of-thought compression4Shortening a reasoning trace while trying to keep what the answer depends on, pursued in this archive in two families that the sources treat as distinct. Selective pruning scores each unit — a token, a step, a graph node — and deletes below a threshold; generative rewriting produces a new shorter rationale by paraphrasing, fusing steps or regenerating against a template. Four archived sources establish how the choices inside each family behave. Granularity governs the criterion: at step level different scores converge on the same backbone so the choice hardly matters, while at token level only symbol-aware scoring avoids deleting operators and numbers. Restructuring inverts by domain, degrading mathematics monotonically as structure is disturbed while acting as a denoiser on general tasks. What is nominally pruning is often mostly rewriting — in one attention-guided method, rewrite and fuse account for three quarters of operations and outright keeping for 3.2% — with steps falling 62.4% while average step length rises 22.5%. And the compression measured on training data is not the compression a deployed student produces: long-CoT students retain verbose habits despite concise supervision, so a training-side ratio is an optimistic lower bound on deployment cost.
chain-of-thought distillation4Training a student on reasoning traces rather than on answers alone, and across 4 sources a practice whose benefit the archive can now locate fairly precisely: out of distribution rather than in it. The controlled study is the anchor -- the two conditions differ in nothing but the training target, and rationales buy nothing in-domain for strong backbones while buying 20 to 28 points on a contamination-audited external benchmark, with a two-annotator human study attributing the measurable effect to language adherence and inspectability rather than to better reasoning. A domain-specific instance agrees on direction and magnitude: chain-of-thought supervision lifts a 9B model to 89.0 percent micro accuracy where answer-only supervision reaches 75.1 and the base model 37.4. Two refinements. The trace must match the student's capacity, which one source implements by having a teacher decompose and prune the student's own reasoning by skill before distilling it back. And the gain is largest for the weakest backbone and smallest for one already tuned for the domain, which is the same capacity-and-idiom pattern the archive's distillation entries record.
Cohen's kappa4Agreement between two raters corrected for chance, and across 4 sources the statistic the archive requires before a labelled result is read. Its instances show what it adds over raw agreement. At 0.85 with 93 percent raw agreement it establishes that a labelling task is reproducible, which is what licenses scoring a model judge against those annotators. At 0.83 across three raters it supports a claim that 200 cards contain no answer leak. In a judge evaluation it is the validity axis that comes apart from reliability -- one provider's judges average 0.770 while another's average 0.467, and the model with the highest test-retest reliability in the study has the third-lowest kappa. And where a class is near ceiling it exposes what raw agreement hides: 0.90 to 0.99 raw agreement alongside kappas of 0.357 to 0.573, which means a headline gain on such a dimension rests on a handful of discordant judgements.

AIME 202452Thirty competition mathematics problems from the 2024 American Invitational Mathematics Examination, and by source count the archive's most used hard benchmark -- 52 papers. Its properties as an instrument are what the archive should carry. It is thirty problems, so a single problem is 3.3 percentage points and most reported differences on it are one or two problems; one source flags this itself, noting that pass@k comparisons at large k are more stable than accuracy deltas but the absolute count stays small. It is where methods separate most: entropy-control work reports its largest margins here (15.0 points against 14.6 on the 2025 edition and far less elsewhere), and parameter-space exploration reports up to 5.83 points here against fractions of a point on easier sets. It is also where averages are carried or destroyed -- one paper notes its own five-benchmark average is carried by the easier sets because this one sits at 5.21 and 7.71 at small scale, and another reports its objective buying tail coverage at visible cost in the columns for this benchmark. Two robustness findings attach to it. Under a symbolic variabilisation that replaces its constants with sampled values and requires consistency across instantiations, strict-metric accuracy falls 58.8 percent on average, though the loose metric that grants partial credit shows far milder drops (1.5 percent for one 32B model), which separates contamination from inconsistency. That makes it the right place to look when a headline average moves and the reader wants to know whether anything stable moved with it. Its position in difficulty produces two recurring patterns. Compression and efficiency methods report their largest savings here -- 53 percent token reduction on the top decile of problems and 81 percent on the top percentile -- because a mid-difficulty set contains many problems the model overthinks. And methods aimed at hard reasoning report muted effects, since the base model already solves it given enough attempts. Two cautions from the corpus. It has been used as the source material for adversarially rewritten evaluation sets, so a set derived from it has whatever difficulty the rewriting model produced. Across 42 sources it is the easy rung of nearly every suite, and its role has changed: later archived work treats it as saturated, uses it to check that a method aimed at hard problems has not broken easy ones, and raises contamination concerns about publicly available mathematics benchmarks in general. Two things make it worth keeping in a suite. Its size means a five-benchmark average is often carried by it and by MATH500 while the 30-problem competition sets swing, so it is where a claimed average improvement is either substantiated or exposed. And per-task ablations frequently invert on it -- one method's largest loss is removing thought-matching here and removing the latent thought entirely on a harder set, and one steering study finds influence falling at the end of a trace here while rising at the end on two other benchmarks. AIME 202538Thirty problems from the 2025 American Invitational Mathematics Examination, used across 38 sources as the harder and less contaminated companion to the 2024 edition. Its release after many base models' training cutoffs is the property that makes it distinctive rather than redundant: one source attributes the sharpest gap in its pass@k-versus-CoT-pass@k comparison to exactly that freedom from contamination. It is where test-time-scaling methods report their largest margins -- one reaches +13.4 points on it against small gains on easier sets, and a sequential-scaling baseline plateaus around 22 percent at 8K thinking tokens and does not improve at 32K while the compared method climbs to 35.6. That same source states the arithmetic honestly: 30 problems means +13.4 is four problems even at three seeds. The archive should also carry that internal signals behave differently here than on its 2024 sibling: Spearman correlation between one entropy diagnostic and average accuracy is 0.417 on the 2024 set and 0.047 on this one, so a diagnostic validated on one is not validated on the other.
AMC2323Forty problems from the 2023 American Mathematics Competitions, used across 23 sources as the middle rung between grade-school and Olympiad-level mathematics. Its size is the first thing to know: at 40 problems one item is 2.5 points, and at least one source explicitly flags reduced statistical power on it while reporting its largest swings there. Its position in difficulty is what makes it useful and what limits it. Length-compression work reports the clearest waste on it -- a median 2.7K emitted tokens against a median 830 needed for the correct answer -- and one compressed model exceeds its uncompressed original outright (77.5 against 72.5), which the authors read as redundant steps having introduced noise. Recovery from injected doubt cues tracks difficulty and places it between the easier and harder sets consistently across two backbones. Conversely it is where the pass@k argument about reinforcement learning is muted, because the base model already solves it given enough attempts, and where several methods' margins are small because the benchmark is near saturation. The most informative result attached to it is a robustness one: under symbolic variabilisation, strict-metric accuracy falls 47.9 percent on average, with reinforcement-tuned 7B models losing 22.9 to 94.5 percent and frontier models only 1.2 to 6.6 -- but the loose metric granting partial credit narrows the robust models' drop to 2.6 to 7.2 percent while a base model still loses 38.5, which separates memorised patterns from mere inconsistency.
GPQA-Diamond18198 graduate-level multiple-choice science questions, used across 18 sources as the archive's standard non-mathematical hard benchmark. Three properties make it distinctive. It is where monitorability is lowest -- dataset difficulty is inversely related to it, and this set scores at the bottom of the range in one study -- and where faithfulness failures are starkest: under KV-cache compression one method slightly exceeds the uncompressed model's accuracy at 57.9 percent while 90.2 percent of its correct answers carry unsupported reasoning, against 42.3 percent uncompressed, which is the archive's cleanest instance of accuracy preserved while the reasoning underneath it is destroyed. It is small: 198 problems, so a per-problem analysis is feasible and a single item is half a point, and it is the set on which majority-vote self-consistency was shown pre-registered to lower per-problem accuracy on 56.6 and 65.7 percent of problems for two small models. And it is the benchmark most likely to move in the opposite direction from a method's target -- one rubric-trained medical system gains 17.41 points here as a generalisation check, while its two components split, one contributing 8.69 and the other 3.53. Its four-option format means chance is 25 percent, which several archived results sit close to.
MATH17A competition mathematics benchmark used across 17 sources, most often as a training corpus and as the full set from which the 500-problem evaluation subset is drawn. In the archive it appears mainly as one column among five or seven, and the useful observations about it are comparative. It is where per-benchmark orderings most often contradict the average: one method with the best overall score loses it narrowly (67.22 against 67.40) while winning three others, and another reports its baseline taking it at one scale (84.0 against 84.2) while the average goes the other way. Ablations are task-dependent on it in a way that is informative -- on one method the largest loss on a grade-school set is removing thought-matching while on this one it is removing the latent thought entirely, so what a component contributes depends on the difficulty of the set it is measured on. Several sources train on it and evaluate transfer elsewhere, which means their generalisation claims rest on a mathematics-only training distribution; a number of them say so explicitly as a limitation.
OlympiadBench17A competition mathematics benchmark at olympiad level, used across 17 sources as the hard end of a suite alongside the AIME sets. Its practical role is as the set where efficiency and steering methods show their clearest gains, because it contains long traces with room to compress: one length-compression method raises accuracy from 56.77 to 59.85 on it while cutting average trace length 42.7 percent across its suite, and moderate positive steering of a reasoning-effort direction improves it as part of an average that rises at every model size from 1.5B to 32B. It is also one of the benchmarks where base models overtake reinforcement-trained ones as the sampling budget grows into the tens or hundreds, which is the archive's standing caveat about large-k comparisons on hard sets. None of the sources describes its construction or size.
LiveCodeBench12A continuously updated code-generation benchmark, used across 12 sources chiefly as the out-of-domain check for models trained on mathematics. Two results make it more informative than that role suggests. It carries the largest single coefficient in one internal-signal correlation table, at Spearman -0.894 against average accuracy -- a strong negative for a mathematics-trained model, which is the archive's sharpest illustration that a diagnostic validated in domain can point the wrong way out of it. And it is where the pass@k debate goes the other way: on code, pass@k itself improves after verifiable-reward training, with one trained model beating its distilled starting point across six versions of the benchmark, so the boundary extends there even from a distilled model. Its continuous updating is the property cited for reduced contamination risk relative to fixed sets.
MMLU10A broad multiple-choice knowledge benchmark spanning many academic subjects, used across 10 sources as the general-capability check that a reasoning-focused intervention has not damaged something else. Its high baseline is what makes it useful in that role and limits it in others: at accuracies near 88 percent, test-time-scaling methods report gains of one to three points and one steering study finds negative steering cutting reasoning tokens on it while maintaining accuracy, which the authors read as models misallocating reasoning strength on easy questions. Its STEM subset appears as a mid-difficulty column in latent-reasoning suites. One transfer result attaches to it: structural geometry learned on grade-school mathematics probes at or above 0.85 here, while a correctness predictor trained on the same data falls to 0.64 -- the same dissociation the archive records between structure and correctness elsewhere.
MMLU-Pro8A harder, ten-option revision of the broad knowledge benchmark, used across 8 sources where the original is too saturated to discriminate. Its distinctive role in this archive is as the setting for the sharpest negative result about reasoning summaries: on outcome-discordant items, linear readers of a model-written summary sit at within-item AUROC 0.503 to 0.545 -- essentially chance -- while readers of the full trace retain 0.544 to 0.590. The source is careful about what that rests on, noting the analysis runs on this benchmark alone, on an unusually difficult discordant subset whose accuracy is 23 to 30 points below the held-out pool with traces 2.5 to 3 times longer. It is also where one influence analysis finds the distinguishing behaviour at the end of a trace rising rather than falling, opposite to a grade-school set, and where one safety-alignment pipeline's utility cost lands hardest on small models.
GPQA7The graduate-level science question set from which the archive's more commonly used Diamond subset is drawn, appearing in 7 sources as a general or out-of-domain evaluation column. Its recurring role is to test transfer: mathematics-only training carries to it in several studies, with one token-selection method improving here while a baseline gains on mathematics and loses on the non-mathematics average, and every model in one cross-domain comparison beating its base here despite mathematics-only training. It is also where one correctness-detection method has its largest margin over the runner-up (0.830 against 0.759, p below 0.001) while margins on easier sets in the same table are within 0.006 -- so a headline advantage on this benchmark can carry a claim that the rest of the table does not support. One quantization study reports it identical at the densest and most compressed settings while dropping at an intermediate one, which the authors treat as descriptive noise.
HumanEval+7A function-level Python synthesis benchmark with expanded test coverage in its plus variant, used across 7 sources as one column in code or general-capability suites rather than as a subject. Its appearances here are mainly cautionary about reading single-run tables: in one quantization study it falls 39.63 to 35.98 while a neighbouring benchmark rises and a third is identical at the densest and sparsest settings while dropping at an intermediate one, a pattern the authors themselves describe as more consistent with single-run noise than with a compression effect. It also appears in calibration-data studies where a general text corpus yields near-zero scores on it. None of the sources describes its construction.
2WikiMultiHopQA5A multi-hop question-answering set requiring evidence from two Wikipedia articles, used across 5 sources as one of the standard in-domain benchmarks for search-augmented agents alongside HotpotQA and the out-of-domain sets. Its role in this archive is as part of the suite over which credit-assignment methods for retrieval are averaged, where the corpus's recurring caution applies -- macro averages across these sets hide per-benchmark losses, and at least one source's method wins one benchmark of seven while losing another by 17.9 exact match without discussing it. None of the sources describes its construction.
IFEval5An instruction-following benchmark scoring whether verifiable format constraints in a prompt were obeyed, used across 5 sources as the capability-retention check that reasoning-focused training most often damages. Two archived results make it worth keeping. It is the benchmark where one rubric-trained system's components split most sharply -- consensus rubrics and three-state scoring each lower it while a tie-breaking stage raises it 30.32 points -- so a generalisation average that moves is often moving because of this column alone. And it is where an internal diagnostic's rank correlation with accuracy is +0.627, the second-largest coefficient in a table whose mathematics entries run from -0.148 to +0.417, so a signal validated on mathematics behaves differently here. It is also used to check that a mathematics-trained policy has not lost instruction following.
MBPP+5A Python programming benchmark with expanded tests in its plus variant, used across 5 sources as a code column in general-capability suites and in calibration-data studies. Its appearances here are diagnostic rather than substantive: it is one of the benchmarks where a general text corpus used for quantization calibration yields near-zero scores, illustrating that calibration data must match the target domain; and it is among the benchmarks in a robustness sweep finding that no extraction configuration for an internal correctness signal is best anywhere. None of the sources describes its construction.
BBH4A suite of BIG-Bench tasks selected for being hard, used across 4 sources as the multi-task testbed for faithfulness and monitorability rather than for capability. Two archived results attach to it. It is where the foundational unfaithfulness measurement is made: under biasing features that models never mention, accuracy drops by as much as 36 percent across its 13 tasks with the explanations rationalising the biased answers. And it sits at the opposite end from a graduate science set on monitorability, scoring highest where that scores lowest -- which is the basis for the finding that monitorability tracks task difficulty rather than reasoning mode.
indirect object identification4The small syntactic task -- predicting the indirect object in a sentence with two named entities -- that serves across 4 sources as mechanistic interpretability's standard testbed. Its value is that a known circuit and a clean logit-difference readout make methods comparable; its limitation is that everything established on it is established on one narrow behaviour in small models. Two archived results use it well: patching dictionary features reaches a given divergence from a counterfactual target with fewer patched features than principal or non-negative components, improving the Pareto frontier of edit magnitude against divergence; and a mode-ablation comparison finds principal directions removing 60 percent of the logit difference at j=8 against an alternative's 25, with the gap decaying in depth-distance and gone by about 8 -- read narrowly by those authors, who note twelve layers is not enough depth to separate depth-distance from layer under a behavioural readout.
the Pile4A large general-purpose text corpus used across these four sources as the substrate a method is fitted or measured on rather than as an object of study. It supplies the activations sparse autoencoders are trained on -- dictionaries in the foundational paper come from the first 10,000 elements, about 7 million activations -- and the same role recurs in the set-level instability work that takes the set of active latents as its unit and finds that adding a semantically compatible adjective to a noun deactivates 20 to 60 percent of the latents the noun alone had active. It is also one of the corpora over which dense trained lenses are fitted at every layer and hookpoint. Its fourth appearance is different in kind: in the contamination-detectability work it figures in an analysis casting benchmark contamination auditing as sparse-mixture detection, where what matters about a pretraining corpus is its size relative to the contaminated fraction. No source here describes its composition.
SciQ3A science question-answering set that appears in three archived papers as a supporting evaluation set, with none of them describing its construction or reporting a headline attributed to it. Two are test-time-scaling papers that use it as the non-mathematical member of an otherwise mathematical suite: the interpretable adaptive-sampling controller is evaluated on GSM8K, MATH and SciQ, and ThinkRetrieve on GSM8K, MATH500, AIME 2025, SciQ and NuminaMath. The third uses it as one of the corpora over which dense trained lenses are fitted and evaluated, alongside the Pile, 2WikiMultiHopQA, ARC-Easy and WikiText-2. Its role in this archive is as a breadth check rather than as a target.
AlpacaEval2An instruction-following benchmark scored by LLM judges, used in the archived sources in two unrelated ways. As a judge benchmark it is part of the preference-evaluation family whose scores are reported not to track safety, world knowledge or instruction following, with style bias offered as the explanation. Separately, its questions serve as the vanilla control in an overthink-detection experiment, paired against versions modified by an overthink attack, where a pre-generation length probe predicts substantially longer reasoning on the attacked versions.
ARC-Easy2The easier split of the AI2 Reasoning Challenge, appearing in this archive twice as a general-knowledge evaluation set. In the dense-lens work it is one of five corpora -- with the Pile, 2WikiMultiHopQA, SciQ and WikiText-2 -- over which trained lenses are fitted and evaluated, and the setting where a lens-based prompt-injection detector at 70B catches attacks an untrained logit lens misses (0.80 against 0.68). In the agent-channel compression study it is one of nine benchmarks, and the one where accuracy rises under compression, from 75.46 to 78.96 -- which that paper reads as evidence its per-task movements are noise rather than as an effect. BigCodeBench2A code-generation benchmark used in both sources as an evaluation set rather than a subject. Its most informative appearance is in the probing-robustness sweep, where it is one of two benchmarks whose disagreement is the finding: on this one, averaging hidden states over the response beats reading the last token by more than twenty points, while on the other the preference reverses -- and the two cancel in a pooled analysis, leaving a decisive factor ranked at under 1 percent of variance. It is also where the fitting data dominates, carrying 75 percent of variance against 16 for construction and 9 for the model, and where a synthetic fitting source drops the signal to 22 percent against a 25 percent chance floor. It appears again in the dynamic early-exit work. COCO2A large image-captioning corpus, used in both sources as the substrate for measuring object hallucination rather than as a benchmark in its own right. Captions generated on its images are scored against its object annotations to count mentions of objects that are not present, which is what makes it the standard ground for the CHAIR family of metrics. The structured read-out work uses self-generated captions on it to reach 92.3 F1 for hallucinated-mention detection against 82.0 for the strongest prior method, and reports word count, correct-object coverage and perplexity alongside the hallucination reduction so that the improvement cannot be a suppression artifact. The attention-versus-evidence work uses it for the same purpose. Neither source describes the corpus; in this archive it functions as the annotated image set that makes an object-level hallucination claim checkable.
OpenCodeInstruct2A large instruction-tuning corpus of programming problems, used by both sources as the coding half of a training or calibration mixture rather than as an evaluation set. One draws coding questions from it, in equal proportion with mathematics, to build the calibration set for ternary quantization — the mixture whose composition turns out to decide whether the quantized model scores zero or fifty-eight on mathematics. The other uses it among the corpora its step-level sparse autoencoder is trained over. In both, it is there to keep a method from being tuned on mathematics alone.
parity2The k-parity task — whether an odd number of k relevant bits among n are set — used by both sources as the canonical testbed for what intermediate supervision buys, because the separation is sharp and proved rather than observed. Without intermediate supervision it is hard for finite-precision gradient methods; with explicit chain of thought even a one-layer transformer learns it efficiently. That makes it the cleanest available instrument for studying how CoT turns an expressible solution into a reachable one. In the archive it carries two results: that CoT changes the generalization bound from exponential to linear in reasoning length, and that internalizing the chain into hidden states preserves polynomial sample efficiency.
POPE2A polling-based probe for object hallucination in vision-language models, asking yes-or-no questions about whether particular objects appear in an image under random, popular and adversarial samplings of the negatives -- the last drawn from objects that frequently co-occur with what is present, so a model relying on co-occurrence priors fails. Both sources use it as their grounding benchmark. It is where the structured trace read-out reports its largest margin, reaching 90.0 area under the curve where every prior detector -- external verifiers and flat read-outs of the same frozen internals alike -- plateaus between 69 and 75, which that paper attributes to representation of the evidence rather than access to it. The Wiener-filtering work reports gains across all three splits. Neither describes its construction; what matters in this archive is the adversarial split, which is designed so that the prior a model would use to guess is exactly the prior that makes it wrong.
ProntoQA2A synthetic deductive-reasoning question-answering task, used by both sources as the near-ceiling control in a latent chain-of-thought comparison: explicit and latent reasoning reach 99.8% and 98.8%, so it separates nothing about representation. Its diagnostic value is what happens when the training curriculum is removed — accuracy collapses from 99.8% to 52.4%, a larger drop than either source reports on the harder benchmarks. A task on which two methods are indistinguishable at ceiling therefore turns out to be the sharpest evidence that the curriculum, not the latent representation, is what makes the method work.
ProsQA2A synthetic graph-traversal question-answering task with binary queries, used by both sources as the case where latent chain-of-thought looks best and, on closer reading, as the case that explains why. One reports latent reasoning at 97.0% against 77.5% for explicit chain of thought while using 14.2 tokens against 49.4, and attributes the advantage to sustained uncertainty: its Symbolic Index stays below 0.6, in the 0.2 to 0.5 band, so a distribution over several latent paths is maintained rather than collapsed. The other exploits the binary answer space as an experimental affordance — with two possible answers, deleting every latent token is a clean do-operation whose counterfactual is read straight off two log probabilities — and finds accuracy changes by at most 1.0 point when they are removed. Read together, the benchmark is where latent reasoning wins and where the win is shown not to depend on the latent tokens carrying the computation.
PubMedQA2A biomedical question-answering set built from research abstracts, used in both sources as a medical benchmark among several. In the consensus-rubric work it is one of six medical benchmarks and one of the places where three-state criterion scoring gains most (6.80 points), though the final system does not lead on it -- a reminder that the method's headline is a macro-average over benchmarks that individually disagree.
