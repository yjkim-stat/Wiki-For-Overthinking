# alignment tax

<!-- auto:begin -->

The capability cost paid for making a model behave as intended. The sources establish it in both directions and disagree on its size. One measures a genuine tension: models that reason better comply less with user directives, and interventions that restore obedience cost reasoning performance, so the trade-off is Pareto rather than a fixable defect. The other reports the opposite, claiming safety alignment from 1K supervised examples with reasoning, QA, summarization and multilingual ability preserved, though without numbers. A third archived source supplies the mechanism the first would predict, finding that prolonged RLVR degrades perception and faithfulness unless replay is added.

## Appears in

- [Scaling Reasoning, Losing Control: Evaluating Instruction Following in Large Reasoning Models](../../archive/papers/2026/doi-10-18653-v1-2026-acl-long-1878/summary.md) — A benchmark showing that as reasoning capacity grows, instruction adherence falls, and that recovering obedience costs reasoning performance.
- [Reasoning Structure Matters for Safety Alignment of Reasoning Models](../../archive/papers/2026/doi-10-18653-v1-2026-acl-long-240/summary.md) — Argues reasoning models' safety failures come from the reasoning structure itself, and achieves safety alignment by altering that structure with 1K supervised examples and no RL.
