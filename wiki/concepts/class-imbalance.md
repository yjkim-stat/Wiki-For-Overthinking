# class imbalance

<!-- auto:begin -->

A label distribution skewed enough that accuracy and agreement statistics stop being informative, and across 4 sources a condition the archive's better instruments handle explicitly. Its consequences here: raw agreement of 0.90 to 0.99 alongside chance-corrected kappas of 0.357 to 0.573, because a class near ceiling makes concordance cheap; a judge ensemble whose errors concentrate entirely at the escalate boundary rather than between accept and reject, so overall accuracy hides where the system fails; and a hallucination detector reporting balanced accuracy and class-conditional rates rather than a single figure. The archive's practical rule is that where one class dominates, a chance-corrected statistic, a class-balanced recall, or an operating-point measure such as true positive rate at a fixed false positive rate is the number to report -- several sources here do exactly that and find their gains concentrated at the operating point rather than in the area under the curve.
