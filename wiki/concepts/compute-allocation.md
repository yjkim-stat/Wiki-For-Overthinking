# compute allocation

<!-- auto:begin -->

Deciding where computation goes rather than how much there is, and across 6 sources a framing that unifies otherwise unrelated systems work. The archive's instances span four levels. Within a search: a zero-sum beam search over reasoning steps prunes weak traces and immediately re-spends the freed capacity by branching from high-scoring prefixes under a hardware budget. Within a forecast: fixed-step refinement anchored to a cheaply predicted shape, where the ablation prices every alternative use of the same compute in normalised time. Within a plan: an anytime search whose budget curve is smooth on a feasible instance and flat on an infeasible one. Within a serving system: admitting a rollout session is a commitment of cache capacity, not a placement decision, and allocating protected capacity per workload class by footprint and residency is what keeps a mixed pipeline from collapsing. And within an evaluation: scaling the compute spent judging rather than generating. The common finding is that the allocation is usually more consequential than the total, and that a system reporting throughput without reporting what the reallocation did to the intended distribution has answered half the question.

## Appears in

- [Thought-Level Beam Search for Reasoning](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-08020/summary.md) — Reformulates test-time reasoning as compute allocation over partial trajectories under a hardware budget, and runs a zero-sum beam search over reasoning steps that prunes weak traces and immediately re-spends the freed capacity by branching from high-scoring prefixes.
- [Efficient Test-Time Scaling for LLM-based Time Series Forecasting](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-08675/summary.md) — Replaces open-ended iterative refinement in LLM time-series forecasting with a fixed-step coarse-to-fine loop anchored to an explicitly predicted downscaled future shape, so test-time compute is spent enriching detail rather than re-deciding the global trajectory.
- [Adaptive Sequential Test Planning for Multi-Mechanism Reliability Qualification via Bayesian Monte Carlo Tree Search](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-09622/summary.md) — Plans semiconductor reliability stress tests as a partially observable sequential decision problem, using Monte Carlo tree search over a seed-action simulator with an extended Kalman filter tracking per-device latent degradation parameters, so each device's test adapts to its own measured behaviour rather than to a population model.
- [Scheduling Mixed RL Rollouts Beyond Prefix Locality](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-11152/summary.md) — A routing-layer admission policy for RL post-training that treats admitting a rollout session as a commitment of KV-cache capacity, allocates protected capacity per workload class by footprint and observed residency time, and leaves the workload mixture itself under the trainer's control.
- [Diagnosis Before Recovery: Turning Agent Failures into Selective Self-Correction](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-11772/summary.md) — Profiles the dominant failure mode of an agent task family on development data, then freezes a policy that permits only the recovery interventions matched to that failure -- so a failure decides which repair is admissible and how much evidence to spend, rather than triggering more context indiscriminately.
- [Scaling Evaluation-Time Compute with Reasoning Models as Evaluators](../../archive/papers/2026/doi-10-18653-v1-2026-findings-acl-2102/summary.md) — Shows evaluator accuracy improves monotonically with reasoning tokens spent, and that buying compute at evaluation time can substitute for buying it at generation time.
