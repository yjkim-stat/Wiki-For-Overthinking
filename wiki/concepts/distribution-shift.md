# distribution shift

<!-- auto:begin -->

A difference between the distribution a model was trained on and the one it meets, used across 5 sources in two distinct senses the archive keeps apart. The training sense is the train-inference mismatch that on-policy methods exist to remove: learning from a teacher corpus leaves the student unprepared for its own prefixes, and supervised self-correction suffers it in a specific way, biasing the model toward following correction templates rather than selectively revising real errors. The evaluation sense is what a deployed model meets, where the archive's material is about detecting the shift rather than surviving it -- guarded captions retaining 95.4 percent of vanilla word count with only a small distribution shift is how one source rules out suppression as the mechanism for its improvement. One further instance from the corpus: flow-based methods that swap a deterministic sampler for a stochastic one during training optimise samples the test-time sampler does not produce, which is the same problem stated in continuous space.

## Appears in

- [On the Robustness of LLMs' Internal Representation of Code Correctness](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-08266/summary.md) — Asks whether a published internal signal for code correctness is a property of the model or of the one extraction recipe used to find it, sweeps every design choice systematically, and finds no configuration is best anywhere -- with the benchmark deciding which choice wins, and a mismatched fitting source able to drive the signal below chance.
- [Improving Generalization Robustness of Multimodal RLVR](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-08802/summary.md) — Traces the brittleness of multimodal RLVR gains under paraphrase and template change to two properties of the standard objective -- a binary verifier that cannot distinguish a wrong answer from a misformatted one, and a training distribution covering a thin slice of the prompts a deployed model meets -- and fixes both with a trinary reward and an invariance penalty across semantically equivalent prompts.
- [Calibrating Post-Training Feature Shifts for LLM Data Contamination Detection](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-10462/summary.md) — Calibrates feature-based data-contamination detectors against the systematic feature shifts that post-training introduces, by measuring how controlled prompt variants move scores on known non-members and then correcting only the recurring shift directions, with the gains concentrated at the low-false-positive operating point rather than in AUC.
- [UniProbe: A Learnable Token-Level Hallucination Detector for Large VLMs using Multi-Structural Internal Representations](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-10835/summary.md) — Reads a frozen vision-language model's forward pass as a structured computational trace -- a graph over image patches, query tokens and generated tokens with attention as edges, processed by interleaved relational, spatial and sequential modules -- and shows that structuring the same internals prior detectors already read lifts hallucination detection from a 69-75 AUC plateau to 90.
- [Reinforcing Step-level Reasoning for Effective Self-Correction in LLMs](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-11573/summary.md) — Trains self-correction as a step-level preference problem -- preferring a detect-and-repair continuation over the continuation that would follow if the error went unaddressed -- after first initialising with ordinary step-level preference optimisation, and finds that correcting more often and detecting more errors both anti-correlate with accuracy.
