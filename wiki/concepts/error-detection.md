# error detection

<!-- auto:begin -->

Judging whether a reasoning step or a solution is wrong, treated by the sources as a competence separable from producing correct work. They approach it from opposite sides. One reads it off internal state, detecting flawed reasoning from residual-stream trajectory motion combined with restricted region and direction information, and improving selection accuracy by up to 12% over displacement-only methods on unseen benchmarks. The other makes it the task itself, benchmarking error step identification and categorization on 2,500 multimodal K-12 problems drawn from real student interactions, where the best model sits about 10% behind expert human evaluators. Between them the archive gets both a cheap internal signal and an external reference for what it should be measured against.
