# figurative language

<!-- auto:begin -->

Expressions whose meaning is not composed from their parts — proverbs, idioms, collocations, noun compounds — used by the sources as a probe for whether apparent understanding is recall. Both find a gap between producing such expressions and reasoning about them. One shows models complete Italian proverbs successfully yet fail multiple-choice selection when no option is correct, and its CoT analysis finds them naming the right ending while missing its absence and preferring literal synonyms. The other consolidates multiword-expression resources into one suite and reports substantial variation concentrated on the tasks it labels semantic reasoning. Neither separates lexical knowledge from reasoning over that knowledge, which is the distinction both invoke.
