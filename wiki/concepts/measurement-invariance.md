## What we have settled

- **Established** — An evaluation baseline, prompt or output format inherited from prior work is an experimental variable, not a fixed point: comparisons against it routinely measure the mismatch between the apparatus and the model rather than the method under test.
  - Four instances in four domains, each with the confound measured rather than argued. Few-shot chain-of-thought prompting with dataset examples — the standard baseline in official model releases — costs a reasoning-specialized model roughly twelve points on GSM8K against no prompting at all (74.2% against 86.1%), so any method benchmarked as an improvement over it collects that deficit as free gain; the same paper shows an apparent nine-point scaling curve in the number of demonstrations is a rule-based parser learning to find the answer, with the underlying solutions unchanged. The standard MQM translation-evaluation prompt is specialized to the model it was authored against, and every other model including that model's own successor performs significantly worse under it, while enforcing structured output degrades judgement quality across all models and minor changes to the parsing heuristic move the reported metric with model output held fixed. And a vision-language selection rule reporting a 31.8-point gain over majority voting loses all of it to a control that merely matches the decoding format, because the baseline aggregated long chains while the method aggregated short answers. A fourth instance, added 2026-08-20, extends the claim from baselines and formats to the measurement units themselves, and supplies the general form. Auditing concept steering across 17 models from five families and 0.6B to 72B, an apparent emergence of steerability with scale — a clean monotone curve that looks like a capability appearing — is produced entirely by three uncalibrated choices: raw activation units, the readout metric, and a fixed hand-picked operating point. A fixed raw coefficient is not a fixed intervention, because the fraction of the residual it displaces varies non-monotonically across models. Under a factorial decomposition holding metric and concept set constant, none of the four cells shows a significant scaling trend, so correcting any single one of the three removes it. That is the structural lesson the other three instances each illustrate once: when several parts of an apparatus are uncalibrated in the same direction, they compound into an effect with a plausible mechanism attached, and no single correction can be credited with having found the artifact — which is why an audit has to vary the choices independently rather than propose a better recipe. The same paper adds the case where the criterion determines its own finding: ranking neurons by linear correlation with number magnitude cannot rank an interior-peaked tuning curve highly, so a literature using that criterion could only ever report monotonic units, and a shape-agnostic criterion finds bell-shaped ones in every model tested. A fifth instance, added 2026-08-20, applies the same audit to the archive's most common instrument and adds a mechanism that makes the problem hard to see. Sweeping every extraction choice behind a published internal signal for code correctness -- construction method, read-out location, fitting and testing framing, fitting source, benchmark, model -- finds no configuration best across settings and, critically, that a pooled analysis of variance ranks the read-out location at under 1 percent of variance while any term involving the benchmark carries 47 percent. The location is not unimportant: averaging over the response beats the last token by more than twenty points on one benchmark and loses by ten to fifteen on the other, and the two opposite preferences cancel in the pool. So a factor can appear negligible in exactly the aggregate a paper would report, because its effects on two settings are equal and opposite. The same study finds the dominant factor family differs by benchmark -- fitting data at 75 percent on one against construction at 42 and model at 40 on the other -- that a mismatched fitting source drives the signal to 22 percent on a task whose chance floor is 25, and that tightening the contrastive pair to isolate the injected fault yields a direction scoring 83 percent on held-out pairs of its own construction and 20 to 30 percent on real programs. The general rule the five instances share is that the apparatus is never neutral, and that pooling over the settings it varies with can hide that rather than average it out.
