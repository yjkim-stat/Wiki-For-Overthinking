# pattern recognition versus reasoning

<!-- auto:begin -->

The question of whether benchmark success reflects reasoning or the retrieval of familiar patterns, posed by both sources as the thing their benchmark is designed to separate. One decomposes mathematical problem-solving into four cognitive dimensions and adds an All-Pass Score requiring success across them rather than on average, on the argument that a single aggregate lets strength in one dimension mask weakness in another. The other picks chess because it demands strategy, exact rule adherence and state tracking together, and measures against a fixed engine rather than against other models. Both treat the distinction as unanswerable from aggregate accuracy alone, which is why each builds a decomposition or an absolute reference.
