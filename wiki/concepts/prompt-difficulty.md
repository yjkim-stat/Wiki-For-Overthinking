# prompt difficulty

<!-- auto:begin -->

How hard a problem is for a given policy, and across 15 sources the variable that both training and inference allocation turn on. Its central property is that it is policy-relative and moving: a prompt is easy when every rollout succeeds and hard when none does, and both cases produce zero group-relative advantage, so the useful band is the middle -- one method shifts 82.7 percent of its gradient budget into the 0.2-0.9 pass-rate band against a baseline's 58.9. It also moves during training, which is why one source tracks per-task reward trajectories across 20 epochs and classifies tasks into six profiles including forgetting and stubbornly-unlearned rather than treating difficulty as a static label. At inference the same variable governs allocation, with several methods conditioning the thinking budget on it, and one finding the saving concentrated on the easy tail -- 53 to 81 percent token reduction for the top decile and percentile. Two cautions the archive attaches. Difficulty defined by a baseline's own pass rate selects for that baseline's failures, so part of any tiered improvement is regression toward the mean. And benchmark difficulty is only an approximation of what a particular policy can answer, which is why the better sources report tiers by measured pass rate rather than by benchmark label.

## What we have settled

- **Established** — A method's reported margin is largely a property of how much headroom its backbone had: the same intervention gains most on the weakest model and shrinks, vanishes or reverses on a stronger one or on a second family — so a single-backbone result establishes the direction of an effect and not its size.
  - Eight sources, in six subject areas, report the pattern and several state it about their own results. Where the same method is run across a size or capability ladder, the margin decreases with backbone strength: a structured spatial-reasoning method leads its category by 16.85 percent at 3B and 9.51 at 7B; a self-correction method's gains shrink on its three newest backbones, which its authors attribute to those models already self-correcting before training; and rationale supervision buys nothing in-domain for strong backbones while buying 20 to 28 points out of domain, with a language-specific model gaining least of all and the weakest general backbone gaining most. Where the ladder is a mismatch axis rather than a size axis the effect is starker: one distillation method's advantage is 1.0 point where the student is close to the teacher -- inside the spread of three other baselines -- and 13.4 where it is far below, and the strong-mismatch baseline is a model emitting 22K-token unterminated responses, so much of what is demonstrated there is repair of breakage. Crossing families rather than sizes can reverse the ordering outright: a preference method reaching 62.33 percent on a mathematics set with one 8B backbone loses to a baseline on another (23.01 against 23.51); three post-training algorithms improve one family's model on every subjective task while the other family's falls below its own reasoning baseline on three of four under all three; and a safety pipeline's utility cost lands entirely on one family, which its authors attribute to reasoning-style mismatch with the teacher rather than to the defence. There is also a floor, not only a ceiling: the same jury distillation that lifts a 12B student lowers a 4B one, a limit that source scopes around rather than states. Two consequences for reading this archive. A margin reported on one backbone should be read as evidence that an intervention does something rather than as an estimate of how much, and a ladder that is monotone in one family is not evidence of a scaling law -- one source reports exactly such a ladder (+2.46, +4.85, +12.01) and explicitly declines to call it one. And the standard control is cheap: run the second family, and report the backbone's own headroom alongside the margin.

## Appears in

- [Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally can be More Effective than Scaling Model Parameters](../../archive/papers/2024/arxiv-2408-03314/summary.md) — Studies how far a fixed model improves when given more inference compute, and shows that allocating that compute adaptively per prompt by difficulty beats a uniform best-of-N budget by more than 4x.
- [Beyond the Mean: Multi-Moment Policy Optimization for LLM Reasoning](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-02149/summary.md) — Treats a policy's per-problem failure probability as a random variable over the problem distribution and shows that REINFORCE, pass@K training and MaxRL each optimize a single moment of it, then proposes minimizing the first T moments jointly — which is exactly minimizing the expected truncated number of rollouts needed to reach a first success.
- [Interpretable Adaptive Sampling for LLM Test-Time Scaling](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-03961/summary.md) — Allocates test-time samples per prompt with a fuzzy controller over human-readable difficulty and confidence signals, and — under a selector-matched protocol that isolates the budget policy from the answer selector — reports the result honestly as an accuracy-compute tradeoff rather than an accuracy gain.
- [Beyond Solvability: Task Learnability as a Static Prior for LLM RL Post-Training](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-09217/summary.md) — Separates how well a policy currently does on a task from how positively that task responds to further training, shows the second is reproducible across independent runs and predicts downstream value at matched current pass rate, and estimates it from a short probe run before RL begins.
- [CAT: Confidence-Adaptive Thinking for Efficient Reasoning of Large Reasoning Models](../../archive/papers/2026/doi-10-18653-v1-2026-acl-industry-152/summary.md) — Feeds a reasoning model's own self-certainty into preference optimization so it compresses confident answers and keeps deliberating on uncertain ones.
- [Think How to Think: Mitigating Overthinking with Autonomous Difficulty Cognition in Large Reasoning Models](../../archive/papers/2026/doi-10-18653-v1-2026-acl-long-1766/summary.md) — Two-stage fine-tuning that first injects difficulty cues into output prefixes for prospective strategy selection, then injects redundancy cues mid-reasoning for retrospective correction.
- [Thinking-Based Non-Thinking: Solving the Reward Hacking Problem in Training Hybrid Reasoning Models via Reinforcement Learning](../../archive/papers/2026/doi-10-18653-v1-2026-acl-long-2122/summary.md) — Fixes reward hacking in hybrid thinking/non-thinking RL by setting per-query token limits for non-thinking responses derived from the solution part of that query's thinking responses.
- [Miner: Mining Intrinsic Mastery for Data-Efficient RL in Large Reasoning Models](../../archive/papers/2026/doi-10-18653-v1-2026-acl-long-237/summary.md) — Addresses wasted rollouts in critic-free RL on prompts where every sampled rollout is already correct and the advantage estimate is therefore zero.
- [Representation-Based Exploration for Language Models: From Test-Time to Post-Training](../../archive/papers/2026/local-1fadd9f07b138261/summary.md) — Uses elliptical bonuses over a language model's own hidden-state representations as a diversity signal, validates it in a clean inference-time selection setting, then transfers the same signal into RL post-training — where it eliminates the diversity collapse that degrades pass@k at large k.
- [Optimal Bayesian Stopping for Efficient Inference of Consistent LLM Answers](../../archive/papers/2026/local-5c4c22504406a6aa/summary.md) — Stops self-consistency sampling by Bayesian posterior over which answer is the mode, and proves that tracking only the top two answer counts plus an aggregate is enough for asymptotic optimality.
- [The Overthinker's DIET: Cutting Token Calories with DIfficulty-AwarE Training](../../archive/papers/2025/local-5feb5d3d92da16e0/summary.md) — Trains reasoning models to be concise in proportion to difficulty by modulating the token penalty and the target length per problem, and fixes a distortion that naive reward weighting introduces into group-normalized RL.
- [On Reasoning Strength Planning in Large Reasoning Models](../../archive/papers/2025/local-77b3413236375923/summary.md) — Shows that a reasoning model decides how long to think before emitting a single reasoning token — the eventual token count is linearly decodable from the question's activations at Spearman 0.84 — and that this plan is carried by one shared direction vector whose magnitude encodes strength and which acts by shifting the logits of the end-of-thinking token.
- [What If We Allocate Test-Time Compute Adaptively?](../../archive/papers/2026/local-80ef8b5ce7217f7c/summary.md) — Replaces uniform test-time compute allocation with a training-free agent that picks reasoning tools, a search strategy and an exploration parameter per problem, using a process reward model both to prune within a trajectory and to select across iterations.
- [RADAR: Reasoning-Ability and Difficulty-Aware Routing for Reasoning LLMs](../../archive/papers/2026/local-acb4e6ecc12e897d/summary.md) — Routes each query to a {model, reasoning budget} configuration by fitting an item response theory model over an evaluation matrix, yielding interpretable query difficulties and configuration abilities, and sending harder queries to higher-ability configurations.
- [OS-Pruner: Pruning Chains-of-Thought of Reasoning Models via Optimal Stopping](../../archive/papers/2026/local-dbfa51b5159a1a77/summary.md) — Recasts when-to-stop-reasoning as optimal stopping rather than classification, and proves that a fixed threshold on the probability of being correct can be arbitrarily far from optimal even when that probability is known exactly, because the decision needs the value of continuing and not the value of stopping.
