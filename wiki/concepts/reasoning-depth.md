# reasoning depth

<!-- auto:begin -->

How many dependent steps a problem requires, used across 3 sources as the controllable difficulty axis in benchmark and curriculum design. Its uses: grading a curriculum by nesting depth, which is what makes staged training possible and worth 8.78 points in one grounded-reasoning system; exposing it as one of four independently controllable axes in a benchmark generated from executable computation trees; and reporting accuracy by required step count, where one source finds refusal rates rising from 2.1 to 14.8 percent between two-step problems and those needing five or more. The archive's related caution is that depth in a benchmark's construction is not the same as difficulty for a given policy, which is why the better sources report measured pass rate alongside.

## Appears in

- [CURV: Enhancing Chart Understanding Through Curriculum Visual Grounded Reasoning](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-02833/summary.md) — Reformulates chart question answering as a chain in which every reasoning step carries a predicted image region, trains that pairing through a curriculum graded by nesting depth, and finds explicit intermediate grounding worth 8.78 points over letting the model attend implicitly.
- [MathShikkha: A Controlled Study of Answer-Only and Chain-of-Thought Supervision for Bangla Mathematical Reasoning in Small Language Models](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-08503/summary.md) — Compares chain-of-thought against answer-only supervision under a protocol where the two conditions differ in nothing but the training target, and finds the rationales buy nothing in-domain for strong backbones while buying 20 to 28 points out of domain -- with a human study attributing the measurable effect to language adherence and inspectability rather than to better reasoning.
- [V-FiLLM: Verified Financial LLM Reasoning Benchmark](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-11047/summary.md) — Generates financial reasoning benchmarks from executable computation trees over real tables so that answers are correct by construction with no model in the labelling loop, exposes four independently controllable difficulty axes, and finds that unit and scale perturbations collapse the strongest model from 98.4 percent to 3.0.
