# reward sparsity

<!-- auto:begin -->

A reward that is silent on almost everything the policy did, and across 4 sources the condition every credit-assignment method in this archive is built against. Its forms: one scalar for a trajectory of ten retrievals; a binary check that gives no gradient at all when every rollout in a group agrees; and a numeric tolerance set strictly enough that the policy receives almost no signal, stays tethered to its initialisation and stagnates -- visible as the lowest divergence trajectory in a sweep. The remedies divide into densifying the signal, which the archive's process-reward entry records as working only as a modifier on the terminal check, and reshaping it so what signal exists is expressible after group normalisation. One source addresses it from the critic side instead, on the argument that a value estimate is exactly the quantity a sparse terminal reward leaves undetermined at intermediate states.

## What we have settled

- **Established** — Under group-relative optimisation the shape of the reward is a more effective lever than the optimiser, because the normalisation cancels a linear rescaling and preserves a nonlinear one — so fixing a sparse or badly-scaled signal at the reward is not interchangeable with fixing it at the update rule, and the two must be compared before either is credited.
  - Group-relative methods standardise rewards within a rollout group, so what reaches the optimiser is the relative spacing of rewards and not their values. Two consequences follow and both are measured. A linear rescaling of the reward is cancelled by the normalisation and changes nothing, while a bounded nonlinear reshaping survives it -- which is why replacing an unbounded error gap with a reciprocal mapping matters: an unbounded reward lets one outlier rollout inflate the group standard deviation and compress the advantages of every near-optimal sibling toward each other, so the group stops distinguishing better from best exactly where the useful gradient is. The direct comparison is the sharpest evidence: standard group-relative training given that reciprocal mapping beats four published optimisation-level variants -- reward normalisation, sample filtering, sequence-level ratios, soft advantages -- each run with the naive unbounded reward. That comparison is deliberately asymmetric, as its authors state, so it establishes that the two kinds of fix are not interchangeable rather than that reward-level fixes dominate. Three further sources give the same asymmetry from other directions. A derivation of what the normalisation optimises shows the induced prompt weighting under binary rewards is one over the square root of p(1-p), diverging at both ends, with the realised waste measured at 36.4 percent of one benchmark's token-level gradient budget spent on prompts already above a 0.9 pass rate -- a property of the reward-to-advantage transform that no optimiser change addresses. A reward-granularity sweep under fixed optimisation settings is monotone from sparse to hierarchical to argument-factorised, while the same paper finds token-level weighting improving the group-relative method and severely destabilising a batch-normalised one, so a shaping choice cannot be evaluated apart from the optimiser it is fed to. And a rubric study finds a reward-shape change -- grading each criterion correct, missing or wrong rather than yes/no -- worth 2.25 points of medical macro-average while an optimiser-side intervention on the same system moves that average by 0.25. The counterweight, and the reason this is stated as an asymmetry rather than a ranking, is that a difficulty-dependent multiplier interacts badly with the same normalisation and weakens a penalty precisely where outcome variance is highest: reshaping the reward is powerful because the normalisation transmits it, which also means a poorly chosen shape is transmitted.

## Appears in

- [BiCAA: Bidirectional Credit Assignment for Search-Augmented Agent](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-01321/summary.md) — Gives each retrieval step of a search agent a dense reward built from two ground-truth-conditioned signals — how much the step raised the model's likelihood of the correct answer, and how necessary the step looks in hindsight — and fuses them asymmetrically so that a step which helps locally but is redundant globally is discounted.
- [Self-Improving Large Language Models via Progressive Experience Evolution](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-02139/summary.md) — Inserts a stage before RL in which the model extracts textual lessons from its own successful and failed rollouts, filters them by measured marginal utility on a held-out probe set, and distills the surviving pool into its own weights — so that GRPO starts from a policy that fails all-eight-samples less often.
- [Start Classifying: Categorical Critics for LLM Reinforcement Learning](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-02181/summary.md) — Replaces PPO's scalar mean-squared-error critic head with a categorical predictor over a discretized value support trained by cross-entropy against Gaussian-smoothed targets, decodes it back to a scalar for an unchanged GAE update, and shows the resulting critic is better calibrated and produces near-symmetric advantages where the MSE critic penalizes failures two-to-three times harder than it rewards successes.
- [MedCalc-R1: Knowledge-Guided Reward Framework for Medical Mathematical Reasoning](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-08623/summary.md) — Replaces the single tolerance threshold that RLVR uses to score floating-point answers with a hybrid reward pairing a hard clinical-safety constraint against a continuous precision-sensitive term, and adds a reward for stating the computational formula explicitly so an external verifier can check it.
