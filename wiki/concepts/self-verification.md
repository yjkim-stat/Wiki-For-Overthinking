# self-verification

<!-- auto:begin -->

A model checking its own output before committing, and across 4 sources a capability the archive separates from self-correction by what it produces -- a judgement rather than a revision. Its instances: a protocol that extracts a problem's answer-space constraints first and then checks intermediate and final results against them, which grounds the check in something outside the model's own confidence; a meta-abilities framework treating it as one of several alignable capacities; and a step-level decomposition that locates verification behaviour in a trace. The archive's related evidence bounds it sharply: the precision of emergent self-corrections is measured at 24.4 percent, agreement does not track correctness on hard problems, and a persistent-state study finds in-context self-reflection ranging from -14 to +45 percent across models with no reduction at all on one. The pattern is that verification against an external constraint works and verification against the model's own belief does not.
