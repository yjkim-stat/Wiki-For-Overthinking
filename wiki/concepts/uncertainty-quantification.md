# uncertainty quantification

<!-- auto:begin -->

Estimating how much a model's answer should be trusted, and across 6 sources a family whose members disagree about what to compute it from. The corpus's options are token-probability statistics, entropy over sampled answers, internal-state geometry, and explicit verbalised confidence -- and its findings are mostly about their failure to interchange. Statistics computed coordinate-wise from hidden states give calibration error of 0.1216 and 0.0858 in two prompt orderings against 0.2451 and 0.1764 for a trained last-token head, and both are competitive using only the first 12 of 28 layers. Verbalised confidence and answer frequency both fail in multimodal settings for the same reason -- neither tests whether an answer depends on the image, so a grounded answer and a confident language-prior guess look identical. And the archive's sharpest result bounds all of them on hard problems: in the highest-agreement bin the plurality is correct about half the time for one model and less often than its own lowest bin for another, so no verifier-free statistic derived from the model's own samples carries the information a gate needs. One source turns a latent estimate into an actionable critique by naming the offending parameter rather than emitting a scalar, which is the direction the corpus suggests when the number itself is untrustworthy.

## Appears in

- [Training-Free versus Training-Based Intent Classification in LLMs: Accuracy, Robustness, and Failure Modes](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-02415/summary.md) — Compares two training-free intent classifiers built from summary statistics of prefill-time activations against trained heads on the same features, and finds the trade-off is not accuracy but where each fails — trained heads win fine-grained distinctions, statistical ones give better uncertainty on mixed prompts and survive adversarial rephrasing that collapses the trained heads to zero.
- [Interpretable Adaptive Sampling for LLM Test-Time Scaling](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-03961/summary.md) — Allocates test-time samples per prompt with a fuzzy controller over human-readable difficulty and confidence signals, and — under a selector-matched protocol that isolates the budget policy from the answer selector — reports the result honestly as an accuracy-compute tradeoff rather than an accuracy gain.
- [Test-Time Scaling in Reasoning LLMs: Inference Regimes, Evaluation, and Reproducibility](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-04001/summary.md) — Formalizes test-time scaling as budgeted inference over a model's implicit prefix tree, separates three structurally different regimes that a single scalar budget conflates, specifies what a reproducible inference protocol must declare, and releases 1.9 million traces — with the empirical section showing a selection score that makes accuracy fall from 75.56% to 65.83% as the candidate bank grows.
- [Actionable Hallucination Detection: Translating Latent Uncertainty into Agentic Critique](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-10430/summary.md) — Detects the class of hallucination where a model confidently fabricates a parameter the user never gave, by running a LoRA adapter alongside the frozen model that restructures the residual stream and then names the offending parameter in words the agent can act on.
- [Quantifying and Understanding Uncertainty in Large Reasoning Models](../../archive/papers/2026/doi-10-18653-v1-2026-acl-long-1511/summary.md) — Applies conformal prediction to the joint reasoning-answer structure of reasoning models, then attributes coverage to specific training examples and reasoning steps with Shapley values.
- [The Confidence Paradox: Unveiling the Latent Discriminative Power of Diffusion Large Language Models in Mathematical Reasoning](../../archive/papers/2026/doi-10-18653-v1-2026-findings-acl-2142/summary.md) — Finds diffusion language models are badly miscalibrated on math reasoning yet rank correct from incorrect far better than autoregressive baselines, because their confidence tracks structural consistency rather than correctness.
