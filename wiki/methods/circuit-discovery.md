# circuit discovery

<!-- auto:begin -->

Finding the subgraph of components responsible for a behaviour, and across 3 sources a method whose results in this archive divide by whether a circuit is found at all. It is found for propositional logic: four families of attention heads executing rule locating, rule moving, fact processing and decision making as sequential steps, recovered by causal mediation across models from 7B to 27B. It is not found for arithmetic: the circuit reverse-engineered to individual neurons is neither a learned algorithm nor memorisation but an unordered collection of sparse heuristic neurons each firing on a numerical input pattern and voting for corresponding answers. And one source builds on discovered circuits rather than discovering them, using sparse-autoencoder circuits to align steering across layers geometrically. The archive's caution for all three is the identifiability one: where the units come from a learned dictionary rather than from the weights, the circuit is partly a property of the dictionary.
