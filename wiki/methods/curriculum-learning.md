# curriculum learning

<!-- auto:begin -->

Ordering training material from easy to hard, or staging what is supervised, and across 6 sources a component whose removal is often the largest ablation in the paper. The sharpest instance is latent reasoning: removing the curriculum collapses one method to 14.4 percent on a grade-school set, 52.4 on one logic set and 76.1 on another -- at or below the no-chain-of-thought baseline in two of three cases -- so the method is not merely helped by staging but fragile without it. A staged curriculum graded by nesting depth is worth 8.78 points in one grounded chart-reasoning system, and removing a staged curriculum costs a further 0.51 to 1.05 mean F1 on top of the latent mechanism itself in another. The archive's related evidence on staging is consistent: initialising a step-level objective before a self-correction objective beats joint training and beats plain reinforcement learning by 2 to 4 points, so the ordering rather than the objectives carries part of the effect. The caution is the usual one for multi-stage pipelines -- attribution is hard, and one source's stage-wise ablation shows most of its headline compression arriving with the final stage rather than with the criteria the paper is about.

## Appears in

- [Evading Chain-of-Thought Monitoring Through Model Poisoning](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-02820/summary.md) — Shows that supervised fine-tuning can install a triggered backdoor whose visible reasoning stays clean, correct and topically benign while the final answer is attacker-chosen — leaving CoT-only monitors at chance (AUC 0.44-0.55) and recovering detection only when the monitor is shown the answer alongside the trace (0.76-1.00).
- [CURV: Enhancing Chart Understanding Through Curriculum Visual Grounded Reasoning](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-02833/summary.md) — Reformulates chart question answering as a chain in which every reasoning step carries a predicted image region, trains that pairing through a curriculum graded by nesting depth, and finds explicit intermediate grounding worth 8.78 points over letting the model attend implicitly.
- [LatentGuard: Efficient and Inspectable Latent Reasoning for LLM Safeguards](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-03838/summary.md) — Compresses a safety guard's textual rationales into continuous latent states by a staged curriculum, cutting 268 reasoning tokens to 1.60 and latency 8.9-fold, and adds an on-demand decoder that reconstructs a human-readable audit artifact — whose own ablation shows the artifact is anchored far more by the source text than by the latent states it is supposed to inspect.
- [Beyond Solvability: Task Learnability as a Static Prior for LLM RL Post-Training](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-09217/summary.md) — Separates how well a policy currently does on a task from how positively that task responds to further training, shows the second is reproducible across independent runs and predicts downstream value at matched current pass rate, and estimates it from a short probe run before RL begins.
- [Capabilities and Fundamental Limits of Latent Chain-of-Thought](../../archive/papers/2026/local-6b66615b7bf3ef86/summary.md) — Explains why latent chain-of-thought excels at exploration but fails at computation by identifying decisional certainty as the governing variable, formalizing it as the Symbolic Index, and proving that curriculum learning is not merely helpful but necessary for training latent reasoners.
- [Transformers Provably Learn to Internalize Chain-of-Thought](../../archive/papers/2026/local-ee30f023d9f2d8fb/summary.md) — Proves that reasoning can be moved from emitted tokens into hidden states without losing sample efficiency, using a curriculum that deletes thinking tokens in geometric chunks and so needs only logarithmically many training stages.
