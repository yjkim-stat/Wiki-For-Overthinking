# GRPO

<!-- auto:begin -->

Critic-free policy optimization for language models: for each prompt the policy samples a group of completions, each completion's advantage is its reward normalised by the group's mean and standard deviation, and the update is a clipped surrogate usually with a KL penalty to a reference policy -- so the baseline comes from the group rather than from a learned value network, which is the property nearly every source cites as its reason for choosing it. Across these sources it functions less as a fixed algorithm than as a substrate everything else is bolted onto: dense per-step rewards replacing the sequence-level one, advantages shifted by a detached teacher-student log-probability gap, credit redistributed by how rare a solution form is, updates restricted to a token subset, and rewards supplied by rubrics regenerated from the model's own rollouts, by an LLM judge, by a verifier, or by SQL executed against a source table. The sources agree on its characteristic failure and disagree about the fix: policy entropy collapses as reward rises, along a curve one paper fits as R = -a*exp(H) + b, with the driver identified variously as the covariance between token probability and advantage, as an imbalance between tokens whose updates lower entropy and those that raise it, and as the behaviour of positive-advantage tokens specifically -- while a theoretical comparison argues an entropy bonus buys control only by permanently moving the stationary point, where covariance-targeted control reaches the unregularized optimum once annealed. Three structural criticisms of the group-relative estimator recur: it produces exactly zero advantage whenever every rollout in a group agrees, wasting the rollouts; its per-completion uniformity is frequency-skewed once credit is aggregated by solution structure, so a recurring correct form accumulates positive coefficient mass in proportion to how often it is sampled; and its standard-deviation and length normalisations introduce optimization biases that a named variant removes. Two further sources bound what it achieves rather than how it runs, and they disagree: measured by pass@k at large k, RLVR-trained models lose to their own base models, which reads as sharpening the sampling distribution rather than adding capability -- but scoring the chain as well as the final answer reverses that verdict at every k, on the grounds that base models win by producing wrong chains that land on right answers. One source adds a consequence outside the training loop entirely: a brief round of GRPO erases the signals benchmark-contamination detectors rely on, with PPO-style importance sampling and clipping identified as the cause.
