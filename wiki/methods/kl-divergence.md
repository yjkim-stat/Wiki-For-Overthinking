# KL divergence

<!-- auto:begin -->

The asymmetric divergence between two distributions, present across 4 sources as a training objective, a regulariser and a measurement. Its three roles carry different cautions. As an objective, its direction matters and the archive documents both -- mass-covering forward KL as the working default and mode-seeking reverse KL as the one that collapses by loss of termination under pseudo-labels. As a regulariser its trajectory is read diagnostically: rising steadily is offered as evidence against representational collapse, while the lowest trajectory in a sweep indicates a policy that never left its initialisation. As a measurement it appears in patching evaluations, in per-token comparisons between context views (p99 of 0.6 nats under one conditioning against 13.0 under another), and in decompositions separating divergence on answer tokens from divergence on reasoning tokens. One theoretical source supplies a counterexample worth recording: with standard one-dimensional finite elements on a four-point grid the divergence converges to log 3 as a parameter grows, so radial unboundedness fails and an argument relying on it does not apply -- the feature conditions in these results are real restrictions rather than formalities.

## Appears in

- [Don't Peek at the Answer: Outcome-Masked Group Relative Policy Optimization for Label-Free RLVR](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-03119/summary.md) — Diagnoses label-free RLVR's collapse as a shortcut in which the same answer-level consensus signal both estimates the reward and receives the gradient, and fixes it by masking the answer span from updates entirely — so a reward can only be raised by improving the reasoning that produces the answer.
- [Safety Cost of Steering Vectors Is Separable and Reducible](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-08383/summary.md) — Shows that the part of a steering vector which breaks a model's refusal behaviour is a separate direction from the part that produces the intended behavioural effect, and learns that direction by constrained optimization so it can be ablated without losing the steering.
- [Interpreting Language Model Hidden States at Scale](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-10260/summary.md) — Makes trained lenses cheap enough to attach densely across a whole model — every layer, and residual, attention and MLP alike — and then uses that coverage to show that where a behaviour is most visible is not where intervening on it works best.
- [Global linear convergence of entropy-regularized softmax policy gradient beyond tabular MDPs](../../archive/papers/2026/local-e7b4993440250612/summary.md) — Proves that entropy-regularized policy gradient converges to the regularized optimum at an exponential rate for log-linear softmax policies over continuous state and action spaces, by using the KL regularizer as a Lyapunov function to keep the Fisher information matrix from degenerating.
