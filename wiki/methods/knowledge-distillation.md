# knowledge distillation

<!-- auto:begin -->

Training a smaller student model on data generated by a larger teacher. In this archive it appears mostly as a comparison arm rather than as a subject: one source contrasts it with self-improvement and with weak-to-strong improvement, finding a weak-cheap teacher beats a strong-expensive one by about 5 points under distillation, and another reports that small verifiers remain unreliable on memorisation-dependent checks even after distillation from larger verifiers. Its other role here is as the origin of the models the archive studies -- the R1-Distill family -- where sources repeatedly separate distilled from RL-trained checkpoints because the two behave differently under the same intervention.

## Appears in

- [Reinforcement Learning Teachers of Test Time Scaling](../../archive/papers/2025/title-21d46f88974ff7dd/summary.md) — Trains a reinforcement-learned teacher model that is given the solution and rewarded for producing explanations that help a student model understand it, outperforming distillation pipelines built from much larger models.
- [Smaller, Weaker, Yet Better: Training LLM Reasoners via Compute-Optimal Sampling](../../archive/papers/2025/title-2e7ac25d673bc96f/summary.md) — Under a fixed inference budget, training data generated by a weaker-but-cheaper (WC) language model consistently produces better-performing reasoner students than data from a stronger-but-more-expensive (SE) model, across knowledge distillation, self-improvement, and a novel weak-to-strong learning paradigm -- challenging the default practice of sourcing synthetic training data from the most powerful available model.
- [When Reasoning Meets Compression: Understanding the Effects of LLMs Compression on Large Reasoning Models](../../archive/papers/2026/title-c593d75efe2e5d8c/summary.md) — Studies how quantization, distillation and pruning affect DeepSeek-R1's reasoning ability using mechanistic interpretation, finding weight count matters more for knowledge memorization than reasoning, and that protecting just 2% of over-compressed weights recovers 6.57 accuracy points.
