# pre-registration

<!-- auto:begin -->

Fixing the criterion a result must meet before the result is measured, so that a threshold cannot be chosen after seeing the data. Both sources are from the same research programme and both use it in the way that costs them something. The identifiability work reports two pre-registered criteria that fail their controls: an invariance-gap criterion met in 49 percent of 75 cells against a registered 80 percent, because the sign of the effect tracks the feature-selection rule, and a universality criterion that separates two seed replicas of one architecture and therefore, in its stated form, is not a universality criterion. Both are reported because both bound what may be claimed. The cross-layer ablation work applies it to mechanism selection, reporting three rejected candidate mechanisms because they had been pre-registered -- which the archive's reading notes cuts both ways, since it also means the retained mechanism was selected for cleanliness. Neither source treats pre-registration as a subject; between them they demonstrate its only real function, which is to make a negative result reportable rather than discardable.

- [PAIR: Pairwise-Aware Inclusion Reweighting for Adaptive Rollout Allocation in RLVR](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-11368/summary.md) — Points out that the group-relative policy gradient is a second-order U-statistic over pairs of rollouts rather than a sum of independent per-rollout contributions, and reallocates rollout compute accordingly -- treating prefixes as vertices and pair-gradient terms as edges, then correcting the resulting selection bias by inverse joint-inclusion weighting.
