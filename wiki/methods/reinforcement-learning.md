# reinforcement learning

<!-- auto:begin -->

Optimizing a policy against a reward rather than against reference outputs, and in both sources the more expensive option that is compared against a cheaper one rather than assumed. One applies its framework under both supervised finetuning and reinforcement learning, finds RL yields up to 12.58 points and the combination 17.04, and then declines it on cost grounds — naming substantially higher computational overhead against the more favourable trade of the supervised route. The other uses it to learn an optimal-stopping policy for pruning reasoning traces. The pairing is a useful corrective to how this archive usually reads RL results: the question is not only whether it works but whether the gain survives being priced.
