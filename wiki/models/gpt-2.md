# GPT-2

<!-- auto:begin -->

The small early transformer, used across 5 sources as the minimal subject where a mechanism can be studied end to end or where a claim about scale needs a floor. Its appearances: as the smallest backbone in a time-series refinement sweep, where it sits within 0.003 MSE of a 7B model and thereby shows the large backbone was close to inert for that task; as a subject in latent chain-of-thought analyses, including one finding that deleting every latent token changes accuracy by at most 1.0 point in the fine-tuned regime; in hidden-state interpretation at scale; and in per-instance multi-layer steering. Its value in the archive is as the model where a null result is most likely to be about the method rather than about capability.

## Appears in

- [Efficient Test-Time Scaling for LLM-based Time Series Forecasting](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-08675/summary.md) — Replaces open-ended iterative refinement in LLM time-series forecasting with a fixed-step coarse-to-fine loop anchored to an explicitly predicted downscaled future shape, so test-time compute is spent enriching detail rather than re-deciding the global trajectory.
- [Deployable Per-Instance Multi-Layer Activation Steering for Large Language Models](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-08829/summary.md) — Shows that which layers a steering vector should be injected at is a property of the individual input rather than of the task, that a greedy per-input rule reaches the exhaustive optimum for structural reasons, and that a label-free predictor trained to imitate that rule recovers most of the oracle at deployment.
- [Interpreting Language Model Hidden States at Scale](../../archive/papers/2026/arxiv-2608-10260/summary.md) — Makes trained lenses cheap enough to attach densely across a whole model — every layer, and residual, attention and MLP alike — and then uses that coverage to show that where a behaviour is most visible is not where intervening on it works best.
- [The Illusion of Superposition? A Principled Analysis of Latent Thinking in Language Models](../../archive/papers/2026/local-043e84b0b0ae0a39/summary.md) — Tests the claim that continuous chain-of-thought lets a model hold several candidate solutions at once, and finds it holds only for models trained from scratch: off-the-shelf models collapse a superposed input to a single token within a few layers, and fine-tuned latent reasoners solve the task in one forward pass and copy the answer through the latent slots.
- [Capabilities and Fundamental Limits of Latent Chain-of-Thought](../../archive/papers/2026/local-6b66615b7bf3ef86/summary.md) — Explains why latent chain-of-thought excels at exploration but fails at computation by identifying decisional certainty as the governing variable, formalizing it as the Symbolic Index, and proving that curriculum learning is not merely helpful but necessary for training latent reasoners.
